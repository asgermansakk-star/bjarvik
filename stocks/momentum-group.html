    In plain words

    Bjarvik gives Momentum Group 81 out of 100. All growth is bought through acquisitions, while existing units shrink slightly. The return on invested money has fallen every year since 2021, and earnings grow slower than the target. The share is down 18% in a year but is not cheap. The CEO bought shares worth about SEK 12m. Watch the debt.

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −6 of −35
    • Execution against own targets
      The target is EBITA growth above 15% a year, but EBITA rose only 5% in 2025 (SEK 337m vs 322) and 10% LTM to September 2026 (366 vs 333). The target of doubling EBITA from 340 to 680m by the end of 2030 requires about 15% a year.

      −2
    • Macro and customer behaviour
      Management calls the market still somewhat challenging: paper and pulp postpone service and purchases, the Danish market is weak after fewer large projects, and Norway was weaker in Q3. Purchase prices are rising, partly because of the situation in the Middle East.
      momentum.group
      −2
    • Debt and minority options
      Financial net debt rose to SEK 678m from 571m at the turn of the year (net debt/EBITDA 1.73), and acquisitions are typically made as 70–80% ownership, where the sellers have put options on the rest at a price that depends on earnings. This gives ongoing cash obligations (11m in 2026, 40m in 2025).

      Two share classes: A shares with ten votes give Nordstjernan approx. 29% of the capital and 36% of the votes, and the Hedelius family has approx. 9%. It is a stable ownership group, but minority shareholders have limited influence.

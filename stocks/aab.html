    • Margin

The company has had significant losses and negative operating cash flow in four of the last five years.

    • Quality of growth
      Relegation is feeding through: revenue in H1 2026 fell to kr 25.8m from kr 47.2m (−45%) on lower TV, sponsor and match-day income. 2025 income was also flattered by a one-off 'parachute payment' of kr 9.0m.
      inderes.dk
      −6
    • Dependence on transfers
      The profit of kr 6.0m in 2025 came solely from a transfer result of kr 59.1m (−4.8m in 2024). In H1 2026 the transfer result fell to kr 11.8m from 48.7m, and the result before tax was −kr 14.5m. Earnings depend on single sales, not on a stronger business.
      inderes.dk
      −5

      AaB was relegated from the Superliga after 2024/25, missed the promotion playoffs in 2025/26 and can earliest be promoted after 2026/27. The company itself writes that several seasons outside the Superliga weaken TV income, sponsors, attendance and players' resale value.

      The share count rose 23.7% in a year (from 2.70 to 3.24m shares through warrants and conversion of subordinated loans in April 2026) after +52% in 2024 and +49% in 2023. The issue shows in the cash flow statement (DKK 14.1m in cash subscription) and in the conversion of DKK 9.8m in loans.

      The 2026 outlook is built on playing at the top of the Betinia Ligaen, but AaB is 8th after 13 matches with 15 points (3 wins, 6 draws, 4 defeats) and without a win in the last five matches, incl. 0–1 vs Vejle and 1–2 vs Hillerød in October.

      Nordic Football Group has over 50% of the votes and controls the company; minority shareholders have effectively no influence. The owner group's Thomas Hitzlsperger left the board in September 2026, the consulting agreement with BPTC Sports had to be terminated as a condition of the loan conversion, and AaB in some cases does not comply with the FIFA/DBU rule of agent fees of at most 3%.

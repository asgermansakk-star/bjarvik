    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −14 of −30
    • Margin and returns
      The running return is low: profit before value adjustments minus financial items was kr 18.3m in 2025 and kr 9.0m in H1 2026, equal to a running after-tax ROE of approx. 4.3–4.8%. ROIC is 2.7% (2.1–3.0% in 2023–2025). The reported ROE of 7.6–8% is lifted by value adjustments, and the return rests on a Swiss loan rate of only approx. 1.5%.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Rental income rose 7.5% in 2025 and 7.2% in H1 2026, but 2025 growth came especially from the full-year effect of the Koblenz purchase (June 2024, 10.7% of revenue) and a stronger CHF. Net asset value per share has risen from kr 110.46 (2023) to kr 148.40, but mainly via value adjustments (kr 60.5m in 2024 alone due to falling Swiss interest rates); no new properties have been bought since June 2024.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −2
    • Capital allocation
      In March–April 2026, 172,800 new shares (+7.6%) were issued at 100, about 0.68 × net asset value of kr 147.52. That dilutes net asset value by about kr 3.3 per existing share, and net asset value per share only rose from 147.52 to kr 148.40 in H1 despite a profit of kr 8.3m. Management plans ongoing directed issues to buy 6–8 properties for kr 500–600m, even though the stock trades far below net asset value.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −4
    • Moat and scale
      A small portfolio of nine commercial properties (DKK 782m) with mixed office, retail, production and warehouse, primarily in smaller towns (Wängi, Weinfelden, Sempach, Koblenz, Liestal) and built 1956–1991 except Wängi (2018). With nine employees and a market cap of approx. DKK 250m there are no scale advantages against the large Swiss property companies.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 6 findings    −17 of −35
    • Valuation of properties
      No external valuer has been used; the properties are valued internally in a DCF model with a weighted required return of 4.35% (3.79–5.21%) and 0% inflation. A required return 1 percentage point higher would reduce the value by kr 147.1m, about 44% of equity at the end of 2025 (before tax), so the intrinsic value that the whole buy case rests on is very sensitive and not independently verified.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −4
    • Debt and interest rate sensitivity
      Mortgage debt is kr 431.5m against properties of kr 782.1m (LTV about 55%), and net debt/equity is 1.09. Interest cover is 3.3 × in H1 2026, but only because the interest rate is about 1.5%; a normalisation of the Swiss interest rate will squeeze both earnings and values. On the positive side, kr 391m of the debt does not fall due until after 5 years.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −4
    • Cash flow
      Operating cash flow was only DKK 10.0m in 2025 and 3.9m in H1 2026 vs net profit of 25.4 and 8.3m, and scheduled repayments are DKK 8.2m a year. Cash flow can support neither dividend nor new acquisitions, so growth depends on issues and new debt.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −2
    • Tenants and concentration
      Only nine properties and relatively few tenants: Wängi is a sale-and-leaseback to one tenant and accounts for 17.4% of revenue, Schwerzenbach for 16.3%. Two properties have 10% vacancy and a third 16%, and Aktieinfo itself mentions dependence on few tenants as a weakness.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −2
    • Governance and related parties
      Operations are handled by management companies in the founders' circle (Swiss Property Management AG, Swiss Property Ventures AG and others), but the annual report has no note on related-party transactions, so the fees cannot be verified. The only analyst coverage is paid for by the company. The CEO changed (1/1-2026) as did a board member within a year, and the half-year report wrongly states the financial year as 01.07–30.06.
      swisspropertiesinvest.dk
      −3
    • Liquidity in the stock
      Average turnover is about 2,660 shares a day (about kr 0.27m) on First North, and on many days fewer than 200 shares or none at all are traded.

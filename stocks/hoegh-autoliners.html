    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a car-carrier shipping company that has earned well, but whose share has become expensive: it is up 96% in a year. Margins and earnings have fallen, and many new ships could press freight rates. The threat of US port fees is postponed, not removed. The balance sheet is strong and contracts have been extended. Analysts see about 21% upside. Watch the 19 November report.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −11 of −35
    • Overcapacity
      The global order book is 147 car carriers with delivery up to 2030, equal to about 21% of the fleet, and 21 ships are delivered in the rest of 2026. The charter index has risen 60% since Q1, so rates are close to the peak when the capacity arrives.
      api3.oslo.oslobors.no
      −4
    • USTR port fees
      The suspension of the US port fees on foreign-built car carriers expires on 9 November 2026; an extension to 9 January 2027 has only been proposed. The fees are postponed, not removed, and hit Höegh's entire foreign-built fleet on calls in the US.
      wfw.com
      −4
    • Cash flow, bunker and the Middle East
      Operating cash flow fell to USD 67m in Q2 from USD 153m a year earlier due to higher bunker inventory, rerouting of cargo and the conflict in the Middle East. Net debt rose from USD 630m to 757m in half a year, and the Q2 dividend was cut to USD 16m.

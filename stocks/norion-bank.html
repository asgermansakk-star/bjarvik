    In plain words

    Bjarvik gives Norion Bank 82 out of 100. 17% of lending is in loans where customers struggle to pay, and much is lent to property companies. The capital cushion has shrunk, and the bank was fined for weak anti-money-laundering checks. On the plus side, the share is cheap compared with rivals, and the chairman bought shares worth about SEK 46m. Watch the sale of bad consumer loans.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 2 findings    −7 of −30
    • Profitability and quality of growth
      Adjusted ROE of 13.7% (LTM) and 14.1% in the first nine months of the year is below the target of over 15% and below 15–18% in 2022–2025. Net interest income fell 1% in January–September despite 16% lending growth, because the interest margin fell from 7.1% to 6.5%; adjusted net income fell 7%, and adjusted EPS (5.48 vs 5.52) is only held up by buybacks.

    • Business model and moat
      Niche bank without a broad deposit franchise: 41% of lending is property loans to mid-sized companies (31% junior loans with an average loan-to-value of 78%, 32% offices, 20% Germany), the rest corporate loans, consumer loans and Walley. The advantage is quick decisions and a higher risk appetite than the big banks, not pricing power; management itself accepts a lower margin to reach wider into the property market.

    • Credit quality (stage 3)
      Stage 3 loans make up kr 10.6bn or 17.1% of gross lending. Corporate customers' stage 3 loans of kr 4.1bn (mainly real estate) are provisioned at only 26%, so the loss risk rests on the property values behind junior loans and office properties; stage 3 volume continues to weigh on the interest margin in the property segment, and management itself says the development can fluctuate between quarters.

      Chairman Erik Selin owns 37.9% and is also one of Sweden's largest property investors, in a bank where 41% of lending goes to real estate companies. The bank merely states that transactions with related parties have been made on normal terms; free float is only approx. 84m of 173m shares.

      In May 2026 Finansinspektionen gave the bank a remark and an administrative fine of SEK 90m because customer due diligence on corporate customers with a high money-laundering risk was inadequate. In addition, the EU Consumer Credit Directive (CCD2) tightens the requirements on Walley and consumer loans from the fourth quarter; management will only assess the effect in the coming quarters.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful (negative operating cash flow is due to loan growth); valuation on P/TBV, P/B and P/E, and buybacks are measured against net profit. Only 2 analysts cover the stock (price targets SEK 71–74). stockanalysis shows only 4.21% insider ownership, but chairman Erik Selin owns 37.9% according to the Q3 report. One-offs of SEK 115m (FI fine of SEK 90m and acquisition costs) have been stripped out of multiples and profitability. The credit rating has not been verified, and the NPL sale to Brocc had not been completed at Q3. The largest single exposure in the property segment, the full extent of related-party transactions (note 38 in the annual report) and competition in Walley have not been examined further (0 points).

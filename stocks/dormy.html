    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −9 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Growth slowed sharply right after the IPO: organic +17.5% in 2025, +6.2% in H1 2026 and only +2.9% in January–October. Store sales are −1.7% and Sweden (82% of sales) −0.3% year to date, and October fell 10.3% (Sweden −13.6%). Only e-commerce and the other Nordic countries are growing.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Margin
      Adjusted EBIT margin of 12.8% (R12) is good for retail and above the target of 10%, but EBIT fell to kr 67.4m (70.3) in H1 2026 despite 6% more sales: external costs rose 20% and personnel costs 12%. The margin is on its way down from the peak of 13.2% in H1 2025.
      assets.ctfassets.net
      −2
    • Future segment and moat
      With about 49% of the Swedish golf market (about 75% off the courses), there is little room for growth at home. Abroad accounts for only about 18% of sales, and the company itself describes its position and brand awareness as substantially weaker there, where competition is tougher. The moat is service and scale in Sweden, but the goods are mainly external brands, which customers can also buy elsewhere.
      assets.ctfassets.net
      −3

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −11 of −35
    • Supplier concentration
      The ten largest suppliers (e.g. Titleist, Callaway, TaylorMade, Ping) account for approx. 72% of sales. The brands decide prices and allocation and also sell online themselves. Several suppliers have signalled price rises due to freight and geopolitics.
      assets.ctfassets.net
      −3
    • Execution and logistics
      All inventory and logistics are in one central warehouse in Örebro, and in October 2026 a new warehouse system was introduced, which according to the CEO took a lot of effort in the same month that sales fell 10%. A disruption in peak season would hit both stores and e-commerce.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Governance and equity market
      The founders' Stymie Invest sold about 42% of the shares right after the record year, while sales in July–August had already fallen about 2%; the company received no new money. The stabilisation manager ABG bought about 875,000 shares (5.8% of the company) at about kr 70 to hold the price, and after the stabilisation the price is below the offer price (kr 66.5). Stymie still has about 51% and control, and turnover is only about 18,000 shares a day.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Macro and season
      Golf equipment is discretionary spending, sales are concentrated in spring and summer, and a hot summer or a cold spring hits directly (cf. July–August). 82% of sales are in Sweden. More expensive container freight due to the Middle East puts pressure on purchasing.
      inderes.se
      −3
    • Debt and leasing
      Net debt incl. leasing is kr 310m (approx. 1.6x EBITDA), and equity is only kr 75m after many years of dividends (kr 40.8m in H1 2026, partly financed with new bank loans of kr 148.5m). Leverage is not dangerous, but the buffer is thin.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Dormy was listed on 24 September 2026, so there is no multiple history, no 52-week data and no forecast page on stockanalysis (404); forward P/E and analyst coverage have not been found (ABG Sundal Collier was global coordinator, so any coverage may be sponsored). The figures are from the prospectus (2023 to H1 2026) and the monthly sales figures for September and October 2026. The Q3 report (Jan.–Sep.) comes 13 November 2026 and is not included; the margin development in Q3 is therefore unexamined. stockanalysis' debt (kr 460m) and D/E 6.8 include leasing of kr 282m and old intra-group loans; net debt excluding IFRS 16 was only kr 28.5m at 30/6. The low equity (kr 75m) is due to many years of dividends to the owners and not losses. stockanalysis' insider ownership (4%) is only the CEO's; the founders own approx. 51% through Stymie Invest. Demand for the golf package is discretionary and weather-dependent, but not a commodity or inventory cycle in the model's sense, so cyclical = false. Golf retail does not fit the industry list; 'Consumer: food, drink & restaurants' was chosen as for other retailers (Rusta, Clas Ohlson, Bokusgruppen). Competition from international e-tailers and the brands' own web shops has not been examined in depth. Analytical framework, not investment advice.

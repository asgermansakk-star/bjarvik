    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −15 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      The record quarter in Q2 2026 (net profit USD 92.9m, EPS USD 0.70) is due to rates: the fleet earned USD 116,260/day and the spot fleet USD 126,022/day (Suezmax 166,754), because the Strait of Hormuz is effectively closed after the conflict between the US and Iran. The growth also comes from the fleet having been built up from zero in 2026, not from organic improvement.

      −8
    • ROIC through the cycle
      Annualised return on equity of about 20% in Q2 on equity of USD 2,061m, but that is measured at record rates; the company was founded in January 2026 and has no return history through a normal freight cycle.
      storage.mfn.se
      −2
    • Moat
      Crude oil transport without pricing power: 83% of days in Q2 were spot, and rates are set by the market. The advantage is a very young fleet (average age 2.2 years), where 23 of 33 vessels are LNG dual fuel and nine of those in operation have scrubbers, but this only gives lower fuel and regulatory costs.
      storage.mfn.se
      −3

      The company states that the order book for crude oil tankers has risen to a record 27.6% of the fleet with deliveries up to 2030, and that the IEA expects oil supply to grow faster than demand in 2027 if the conflict is resolved.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −21 of −35
    • Macro and geopolitics
      Rates depend on the conflict with Iran and the closure of Hormuz; the MoU between the US and Iran on 17/6 only lasted until new attacks in July, and rates already fell late in Q2, especially for Aframax. A lasting reopening could normalise rates quickly, and 83% spot exposure feeds straight through.
      storage.mfn.se
      −8
    • Governance and related parties
      Capital Maritime (Evangelos Marinakis) owned 76.3% at listing. The commercial manager Heidmar, which receives USD 300/day per vessel plus 1.25% commission on freight revenue, is partly owned by board member Miltiadis Marinakis (son of the main shareholder), and the technical manager CSM (USD 550/day per vessel) belongs to the same group. The fleet, three VLCC contracts (June) and 13 options are bought from the main shareholder, and the prospectus itself cites media and legal scrutiny of Marinakis as a risk.
      capitaltankers.com
      −5
    • Newbuild programme, FCF and debt
      Remaining newbuilding payments of USD 1,806.6m at 30/6 (of which 421.5m in Q4 2027 alone) vs cash of 356.7m. Operating cash flow in H1 was 82.8m vs investments of 797.8m, so FCF is strongly negative. Interest-bearing debt of USD 523.9m is growing with new loans of approx. USD 1bn for 11 VLCCs (agreed in September–October) and sale-leaseback agreements; all debt has a variable interest rate and large balloon payments in 2031–36.
      capitaltankers.com
      −5
    • Dilution risk
      13 options on newbuildings from Capital Maritime (11 VLCCs at about USD 120m and two Suezmaxes at 89.5m, about USD 1.5bn in total) can be exercised until 31/12-2026, and the company's lock-up expressly exempts new shares to finance the options.
      capitaltankers.com
      −2
    • Share overhang
      The board's lock-up expired in September, and Capital Maritime's lock-up on about 76% of the shares expires in March 2027; free float is only about a quarter, so a sale by the main shareholder could pressure the price. The price has fallen from NOK 206.85 to 178 since 30/10.

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The company was founded 9/1-2026 and listed on Euronext Growth Oslo (not Oslo Børs' main list) 17/3-2026 at NOK 134. There is therefore no multiple or ROIC history of its own, no TTM figures and no 52-week change or 200-day average on stockanalysis. Accounts in USD, price in NOK; P/B (approx. 1.15) is roughly calculated on a market cap of NOK 23.8bn (approx. USD 2.36bn at approx. 10.1 NOK/USD, derived from stockanalysis' EV) vs equity of USD 2,061m at 30/6. The Q3 accounts come 30/11 and are not included. No NAV statement was found; book value is based on ships transferred from Capital Maritime in 2026 and is therefore close to today's high ship prices. stockanalysis shows only 1.08% insider ownership and a float of 132m shares, but Capital Maritime (the Marinakis family) owned 76.3% at listing. Only 3 analysts, and stockanalysis' revenue estimate for 2027 (NOK 3.67bn) looks low against the EPS estimate and has not been used. Customer concentration among charterers and insider trades after listing have not been examined (0 points). The dividend is not given as a plus, because FCF is strongly negative during the newbuilding programme.

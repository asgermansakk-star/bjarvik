    • Margin
      EBIT margin 4.5% on a rolling 12 months and 2.1% in 2025 (adjusted 3.8%) vs its own target of over 10%, which has not been reached in any of the last five years (best 7.3% in 2023). The net margin is about 2.5%.

      −6
    • ROIC
      ROIC 3.9% and ROE 3.3% vs 10.6–11.8% and 11.9–13.5% in 2023–2024; the return is below the cost of capital after the machine fleet was expanded.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue has been largely flat since 2021 (kr 374m → 408m TTM, peak 473m in 2023). Iberia, just under half of revenue, has posted losses year after year (EBIT −9.9m in 2025 and −6.0m in the first nine months of the year), and the improvement stalled in Q3.
      mb.cision.com
      −5
    • Moat
      Drilling contracts are won in tenders with a price per metre drilled; Drillcon is a small player, and earnings depend on the customers' underground conditions (standstill, staffing), which the company does not control itself.
      drillcongroup.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      Investments of kr 73m in 2025, more than double depreciation, financed with loans, while ROIC fell to under 1% the same year. The dividend of kr 11m in 2024 was paid even though FCF was negative.

      −3

    • Debt and refinancing
      Net debt incl. leasing approx. kr 107m (2.1x EBITDA) vs 41m at the end of 2024. Short-term interest-bearing loans have risen to 87m (49m a year earlier), and current ratio is 1.2; Altman Z-score 1.77.

    • Customer concentration
      Nordic relies on a few mines, especially Boliden Garpenberg and Zinkgruvan (alone 11 rigs and approx. 50 employees), and Iberia's result is dragged down by one Portuguese customer whose conditions do not give the productivity the contract is built on.

      −4
    • Execution
      Production stop in Q1 2026 and volumes below forecast at Garpenberg and Zinkgruvan in Q3 due to high absence and downtime in the mine; earnings swing sharply from quarter to quarter.

      −2
    • Governance
      Traction owns approx. 54% and controls the company; other insiders own under 1%. In 2024 the company took legal action against the former managing director of Drillcon Iberia, which points to weak control in the subsidiary.

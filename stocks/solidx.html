    A score of 51 out of 100 is mid-range: Bjarvik sees a small, shrinking consulting business with almost no profit. Revenue fell 10% to 133.9m SEK and earnings are close to zero. On the plus side, costs have been cut and there is slightly more cash than debt. The founder owns 72% and only about 8,000 shares trade per day.

      Gross margin 10%, EBITDA margin 6.2% and EBIT margin 2.1% (TTM); in Q1 EBIT was −SEK 0.1m (about 0.8 before goodwill amortisation). The margin has improved from −2.7% in 2024/25, but is far below 5.7–10% in 2022–2023 and below Nordic IT consulting houses.

    • ROIC
      ROIC 3.6% and ROE 1.9% (TTM) vs WACC of approx. 7%; before the acquisition in 2023, ROIC was 64–75%. The capital base is now dominated by goodwill (SEK 26.7m of SEK 31.4m in equity).

    • Capital allocation
      The acquisition of Utvecklarbolaget in December 2023 cost SEK 10.4m in cash and approx. 16% dilution (31.0 → 35.95m shares) and created approx. SEK 35m of goodwill; revenue has fallen since. The group also owns a non-core rental company (Sekel AB, 12 apartments in Umeå), and the merger with Time People Group was called off in 2024.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Microcap (market cap approx. SEK 67m) with no analyst coverage, so there are no forward estimates, price target or PEG. The stock is very illiquid (approx. 8,000 shares a day), and stockanalysis shows only a closing price from 30 September 2026, so the price date may be outdated. FCF is adjusted for capitalised development (SolidMatch). Customer concentration is only assessed from the number of customers (40) and the names of the largest, because the company does not disclose shares. There are no insider trades since November 2023 (CEO purchase), so the criterion scores 0. Macro and competition from AI matching platforms are not scored separately.

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 5 findings    −18 of −30
    • Margin
      Operating margin is 3.5% TTM (3.9% in H1 2026) vs the company's own target of over 6%, and net margin is only 1.6%. 2023–2025 gave operating margin of −4.1% to 1.7% and a loss on the bottom line every year.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • ROIC
      ROIC is 5.3% TTM, ROE 5.8% and ROCE 7.0%, all below the cost of capital. In 2021–2025 ROIC was between −10.7% and 2.4%, so the company has not earned its cost of capital for five years.
      stockanalysis.com
      −6
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue rose from SEK 2.9bn (2023) to 5.0bn TTM, primarily through the purchase of Rudd in the US; in 2025 revenue fell 6%. Q2 2026 grew 43%, but in Germany part of the growth came from deliveries postponed from Q1 and low-margin fleet agreements. The target of doubling 2024 revenue organically by 2029 requires approx. 15% a year.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Moat
      Ferronordic is a dealer with territories assigned by Volvo CE and Volvo/Renault Trucks, not the owner of the products. In the US the company describes intense price competition, because manufacturers and dealers seek to offset tariffs and a weak dollar, and the aftermarket is held back by a shortage of mechanics.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      After the forced exit from Russia in 2022, the company bought Rudd for about SEK 1.1bn in 2023, debt-financed; net cash of 957m became net debt of 1.35–1.98bn, and ROIC has since been below 6%. Housby in Iowa (about USD 17m, January 2026) is also primarily debt-financed.
      mfn.se
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The figures are based on TTM to and including Q2 2026; the Q3 report comes on 11 November 2026 and is not included. Only one analyst (ABG) covers the stock, so forward P/E of 8.9 rests on one estimate, and stockanalysis has no forecast page. EBITDA is lifted by depreciation on the rental fleet, so EV/EBITDA is not comparable with other industrial companies. The peer comparison is thin: there is no Nordic-listed machinery dealer, and Titan Machinery is loss-making. Customer concentration at end-customer level and the terms of the agreement with Volvo have not been examined (0 points beyond the supplier dependence). Marked cyclical because earnings follow the machinery and truck cycle and dealer inventories, in line with Volvo and Daimler Truck.

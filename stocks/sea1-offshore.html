    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a Norwegian shipping company serving oil and gas, priced low against its assets but with weakening prospects. The order book has fallen to $573m, part of the fleet has no work in 2027-2028, and four new ships with no agreed customers must be financed with loans. In its favour, an order book worth about two years of revenue and a solid balance sheet.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    • Margin and cycle
      The EBITDA margin is 51% in Q3 and the first nine months vs 54% and 56% a year earlier; the lift from about 41-42% gross margin in 2021-2022 follows a tight offshore market with high day rates, not a structural improvement.

    • Quality of growth
      Revenue rose 14% in the first nine months of the year, but EBITDA only 4.6% (USD 119.4m vs 114.1m), because part of the growth is bareboat income from Ben Viking and Odin Viking with corresponding costs. Analysts expect EPS −26% in 2026 and −24% in 2027, and ROIC has fallen to 8.2% from 14.5% in 2025.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Capital allocation and track record
      The company (formerly Siem Offshore) has debt restructurings behind it with a 1:100 reverse split in 2021 and heavy dilution in 2021–2022; equity still rests on earlier write-downs and reversals (accumulated impairment USD 173m).
      stockanalysis.com
      −3

      The entire fleet works in oil and gas (WIV, OSCV, PSV and oil spill vessels for Brazil, AHTS in the North Sea, APAC and Canada); the new 'energy support' vessels are methanol-ready tonnage for the same market.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −18 of −35
    • Newbuilds and financing needs
      Four OESV newbuilds from COSCO for delivery in 2027-2028 with no published charter contracts; USD 127.6m has been capitalised, and the rest is financed with a USD 315m facility from an alternative source of capital. The company itself writes that more newbuilds from 2027 will pressure rates, and that charterers are in 'wait and see'. NIBD has risen to USD 265.6m from 196.6m a year ago, 74% of the debt has a floating interest rate, and FCF after capex and leasing was approx. USD 6m in the first nine months of the year.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −5
    • Dividend above cash flow
      Extraordinary dividend of NOK 4 per share (USD 63.2m) in April 2026, while the newbuilding programme took the cash flow; cash fell from USD 86.4m to 35.2m, and the FCF payout ratio is 155% according to stockanalysis.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −3
    • Governance and related parties
      Kistefos (Christen Sveaas, chairman) owns 51.8%, and institutions only 1.7%; listed on Euronext Growth with low liquidity. The large AHTS vessels are part of a revenue-sharing agreement with Viking Supply Ships, which also charters out two vessels to the company and has 8 vessels managed by it, and the board was replaced in October 2026.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Customer concentration
      Subsea accounts for 85% of the firm order book, and all subsea vessels are working in Brazil; Petrobras' tenders are, according to the company, uncertain in timing until the investment plan is available after the presidential election.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −3
    • Order book and market outlook
      The firm order book has fallen to USD 573m from 701m at the turn of the year and 699m a year ago. The AHTS fleet is 74% idle for the rest of 2026 and 100% idle in 2027–2028, AHTS utilisation was 77% vs 88%, and management calls the next 6–9 months 'challenging' for AHTS.

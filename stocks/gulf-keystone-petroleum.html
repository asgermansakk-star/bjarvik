    In plain words

    Middling score (63 of 100): cheap oil production, but large political risk. The oil field was shut for several months because of the war between the US and Iran, and production fell 67% in the first half. The export agreements only run to January 2027. Management has bought shares. The merger with ShaMaran doubles the share count, and earnings rest on a high oil price.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    stockanalysis has no page for the Oslo listing (quote/osl/GKP gives 404), so the path is the London listing quote/lon/GKP, and price and currency are in GBX; the stock is traded secondarily on Euronext Growth Oslo since 18/2-2026. The financial statements are in USD and updated only half-yearly (latest data 30/6-2026). On 20/10-2026 GKP signed a binding merger agreement with ShaMaran Petroleum, but GKP is the surviving company and pays with new shares, so the price does not follow the deal terms, and there is no special situation; the share count doubles to approx. 435m on completion (general meetings approx. 22/12-2026, requires a 75% majority at GKP), and all multiples are therefore on GKP alone. The new bond of USD 175m settles approx. 10/11-2026 and is not included in stockanalysis' net cash. Stockanalysis' share count change of +6.7% is not used, because the share count only went from 216.9 to 217.4m (the retail offering in February). Only 4–5 analysts cover the stock. Insider ownership of 12.6% has not been verified (may include Lansdowne Partners' approx. 13%), and governance and customer concentration beyond sales via SOMO have not been investigated separately.

    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a share trading at less than half the value of its properties per share, but with very high debt. Debt sits at the company's own ceiling, and a third must be repaid or renewed within six months. On the plus side, tenants are spread across about 5,000 apartments, insiders own around 40%, and analysts' targets are about 22% above the share price. Watch the 10 November report.

      The entire portfolio is in the Malmö region, and 77% of the property value is housing. Malmö is growing, and few new homes are being built. On the other hand, the regulated utility-value rents limit rent growth. The project portfolio of about 600 apartments and Burlöv Center (72,000 sqm building rights) has no construction start, so it creates no earnings for now.

Before that, however, the company diluted heavily below net asset value: the share count rose from 157.5m in 2022 to 200.4m in 2024 via set-off issues and a new issue. In 2026 properties worth more than kr 500m have been bought, financed with debt, even though the loan-to-value ratio is at the company's ceiling.

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Property company: the main multiple is price vs långsiktigt substansvärde (NRV SEK 34.12 per share at 30/6-2026). At a price of 16.45, P/NRV is approx. 0.48 and P/B 0.56. The Q3 report is only published 10/11-2026, so all balance sheet figures are from Q2: it has not been investigated whether the SEK 1,073m maturing in Q3 2026 and SEK 1,343m in Q4 2026 have been refinanced, and at what margin. stockanalysis' 6.1% fall in share count is due to the redemption of Briban's 16m shares in the Briban deal (paid with properties, not cash); the 'Repurchase of Common Stock' item of SEK 54m is a buyback of a hybrid bond. The company has no credit rating. The tenants' distribution across areas (e.g. Rosengård and Sofielund) and the City of Malmö's share of rent have not been investigated.

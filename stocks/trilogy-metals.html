    In plain words

    Trilogy Metals gets 53: the company has no revenue, five employees and half of a copper project in Alaska that needs a 211-mile road which is not built, not funded and not free of lawsuits. Cash only covers the company's own costs for about 12 months, and its share of construction is more than twice its market value. The Department of Defense owns 9.1% and can reach 15.85% through warrants and an option at $0.01; the government bought at $2.17, the price is now about $1.44, and its entire stake has been registered so it can be resold. On the plus side, the project now has a fixed timetable for environmental approval in 2028. Watch the lawsuits over the road.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    New in Bjarvik (24 Nov 2026), scored in light of government equity stakes. Exploration and development company with 5 employees; its only asset is 50% of Ambler Metals (JV with South32), which owns Arctic (Cu-Zn-Pb-Au-Ag) and Bornite in Alaska. Fiscal year ends 30 Nov; the latest figures are Q3 (to 31 Aug 2026). The government stake (verified in the S-3 of 10 Nov 2026): the Department of Defense owns 16,431,140 shares (8,215,570 new units at $2.17 plus the same number bought from South32) and holds 6,161,678 warrants from Trilogy and a call option on 6,161,678 shares from South32, both at $0.01 for 10 years and exercisable only on completion of the Ambler Road (phase 1), agreed usage milestones or a change of control – 15.85% beneficial ownership in total (the tracker figure of 17.5% is the original 10% + 7.5%). Closed 11 Sep 2026 (the deal was delayed from 31 Mar). The Arctic feasibility study is from February 2023 (copper $3.65/lb); NPV at today's metal prices has not been recalculated. The Ambler Road lawsuits have not been decided on the merits. Insider trades and the shareholder base (Electrum, Paulson) have not been reviewed systematically. Price targets (median $6.50, 6 analysts) are outdated after the fall to about $1.44.

    In plain words

    Caterpillar makes construction and mining machines, plus engines and generators, partly for data centres. Sales are growing well, and the company has never had more orders. But profit per machine sold has not risen to match, and tariffs cost a lot. The share price has fallen sharply, yet the stock still costs more than usual for a company whose earnings rise and fall with building and mining.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The USD 45.2bn of debt is mostly Cat Financial (financing for customers and dealers), so net debt/EBITDA overstates industrial leverage; stockanalysis does not separate ME&T cash flow. FCF is adjusted for customer prepayments (unearned revenue). IEEPA tariff refunds are one-offs (128m in Q3, about 520m for the year). The size of dealer inventories is not quantified in the Q3 release. Insider trades were only reviewed for February–May 2026; aftermarket/service and customer concentration in Power & Energy were not examined. Deere is a poor peer right now because its earnings are at a trough. The share price has fallen about 30% from the October peak and is about 26% below the 200-day average, so price targets may lag.

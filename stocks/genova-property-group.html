    In plain words

    Genova scores 64 out of 100: a property company with high debt and low returns. Debt is about 55% of the properties' value, rent covers interest only 1.8 times, and the shares have fallen 24% in a year. On the plus side, the shares cost only 0.4 times the company's long-term value, and board members have bought shares. Watch interest costs.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −14 of −30
    • ROIC and returns
      ROIC is 2.4% (TTM) and has been 2.1–2.7% every year since 2021, while the average interest rate is 4.8% and interest cover 1.8x. Operations thus do not earn a return on the debt that finances them. ROE of 9.3% comes mainly from unrealised value increases on properties (kr 152m) and derivatives in 9M.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Rental income rose 8% in 9M, but only 2% in the comparable portfolio, and the comparable operating surplus fell 1% (unchanged in Q3) due to higher property costs. Förvaltningsresultat stood still at SEK 107m (107). The increase per share (+16%) is due to lower hybrid interest, not stronger operations; growth is bought with debt and shares.
      mb.cision.com
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      Genova has exercised the options on the remaining 49% of the subsidiary Järngrinden for about kr 800m (about 55% of market cap), paid in 2027–2030. That is equivalent to buying assets at roughly book value (minority interests kr 763m), while its own shares trade at 0.40 × NAV. Positive: the shares to Landia were transferred at kr 76.72, more than double the price.
      mb.cision.com
      −3
    • Segment and building rights
      A large part of the value lies in 7,881 residential building rights and projects (ongoing construction and projects of SEK 2.24bn of the property value of 11.0bn). The Swedish housing development market is weak, and building-right sales are waiting for local plans to gain legal force (approx. SEK 140m outstanding, H1 2027 at the earliest).
      mb.cision.com
      −2

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −17 of −35
    • Debt and interest
      The loan-to-value ratio is 54.7% (55.4 at year-end), interest cover 1.8× (LTM) and net debt/EBITDA 19×. Debt maturity is only 2.4 years, and the fixed-interest period has fallen to 2.0 years (2.7). About kr 700m falls due within 12 months, and 46% of debt has its interest renewed in 2026 at 7.3% incl. margin. Hybrid bonds of kr 550m are counted as equity.
      mb.cision.com
      −8
    • Cash flow and financing need
      Operating cash flow was kr 178m (TTM), but kr 91m of that came from working capital; underlying it is approx. kr 87m before hybrid interest (approx. kr 30m a year). At the same time approx. 30% of financing costs on projects are capitalised (kr 98m in 9M), so the förvaltningsresultat overstates cash earnings. Against that stand Järngrinden (approx. kr 800m), the rest of the Landia portfolio at year-end and projects, while liquidity incl. unused credit lines is kr 950m.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Valuation and project risk
      Property values were written up by SEK 152m in 9M, and NAV rests on valuations where +/−0.25 percentage points in required yield moves the loan-to-value ratio by approx. 2 percentage points (adjusted 53–57%). The building-rights portfolio's excess value of SEK 1.4bn is an internal/external estimate without a market price.

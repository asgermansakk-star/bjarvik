    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a solid company at a fair price, but with signs of slowing down. Revenue and profit have fallen, the profit margin has dropped three years in a row, and much of the sales depend on a few customers, especially oil and gas. On the plus side, there is no interest-bearing debt, and a board member has bought shares. Watch the cash balance, which has shrunk.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Only 2 analysts cover the stock on stockanalysis (both price targets kr 50), so upside and estimates are thin. The price of kr 49 is after the ex-date for the supplementary dividend of kr 0.70 on 6/11. Stockanalysis' net debt (−94m) is lease debt alone; the company has no interest-bearing debt. The ROIC figures on stockanalysis (76–384%) swing wildly because of negative working capital and have not been used; ROCE of approx. 70–76% shows a capital-light model. The cash fall in 2026 is partly due to the abolition of the tax withholding account (one-off shift). The share from Equinor alone is not disclosed; concentration has been assessed on sector share and the 20 largest customers. The company gives no financial guidance. Short interest and management remuneration have not been investigated.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −13 of −30
    • Margin and returns
      ROIC is 2.1% vs WACC of 2.9% and has been 1.0–3.8% since 2021. ROE was 1.9% in 2025 and 0.7% in 2024. The properties earned 4.6% in 2025 vs an average borrowing rate of 6.1% (return margin −1.5%), and in H1 2026 the margin was only 0.8%. Even on the downgraded EBVAT guidance of kr 45–50m the ongoing return is approx. 4.7% of equity before tax.
      attachment.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −6
    • Quality of growth
      EBVAT has fallen from kr 56.9m in 2021 via 42.9 and 41.4m to 2.8m in 2024 and 11.5m in 2025. Rental income was kr 133m in 2025 vs 145.5m in 2023, and net revenue fell again in H1 2026 (71.7 vs 73.7m) due to divestments. Equity has fallen from kr 1,217m to 998m since 2021. The improvement in 2026 comes from lower interest expenses, not from operations.
      attachment.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −4
    • Moat and segment
      The portfolio is a mix of 32 properties. 31% of the value is retail, mostly in secondary provincial towns (Næstved, Holbæk, Herning, Kolding, Lillerød). 13% is office, and in addition there are two hotels and 'design' properties in Glostrup and Odense, which were bought with over 80% vacancy. Only the Pulse housing (55% of the value) is a clear core. The company has 16 employees and no scale advantages against larger landlords.
      attachment.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −3

EV/EBITDA 20.4 is at the low end of its own history (19.1–28.8 in 2021–2025) and below Jeudan's 30.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 7 findings    −26 of −35
    • Debt and interest rate sensitivity
      Net debt is kr 1.10bn (LTV approx. 50% vs a covenant of 65%), net debt/EBITDA is 12.8, and interest cover is only 1.86. The company has no interest hedging. The annual report states that 1,022 of 1,153m must be repriced within 12 months (Cibor6 597m plus F3/F5), and the regional portfolio must be refinanced in Q1 2027. It is the same profile as Copenhagen Capital, which scored −8.
      attachment.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −7
    • Letting and vacancy
      Average vacancy was 20.1% in 2025 (20.6% in 2024): retail 21.7%, office 18.7%, warehouse 39.0% and residential 4.7%. Management has promised full letting in Glostrup and 100% in the regional portfolio's residential units before the end of 2026, but this is not yet documented.
      attachment.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −3
    • Guidance and execution
      EBVAT guidance for 2025 of kr 20–25m was lowered to 12m, and the result was 11.5m (weaker hotels and a new office community in Odense). Guidance for 2026 was lowered after H1 from kr 50–55m to 45–50m because of unspecified costs for settlement and structuring. With 19.4m in H1, guidance requires 26–31m in H2, and that depends on the timing of divestments.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −3
    • Governance and main shareholder
      Park Street Asset Management (London) owns 94.61% after the company bought 10m B shares from the main shareholder's own company in 2024 and cancelled them. CEO Pradeep Pattem sits on the board, and three of six board members are from the Pattem family, including a student son. Software is bought from the related party Xplore Review (kr 1.8m in 2025). With over 90%, the main shareholder can squeeze out the minority.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −5
    • Reporting and control
      In 2024 the company by mistake in the share register cancelled 2.14m A shares belonging to the main shareholder, and the Danish Business Authority had to reverse the capital reduction. The 2023 half-year report was corrected, and so was the 2024 annual report. The 2025 annual report states price/book value as 0.3, even though the figure is 0.74 (16.70/22.6).
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −2
    • Liquidity in the stock
      On average 2,584 shares a day are traded (about kr 39,000), and free float is only about 2.3m of 43.4m shares (5.4%). The B shares are unlisted. It is practically impossible to get in and out of a position, and price formation is thin.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Valuation of properties
      The properties are valued at a weighted yield requirement of 6.48% (6.59% in 2024). If the yield requirement rises 0.25 percentage points, the value falls by kr 109m, approx. 11% of equity. In H1 2026 only properties with rent changes were revalued, and the NOI figures in the strategy are 'estimated and unaudited'.
      attachment.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Property company: the main multiple is price vs intrinsic value (DKK 23.0 per share at 30/6-2026). The company only publishes half-year reports, so there are no Q3 figures, and the latest report is H1 2026 (25/8). There is no analyst coverage and no forward estimate; P/E is calculated on the company's EBVAT guidance. Park Street Asset Management owns 94.61% and is thus above the 90% threshold for compulsory acquisition. No compulsory acquisition or offer has been announced, so special situation has not been set, but a buyout can come at any time and on terms the minority has no influence over. Insider trades beyond the main shareholder's and the costs of 'settlement and structuring' in 2026 have not been investigated in detail, because the search budget was used up. The analysis is based on the company's announcements via Nasdaq and stockanalysis.com. Customer and tenant concentration has not been investigated (0 points).

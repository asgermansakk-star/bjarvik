    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −9 of −30
    • Profitability
      ROE was only 11.6% in Q3 and 12.4% over the last 12 months, and guidance for 2026 is approx. 13%. It is low for a consumer lending bank with unsecured loans and credit cards and in line with 9.9–13.4% in 2021–2025; the ambition of over 22% in 2030 is not guidance.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Business model and moat
      Digital niche bank without a real moat, distributing via agents and digital channels (prepaid agent commission of kr 261m on the balance sheet). Credit cards make up 19% of lending and had a loss of 8.5% annualised in Q3, and business loans 5.7%.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Half of the record lending growth in Q3 came from German credit cards, which were only launched in Q2 2025. Credit losses rose 51% in the first nine months of the year (kr 216m vs 142m) vs 35% growth in gross lending in a year, and the loss rate rose to 3.5% from 2.5% a year ago, so growth is happening in the riskiest segment.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −2

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −14 of −35
    • Capital and dilution
      CET1 fell from 19.7% at the turn of the year to 16.1%, only 2.1 percentage points (about kr 164m) over the requirement incl. P2G. In Q3 risk-weighted assets rose kr 708m, while CET1 capital rose only kr 31m, so the buffer lasts only a few quarters at the current growth. The ambition of 27% annual lending growth towards 2030 cannot be financed by an ROE of 12–13%, and the share count has already risen from 378 to 465m since 2024 (issue of kr 205m in 2025 and options).
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −5
    • Credit quality
      Stage 3 loans (over 90 days past due) rose to kr 935m or 8.4% of gross lending (kr 613m at the turn of the year and kr 849m after Q2), and the provisioning ratio is only 36%. Even mortgages have 6.4% in stage 3, and business loans had increased individual provisions on defaulted exposures.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Macro and regulation
      Management itself expects rising interest rates in Norway and the euro area to increase funding costs and press customers' repayment ability. The EU's new consumer credit directive (CCD2) applies in Germany and Finland from 20 November 2026 with stricter requirements that hit the bank's two growth markets for cards and consumer loans.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −2
    • Governance and insider trades
      COO, CTO, CFO, CRO and others exercised 3.6m options at NOK 1.48 and sold 2.3m shares at NOK 4.85 in August, and a deputy board member sold in September; management owns only 3.1% in total. Kistefos owns 23.7% and has a representative on the board, and the 20 largest own 70.5%, so free float is small.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −2
    • Execution
      The move of domicile to Finland was started and abandoned again in June, and the mortgage business had to be reorganised in Q3 with a review of mandates and control systems, so growth there almost stood still (+kr 28m).
      newsweb.oslobors.no

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful (negative operating cash flow is due to loan growth); valuation on P/TBV and P/E, and dividend is measured against net profit. The stock trades on Euronext Growth Oslo (not the main list), which means lighter disclosure requirements. stockanalysis calculates P/B and P/TBV on equity including NOK 145m of AT1 capital; on shareholders' equity the multiples are approx. 8% higher. stockanalysis has no forecast page and no price targets for the stock, so analyst coverage has not been examined. Peers are only Morrow Bank and Norion Bank; Lea Bank, Komplett Bank and Avarda have not been retrieved. The provisioning model for the German credit cards has not been examined in detail (0 points). The Q3 report came out on 6/11-2026, the same day as the analysis.

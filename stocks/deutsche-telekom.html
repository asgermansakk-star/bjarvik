    In plain words

    Deutsche Telekom gets 97. Growth is low, and about two thirds of revenue comes from its US subsidiary T-Mobile, whose share has fallen 31% in a year as rivals cut prices. Debt is large, at €141bn. On the plus side, expectations were raised and dividends plus buybacks come to about €9bn. Watch the plans to merge with T-Mobile, which one shareholder opposes.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −12 of −35
    • Competition in the US
      The TMUS share has fallen 31% over a year: Verizon's 'Value Pivot' with price cuts and a three-year price guarantee presses price growth of 2.5–3% per account, and TMUS has stopped reporting postpaid phone additions. The main asset is under real competitive pressure.
      lightreading.com
      −4
    • New entrant in the US (SpaceX/Starlink)
      SpaceX plans a nationwide 5G network with about 79 MHz of spectrum, Qualcomm components and access to up to 80,000 towers; TMUS has lost about a fifth of its value this year, mostly after Starlink's announcement in July. Höttges wants to 'accelerate' the US strategy without more capex and estimates that a full network would cost SpaceX about 200bn; no measurable effect yet, but a risk of price pressure.
      lightreading.com
      −2
    • Merger plans and capital allocation
      The plan for a full merger with T-Mobile US has been neither confirmed nor abandoned: at Q3 Höttges called an acquisition of TMUS a 'theoretical consideration' and declined to comment on the merger rumours. According to Semafor, TMUS' management rejected a merger in July, Elliott opposes the plan in favour of larger buybacks, and the CFO caps buybacks at EUR 2bn in 2027. The strategy remains unresolved, with a risk of value-destroying share issuance.
      boersen-zeitung.de

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Re-evaluated after the report of 2026-11-05. Telecom (no suitable industry; nearest chosen as for Telenor). T-Mobile US (about 53% owned, 66% of revenue in Q3) is fully consolidated, so stockanalysis' FCF (29.4bn) and P/FCF 4.3 include the minority share; the company's FCF AL guidance is about 20.1bn. The stake in TMUS is worth about USD 95bn (about EUR 83bn) of a market cap of EUR 128bn. Reported EPS is distorted by one-off gains in 2023, and the difference between reported and adjusted net profit (EUR 1.1bn in 9M) is not specified in the release. SpaceX/Starlink's planned US mobile network is new since the previous analysis and stands as a separate finding, apart from Verizon's price pressure. The state's stake (about 28% via KfW and the federal government) and German regulation (fibre, spectrum) have not been investigated. The merger plan with TMUS is not binding and does not give a special situation.

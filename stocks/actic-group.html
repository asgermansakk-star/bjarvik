    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a Swedish fitness chain recovered from loss-making years but with high debt. The biggest drags are low margins, debt including leasing of 793m SEK against a market value of about 620m, and few owners with thin trading. The price rose from 5.78 to 26.70 SEK since 2024. The CEO has bought shares. The January 2027 takeover of Gustavsvik is worth watching.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −11 of −30
    • Margin and ROIC
      The EBIT margin excluding IFRS 16 is only 9.2% over the last 12 months (kr 63.7m on revenue of 693m), and ROIC is 8.8%. After the loss-making years 2020–2024, retained earnings stand at −kr 401m, and tangible equity is negative at −kr 270m, because goodwill (513m) is larger than equity (296m).
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      In Q3, revenue rose 8.7% in comparable centres (price +6.2% per member, members +3.2%), but for the first nine months EBIT rose only 0.1%, excluding the sold Norwegian centres. The number of members per centre fell 2.8%. Q3 is the first quarter with a clear lift.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Moat
      Fitness has low barriers to entry and tough competition from larger chains and low-cost chains. Actic's niche is gyms in municipal swimming pools, but those contracts are put out to public tender and can be lost; the Gustavsvik tender was appealed and only decided in court in October 2026.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The company has gone from optimising the centres to growing through acquisitions: Ego in Sundsvall was bought for SEK 35m (of which 15m in new shares) plus an earn-out of up to 25m, and Gustavsvik is taken over in January 2027. The price for Ego is reasonable (revenue of 35m and a 22% margin), but the acquisitions are made at a gearing of 2.2x, close to the target of at most 2.5x.
      mfn.se
      −1

    • Lease debt and operating gearing
      Lease debt is kr 523m and bank debt kr 280m, together kr 793m against a market value of about kr 620m. Net debt is 3.1x EBITDA vs 2.7x a year ago, interest cover 2.45 and Altman Z-score 0.51. The rent is fixed (kr 121m a year), so a fall in membership hits hard.

    • Governance and liquidity
      Three owners hold approx. 76% of the shares: Pure Zen (board member Johan Ahldin) 28.9%, AFA Försäkring 23.7% and Axel H. Ågren Placering 23.5%. Free float is approx. 9m shares, and on average only approx. 25,000 shares a day are traded (approx. SEK 0.65m). The company pays no dividend.

      Gustavsvik (baths, adventure pool and gym, in cooperation with First Camp) is according to the company expected to give kr 70–80m in revenue, about 10% on top of the current. It is a new and large facility, taken over as early as January 2027 after a long court case, at the same time as Ego is to be integrated. The company has not stated any margin.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    No analyst coverage (stockanalysis has no forecast page), so there is no forward P/E, price target or EPS estimates; PEG points become n/a. Stockanalysis' price history only goes back to 2021, when the company was in crisis, so its own 5–10-year history has only been partly examined. Multiples with IFRS 16 (EV/EBITDA 5.6) and without (EV/EBIT about 13) give very different pictures; the findings use the company's figures without IFRS 16 where possible. The result for the last 12 months does not include the gain of kr 31.9m from the sale of the Norwegian centres (it was in the first half of 2025). Gustavsvik's margin and start-up costs are not disclosed. Competitors' membership numbers and prices in Sweden have not been examined. The stock is very illiquid.

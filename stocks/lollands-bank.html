    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −10 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Profit after tax per kr 100 share fell from kr 508.9 in 2023 to 458.5 in 2024 and 415.6 in 2025, and core earnings in H1 2026 (kr 47.7m) are below H1 2024 (51.6) and H1 2023 (53.3). Net interest and fee income has stood still for three years (H1: kr 146.4m, 141.1m, 142.8m); the progress in 2026 comes from price adjustments, reversed impairments and the BEC sale.

    • Efficiency and ROE
      Return on equity after tax fell from 14.1% in 2023 to 11.3% in 2024 and 9.4% in 2025 (4.5% in H1 2026, approx. 9% p.a.). Costs of DKK 95.3m vs income of DKK 142.9m in H1 give a cost ratio of approx. 67% (Djurslands Bank approx. 53%), and payroll costs rose DKK 4.7m after a decision to hire more staff.
      lollandsbank.dk
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      The capital ratio is 29.6% vs a total requirement of 20.5%, a surplus of 9.1 percentage points vs the bank's own target of 4. The excess capital (about DKK 150m) is not distributed: the dividend is 33% of profit, and there is no buyback, so the share count has been unchanged at 216,000 (DKK 100 shares) for five years.

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −9 of −35
    • Interest rate sensitivity
      Deposits exceed loans by kr 2.1bn (loans/deposits 54.5%), and the surplus sits at the central bank and in bonds. In January the bank expected falling interest income, and the upgrades in July and October are justified by a higher interest rate level and larger price adjustments – so earnings follow the interest rate more than the business.
      lollandsbank.dk
      −2
    • Credit quality and concentration
      Impairments have been income for five years (−kr 5.1m in H1 2026), and the accumulated impairment ratio has fallen from 6.5% in 2021 to 3.9%. At the same time lending rose 11.9% and the sum of the 20 largest exposures from 77.1% to 93.0% in half a year; real estate (15%, incl. temporary financings) and agriculture (14%) are the largest sectors. Losses can only normalise upwards.
      lollandsbank.dk
      −2

      Sparekassen Sjælland-Fyn – a competitor in the same region – owns 24.9% and AHJ A/S 10.1%, so free float is approx. 65% (696,625 shares). Insiders own only 0.45%, and the board and executive board together owned under 5,000 shares at the end of 2025.

DKK 15-20m based on the upgrade on 24/4 (the gross proceeds of DKK 55m include repaid capital contribution). The first-to-third-quarter announcement will not come until 18/11 2026 (stockanalysis writes 13/11), so figures are as of H1 2026 plus the upgrade on 16/10. Insider trades in 2026 have not been examined (reporting files could not be read); management bought shares in 2025 according to the annual report, but the amounts are small.

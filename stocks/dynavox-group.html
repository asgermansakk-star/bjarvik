    In plain words

    The model sees a growing medical technology company that depends heavily on the US. A full 74% of sales come from North America, where public programmes and insurers pay, and case handling has slowed. Growth excluding acquisitions has fallen from 34% to 12%. In its favour are insider purchases and falling debt. Watch the reimbursement system and the weaker dollar, which has cost 7% of sales.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Only 3–4 analysts cover the stock. Peers are measured on stockanalysis multiples for Ambu and Demant (not pure AAC companies; there are no listed direct competitors in the Nordics). Supplier dependence (e.g. on eye-tracking components) and the effect of the US Medicaid savings from 2027 are not quantified and score 0. The company gives no short-term guidance, only financial targets (20% growth, EBIT margin above 15%). The TTM cash flow at stockanalysis repeats the 2025 figures for receivables and inventory, so the FCF figures are an estimate.

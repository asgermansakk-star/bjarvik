    In plain words

    The score is high mainly because the shares are cheap relative to expected profit. Revenue and the number of consultants on assignment are falling, and the company earns very little on each krona of sales. The dividend is larger than this year's profit. The chairman has bought shares for about SEK 30m, and the third quarter looked stable. The shares trade in small volumes only.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

      ROIC has fallen to 16.4% from 37–43% in 2021–2024 and 25.4% in 2025, and ROE to 22.6% from 47–61%. The return is still above WACC (6.1%), but the trend is clearly downward, and the high ROE is due to very thin equity.

    • Quality of growth
      Revenue fell 8.4% in 2024, 12.8% in 2025 and 11.9% in the first nine months of 2026; the number of consultants on assignment fell to 8,911 from 10,373 (−14%) in Q3, and order intake fell 8.9% in Q3. EPS has more than halved from kr 8.01 in 2024 to 3.07 (TTM). Part of the decline in 2025 was a planned phase-out of unprofitable contracts.
      mb.cision.com
      −5
    • Future segment
      The business is brokering external IT, R&D and engineering consultants. Several customers introduced consultant freezes or cut back in 2025, and competition has increased; AI is changing the content of many roles and may squeeze demand for traditional IT consultant hours, even though the company sees growth in AI, data and security competencies.
      mb.cision.com
      −3
    • Moat
      A gross margin of only 4.1% shows an intermediary position with limited pricing power; framework agreements and the MSP role (e.g. Tetra Pak) give some stickiness, but customers can re-tender, and the company itself describes increased competition in Sweden.
      mb.cision.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The dividend of SEK 4 (SEK 69.1m) in 2026 corresponds to 130% of net profit of SEK 53m (TTM), and in 2025 the payout ratio was 159%; the equity ratio is 8.0%. At the same time funds are used for expansion in the Netherlands and Germany (purchase of a dormant company for EUR 66,000 to obtain an AÜG licence), while the core markets shrink.

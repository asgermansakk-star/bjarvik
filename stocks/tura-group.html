    • Quality of growth
      Revenue fell from SEK 919m in 2022 to 826m in 2024. The 18% growth in 2025 came mainly from the purchase of Exertis' Nordic Entertainment division (approx. SEK 116m in revenue). Q2 2026 delivered +11.5%, mainly organic, but the CEO expects growth to slow when the division is included in the comparison figures.

    • Moat and future segment
      Tura mainly distributes others' brands within electronics accessories, books and toys to retail with a gross margin of around 20%. The company itself cites direct import, technology shifts and fashion trends as risks, and own brands make up only a minor part of sales. There is no clear pricing power.

Happy Plugs, which accounted for SEK 37.6m of the goodwill, has now been folded into Tura Scandinavia.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    No analyst coverage and no forward estimate, so PEG and upside have no data basis. The latest trade on stockanalysis is from 30/9-2026 (SEK 4.50), and only approx. 3,600 shares are traded a day, so the price may be outdated. The company itself writes that the stock is listed on Spotlight Stock Market, while stockanalysis states NGM; the trading venue has been set according to the item. The accounts follow K3: goodwill is amortised on a straight-line basis over 5 years (approx. SEK 9m a year), which depresses reported EPS relative to IFRS peers, and leasing sits in operating costs, so FCF is after leasing. Customer concentration is not scored: the annual report mentions both a 'very broad customer base' and customer concentration as a risk, but gives no figures. The Q3 report comes 18/11-2026 and is not included. The insider register has not been examined; the annual report only states that no senior persons sold shares in 2025, so no points have been given for insider trading.

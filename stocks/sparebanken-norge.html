    In plain words

    The score of 67 shows a profitable and fairly safe bank, but at a price above both its own history and its competitors. Interest income and profit per equity certificate have fallen, and more business loans have gone unpaid. On the other hand, the dividend yields 5.9% and 97% of retail loans are secured on homes. Watch the integration of two mergers and new rules for savings banks.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bank with equity certificates: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful; valuation on P/B (per equity certificate) and P/E, and dividend is measured against the equity certificates' share of profit (ownership fraction approx. 41%). Stockanalysis' P/B (0.63) and book value per share are calculated on total equity incl. primary capital and are not used; the bank's own P/B per certificate (1.82 at end of Q3, approx. 1.74 today) is used. The mergers with Sparebanken Sør (2/5-2025) and Oslofjord Sparebank (1/12-2025) distort the stockanalysis history: share count +28% and revenue growth are merger effects, and the bank's own pro forma figures are used; P/B history before 2023 has not been retrieved from the company. The completed buyback (407,458 certificates, approx. NOK 86m) mainly covers the employee programme and is not scored. Only 5 analysts cover the stock (consensus Hold), and stockanalysis has only 2 EPS estimates for 2026. SpareBank 1 SMN and Østlandet are not used as peers. Insider trades are only the employee programme (30% discount) and are not scored. The proposal from the savings bank committee may also give structural benefits for certificate holders; only the risk is scored.

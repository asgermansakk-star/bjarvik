    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −11 of −30
    • ROIC and returns
      ROIC is 2.5% vs WACC of 4.3% and has been 1.4–2.8% since 2021 with no improvement. ROE of 8.3% is lifted by revaluations of properties (kr 338.5m) and interest rate derivatives (kr 105.0m) in 9M.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Profit from property management per share rose 14.6% in 9M, but adjusted for a one-off dividend of SEK 23.6m from Turako, growth was only 1.2%. In Q3 profit from property management fell to SEK 61.5m (69.8), or SEK 0.83 per share (0.92, −9.6%), because the average interest rate rose to 3.26% (2.97). Rent growth of 5.7% is partly due to the acquisition in Uppsala; regulated housing rents rose 3.2–3.6%.
      corporate.johnmattson.se
      −4
    • Future segment
      89% of property value is housing in the Stockholm region and Uppsala, where demand is high, and the housing occupancy rate is 98.7%. The bruksvärde rents, however, limit rent growth to the annual negotiation, so growth must come from apartment upgrades (about 1,500 possible at about 5% direct yield).
      corporate.johnmattson.se
      −1
    • Capital allocation
      The company has bought own shares for kr 129m since 2025 at approx. half of net asset value, which is sensible. At the same time it is returning to new construction (the care home Villa Västerled in Bromma) and bostadsrätt projects in joint venture with Besqab, while the loan-to-value ratio is close to the ceiling. That increases project risk.
      corporate.johnmattson.se
      −1

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −15 of −35
    • Debt and refinancing
      The loan-to-value ratio rose to 47.6% (46.1) vs the company's cap of 50%, and net debt/EBITDA is 17.6×. Interest cover fell to 2.0× in Q3 (2.6). All financing is bank debt with a debt maturity of only 2.1 years: kr 1,771m (24%) falls due within a year and a further 35% within two years. Available liquidity is only kr 406m, and cash was −kr 3.2m at quarter-end. The fixed-interest period has fallen to 2.5 years (3.2), and swaps cover 68.5% of floating-rate debt (87.1% at year-end).
      corporate.johnmattson.se
      −8
    • Cash flow and financing need
      Operating cash flow was DKK 218m over 12 months, while investments in own properties were DKK 331m and acquisitions DKK 227m net. Buybacks (DKK 129m) and dividend (DKK 19m) are therefore financed with property sales (DKK 238m) and new debt.
      corporate.johnmattson.se
      −2
    • Valuation of properties
      The properties were written up by kr 338.5m in 9M, even though market rates have risen, and the average valuation yield has only been raised to 3.5% (3.4 at the turn of the year). That is only approx. 0.2 percentage points above the average interest rate of 3.26%, and the variable part of the debt costs 5.81% incl. credit margin. According to the company's own sensitivity, a 10% fall in value lifts the loan-to-value ratio to 52.8%, i.e. above the ceiling of 50%.
      corporate.johnmattson.se
      −2
    • Governance and ownership concentration
      AB Borudan Ett (Kerstin Skarne, daughter of John Mattson) owns about 38% and Tagehus Holding (the Ljungberg family, with Johan Ljungberg as deputy chair) about 13%. Free float is only about 32m of 74m shares, and on average only about 31,500 shares a day are traded (about kr 1.7m). Insiders themselves own only 0.08% according to stockanalysis.
      affarsvarlden.se
      −2
    • Execution and management
      CFO Ebba Pilo Karth left the company in July at her own request, and an interim CFO took over. This happened in a quarter with rising interest rates and a large refinancing need. David Juhlin has since been appointed as new CFO.

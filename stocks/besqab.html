    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Besqab (formerly Aros Bostad, which took over Besqab in 2024) reports under the segment principle (successive recognition) and under IFRS (profit only on handover); the findings are based primarily on segment figures. stockanalysis' P/E of 4.13 is based on EPS of kr 6.08, which is wrong (average share count miscalculated after the merger); the company's own EPS is kr 2.78 (segment) and kr 2.57 (IFRS) over 12 months, corresponding to P/E about 9–10. stockanalysis' −42.5% in share count is also a data error; actual buybacks are 1.55m ordinary shares. Market cap, EV and P/B at stockanalysis do not include the 11.68m listed preference shares B (annual dividend of kr 7.50), which are valued at about kr 100 per share based on an older price (about kr 97 in March 2026); the current preference share price and the redemption terms have not been verified. The IFRS result for Jan.–Sep. contains negative goodwill of kr 41.6m and −kr 47.2m on the consolidation of Wallin, which largely offset each other. Only 2 analysts have price targets and 1 has an EPS estimate; the price targets (kr 41–49.50) are probably outdated. Customer behaviour beyond the sales rate and credit terms in the construction loans have not been examined.

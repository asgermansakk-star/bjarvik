    In plain words

    GlobalFoundries gets 72: the price is at the low end of its own history but almost level with TSMC, even though margins and the return on invested money are far lower. Revenue is below 2022, and investment is set to rise to about $4bn in 2027, so cash flow does not support the price. The US government receives shares as payment for grants and will end up with about 7% according to the CEO; that means about 9% more shares and brought an accounting cost that caused a loss in the third quarter. On the plus side, net cash is about $1.5bn and the customer base is broad with long contracts. Watch how much of the growth rests on government support.

    In the 70+ zone: a score above 70 with more than 20% upside to the median analyst target.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    New analysis 24 Nov 2026 (government equity stakes). The government stakes are verified in GF's own 6-Ks: (1) Securities Issuance Agreement of 3 Sep 2026 with the Department of Commerce (DOC): 9,907,399 shares at an 'issuance price' of $37.85 (about $375m), tied to the CHIPS R&D grant of up to $375m for a quantum foundry (letter of intent 21 May, final 8 Sep); the government may vote only on matters concerning the share class and mergers, may not sell privately to competitors and has been given a resale prospectus (F-3ASR 15 Sep). (2) A letter of intent from July on $300m for silicon photonics with about a 1% stake. (3) 14 Oct 2026: a separate agreement gives DOC about 4% more in connection with the reallocation of $955m in CHIPS support; CEO Tim Breen says the government's total stake will be about 7%. Together about $1.63bn spread over three grants. The shares are in effect payment for grants: the September issue produced a non-cash expense of $441m and an IFRS loss in Q3, and future issues are not included in guidance. The trailing P/E (about 105) is distorted by this item; the forward P/E is based on non-IFRS EPS. Stockanalysis shows only +0.1% in share count over a year, but the company guides about 575m diluted shares in Q4 and about 600m in 2027 (551m in Q3). Mubadala (Abu Dhabi) is the majority owner; only about 157m of 557m shares are free float. Cyclical set to yes: mature nodes follow the inventory and utilisation cycle (revenue −9% in both 2023 and 2024, utilisation in the high 80s%). The Japanese-financed fabs (up to $21bn, first in 2029) generate fee income, but the terms have not been disclosed. Insider trading, customer concentration and the Q3 earnings call have not been reviewed; IFRS figures, reported in USD.

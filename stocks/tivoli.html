    In plain words

    The score is middling: a unique asset, but the share is expensive relative to earnings, which are falling. Visitor numbers are flat, profit before tax fell 62% in the first nine months, and the cash return to owners is almost zero. Heavy investment has squeezed cash flow. In its favour, the park is one of a kind. The fourth quarter, with Christmas, must deliver a strong result.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −12 of −30
    • Quality of growth

Growth comes from price and spend per guest and the takeover of the gaming business, while the costs of Hikari and 'strategic initiatives' eat up more than the improvement.     Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    No analyst coverage (stockanalysis /forecast/ gives 404), so forward P/E is an estimate based on the company's guidance (profit before tax about kr 145m, 23% tax, 5.72m shares = EPS about kr 19.5). Upside, PEG and revision points are therefore based on missing data. stockanalysis shows +1.8% in share count, but the balance sheet and annual report show an unchanged 5,716,660 shares and no issue in the cash flow, so it gives no points. Tivoli is strongly seasonal (loss in H1, profit in Q3 and Q4), so quarterly TTM figures swing; net debt as of 30/9 is seasonally high. Peers are Compagnie des Alpes and Six Flags; Parken (Lalandia) is only used as a Danish reference. The quarterly report PDF and 2026 segment figures (incl. revenue from the acquired gaming business) have not been read; only the headline figures from the company announcements. Web search was used up during the research, so press and any insider trades in 2026 have not been examined beyond the annual report's holdings as of 31/12-2025. Not examined: customer concentration (not relevant for an amusement park with 4.3m guests beyond the tourist share).

    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a large property company whose share trades at about half the value of its properties after debt. That is cheap, but debt is high and three of the company's four financial targets are breached. The return on capital is below the cost of capital, and earnings per share have fallen. Many tenants and buybacks below value count in its favour. Analysts see only about 4% upside.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Property company: price vs long-term net asset value (NAV SEK 97.03 at 30/9-2026) is the main multiple; at a price of 52.26, P/NAV is approx. 0.54 and P/B 0.64 on equity per share (stockanalysis shows P/B 0.56 incl. minorities). Ongoing earnings are measured as förvaltningsresultat per share in the company's earnings capacity (SEK 5.27) and SEK 4,900m after tax; stockanalysis' P/E 11.3 is affected by value adjustments and derivatives. Norion Bank was distributed in May 2026, so figures for 2025 include Norion, and stockanalysis' history of earnings per share is not fully comparable. stockanalysis shows +0.69% in share count, but the report shows 29m repurchased shares and 1,157m outstanding vs 1,190m a year ago; no points are given for dilution. The properties are valued internally, only parts externally. Insider trades in 2025-2026 have not been investigated (the search quota ran out), and the finances of Hedin Mobility Group as a related-party tenant have not been investigated. The convertible's conversion price and size have not been investigated. The price targets on stockanalysis were last updated 11/5-2026, and only 6-7 analysts cover the stock. Peers are only compared on P/B (Castellum and Wallenstam) from stockanalysis.

    In plain words

    The model sees a medical company with a promising treatment but a very high price. Revenue is only about 2.5m EUR against a large loss, and the stock is pricier relative to sales than ever before. The founder owns 46.5% and in effect has control. On the other hand, there is 41.5m EUR in cash, no debt, and US approval came in August. The US launch has only just begun.

    The model found several serious problems. That does not mean the stock will fall.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −21 of −30
    • Margin
      Revenue was EUR 2.47m TTM vs an operating loss of EUR 19.9m, i.e. an operating margin of −805%. The adjusted gross margin is high (94% in Q2), but administrative costs alone (EUR 12.6m in 2025) are six times revenue. There have been losses every year, and the operating loss has been EUR 18–25m a year since 2022.
      stockanalysis.com
      −11
    • Quality of growth
      Sales rose 66% in Q2 to EUR 717,000 and 34% in H1, but in 2025 growth was only 7% (EUR 2.07m vs 1.94m). After several years with CE marking and over 60 Centers of Excellence, only approx. 1,800 patients have been treated, and revenue in Europe is still approx. EUR 2–3m a year.
      implantica.com
      −5
    • Moat
      RefluxStop has FDA approval (PMA), patents and five-year data, and a study of 602 patients showed under 2% serious events. The product must nevertheless compete with established treatments (Nissen fundoplication, magnetic sphincter augmentation and medication), and in Germany reimbursement via InEK is still missing.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Capital allocation
      The accumulated deficit is EUR 437.6m. Capitalised development of EUR 32m is amortised at EUR 1.3m a year, and EUR 1.26m was written down in 2025. Management spends resources on a broad pipeline (eHealth and wireless energy, over 25,000 pages of patent applications) which it believes will 'change healthcare forever', while the core product is not yet profitable.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −19 of −35
    • Funding need
      Cash was EUR 41.7m at 30/6, and there is no interest-bearing debt. Operating cash flow was −EUR 3.9m in Q2 (−EUR 7.6m in H1), so cash lasts approx. 2.5 years at current spending. Building an organisation in the US (sales, training, reimbursement, logistics) will however increase spending, and the company has said nothing about financing. An issue within 1–2 years is therefore likely.
      implantica.com
      −4
    • Commercial rollout and reimbursement
      The US launch has only just begun: the first commercial procedures were performed at USC on 4/11-2026, and management expects adoption to 'build up gradually' centre by centre. There is no dedicated US procedure code, and in Germany there is still no InEK reimbursement after several years (7 reporting hospitals).
      mfn.se
      −6
    • Governance and control
      The founder Peter Forsell is both CEO and inventor and owns 46.5% of the capital. With 1,125m B shares at CHF 0.02 vs 58.4m A shares at CHF 2.00 he effectively has control of the company, which is domiciled in Liechtenstein and listed on First North. Management's total remuneration was EUR 2.0m in 2025, equal to the whole year's revenue.

      CFO Andreas Öhrnberg sold 63,300 depositary receipts at SEK 71–72 on 19–23/9-2025, shortly after subscribing for exactly 63,300 shares via the incentive programme in July 2025. There are no registered insider purchases in the market.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Stockholm listing (IMP A SDB) does not exist on stockanalysis (quote/sto/IMP.A.SDB and variants give 404), so the path is set to the Frankfurt line quote/fra/8IM1 in EUR (price EUR 6.04 on 6/11-2026). The OTC line IPLTF has not traded since 13/10. The market cap at stockanalysis (approx. EUR 424m) is based on 69.63m A equivalents: 58.38m A shares plus 1,125m B shares at 1/100 of the A share's par value. That matches the company's share capital. Stockanalysis shows +60.7%/−75.4% in share count, but that is an artefact of the two share classes. According to the company, no new shares have been issued since 2021 beyond employee shares, so no dilution points have been given. P/TBV of 9.1 is misleading, because capitalised development of EUR 32m was moved to intangible assets in 2026. P/B is therefore used. No analysts at stockanalysis (forecast 404); only Pareto covers the company. The voting split between A and B shares and whether the B shares are owned directly by Peter Forsell are not confirmed in the articles of association. Customer and centre data beyond the company's own announcements have not been investigated. The Q3 report is due 19/11-2026 and may change the picture of the costs of the US launch.

    In plain words

    A score of 61 shows a company after a major divestment. Selling the European production caused a write-down of SEK 1.2bn, and the remaining business is not growing. Over 40% of revenue comes from the UK, and carmakers still matter. Margins have improved, and the sales should almost remove net debt. The finance chief has left, and the share is up 75% in a year.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bulten divested its European contract manufacturing for the automotive industry (about kr 1,900m in revenue, 38% of the group) and the Tianjin unit in October 2026. Under the 12-month pro forma rule, T1-T3 are therefore based on the company's own figures for the continuing operations. stockanalysis' TTM figures (net loss −kr 1.15bn, P/B 2.43, P/TBV 6.4, FCF) blend in the divested operations, and the history and PEG from the period have not been used as a basis for findings. Pro forma net debt (about kr 240m incl. leasing) is an estimate: net debt of kr 782m at 30/9 minus the announced proceeds of about kr 545m in Q4. stockanalysis has no forecast page (404) and forward P/E 17.9 has no known estimate basis, so analyst coverage is thin or missing. JLR's exact share of revenue is not disclosed; the concentration is assessed from UK sales and the effect of the cyberattack. No guidance. Not investigated: rating and any insider trades after March 2026.

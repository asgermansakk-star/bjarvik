    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a solid Danish bank with plenty of capital, but earnings have fallen and the shares are no longer cheap. Pre-tax profit fell from 628m to 537m kronor between 2023 and 2025, and the price rose 91% in 2025 while profit per share fell. The shares trade very rarely. In its favour, capital is far above requirements and losses are very small.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    • Quality of earnings
      Profit before tax has fallen from kr 628m in 2023 to 619 in 2024 and 537 in 2025, and in Q1–Q3 2026 it fell again to 421.8 vs 444.1 (EPS kr 93.5 vs 96.8; Q3 alone kr 111m vs 130m after tax). Net interest and fee income rose 6.1%, but staff and administration costs rose 8.1%, and the improvement in core earnings is helped by reversed impairments.

    • Efficiency and ROE
      The bank spends 65 øre on costs per krone earned (C/I 65.4% in Q1–Q3 2026, 67.1% in 2025 vs 56% in 2023) – Ringkjøbing Landbobank is at 26% and Djurslands Bank at approx. 53%. The number of employees has risen from 459 in 2021 to 621, and return on equity after tax has fallen from 17.9% (2023) and 15.1% (2024) to 11.6% – close to the bank's own minimum target of 10%.

      The dividend has been held at kr 20 per share (payout ratio about 17% of EPS of 118.8), and the authorisation to buy back up to 10% from March 2026 has not been used (share count −0.09% over a year). The capital ratio is 23.3% vs a requirement of 14.8%, a surplus of kr 1,367m – surplus capital that drags down return on equity.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −11 of −35
    • Governance and ownership structure
      Trade unions and their pension companies own more than half (LB Forsikring 14.8%, PKA 11.1%, Politiforbundet 10.7%, Danmarks Lærerforening 10.2%, Interesseforeningen 5.8%). No one may vote for more than 15%, acquiring more than 15% requires the bank's consent, the bank does not follow the recommendations on takeover bids, and the board of representatives elects the board, whose chair is chair of Danmarks Lærerforening. The owners are also customers via member benefits such as 5% interest on the salary account, so minority shareholders in reality have no influence. Insiders own 0.01%.
      lsb.dk
      −4
    • Liquidity in the stock
      On average only about 99 shares are traded a day (about kr 0.13m) at a market cap of kr 4.7bn, and no analysts cover the stock. Even small orders can move the price, and it can be hard to exit.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Interest-rate sensitivity and market risk
      Deposits are almost twice as large as loans (loans/deposits 55.7%), and DKK 12.1bn sits at Danmarks Nationalbank, so earnings follow the interest rate level closely, while the bank pays 5% on the first DKK 50,000 in members' salary accounts. The bond portfolio has almost doubled to DKK 10.0bn since the turn of the year, interest rate risk rose from 1.9% to 3.1%, and the portfolio result was DKK −13.1m in Q1–Q3 (−24.6m in Q3 alone) vs +54.1m the year before.
      lsb.dk
      −2

      The Swedish branch has DKK 4.6bn in exposure, mainly unsecured personal loans (about 22% of lending), after several years of rising losses. Arrears are 0.5% vs 0.2% in Denmark, the bank describes tougher competition in the Swedish consumer loan market, and Swedish income fell from DKK 207m to 200m in 2025.

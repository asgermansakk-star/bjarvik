    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees an investment company with low debt but weak returns. The share has returned about 4% a year over five years against 9% for the Swedish market, and over half the value sits in three companies. On the plus side, dividends received are rising and the CEO bought SEK 55m of shares. The share trades about 9% below the value of its holdings. Watch the succession plan.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Investment company: valued on price to net asset value (NAV) instead of P/E. The group's P/E of 8 and ROE are distorted by Holmen and Hufvudstaden, which are consolidated, and by fair-value changes in associates (Industrivärden, Indutrade); they have not been used. The latest official NAV is kr 661 at 25 August 2026; the Q3 report is due 24 November, so the discount of approx. 9% is an estimate. Stockanalysis has no analyst estimates (the forecast page does not exist), so PEG and price targets are missing. The buybacks in the cash flow (2.75bn) are Holmen's, not Lundberg's; the share count is unchanged at 248m. The option exercises in February and March 2026 are Fredrik Lundberg's own-issued employee options and have not been counted as insider trading. Price targets and analyst coverage have not been examined.

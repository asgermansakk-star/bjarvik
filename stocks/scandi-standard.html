    In plain words

    The score is middling: the chicken producer has become markedly more expensive, which pulls the score down most. The share price has doubled in a year and is pricey compared with both its own history and competitors. The company still earns less than it targets, and the Glenhaven acquisition is paid for with new shares and loans. In its favour, the deputy chairman has bought shares.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −9 of −30
    • Margin and ROIC
      Adjusted EBIT margin of 5.0% for the first nine months of the year (5.4% in Q3) is still below the company's target of over 6%, and adjusted ROCE of 13.1% is below the target of 15%. EBIT per kg is kr 2.72 vs the target of over kr 3. ROIC is 9.3% (TTM). The direction is good (operating margin 2.2% in 2021), but the level is thin for a chicken producer.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Moat
      Chicken is a commodity-linked protein category. The company has strong Nordic brands (Kronfågel, Danpo, Den Stolte Hane) and an integrated value chain, but sells to large grocery chains with bargaining power, and management itself writes that earnings are periodically affected by raw material prices.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Processed volume grew only 3% in Q3 and 4% in the first nine months of the year. The rest of the sales growth of 7–8% comes from prices and mix, incl. price increases in Ready-to-eat. Growth next year (+16% according to consensus) comes mainly from acquisitions.

    • Acquisitions, dilution and debt
      Glenhaven Foods is bought for EUR 127m (about 8.2x normalised EBIT). The acquisition is financed with a rights issue of kr 408m (3.27m new shares at kr 125, 4.9% dilution), bank debt and an interest-free vendor loan of about a third of the price, due within a year. Net debt was kr 2.06bn (1.7x) before closing. Pro forma it is estimated at about 3.4bn and about 2.4x adjusted EBITDA, close to the target of under 2.5x. At the same time the company enters the British retail market, where it has hardly been present before.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Glenhaven Foods was consolidated 9/10-2026, so the TTM figures and the stockanalysis history exclude the acquisition. The pro forma leverage is my own estimate based on EV of EUR 127m and 8.2x EBIT, not a figure from the company. The price targets on stockanalysis are from May 2026 (price 128–147), and only 3–4 analysts cover the stock. Customer concentration (Nordic grocery chains) and the risk of avian flu have not been investigated and score 0. There is no bid for the company; the large purchases by the Gaspar family (Lusiaves) are an increase in holding, not an offer.

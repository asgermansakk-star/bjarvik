    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −12 of −30
    • ROIC and returns
      ROIC is 4.5% vs WACC of 6.1% and has been 3.0-4.3% since 2021 with no improvement. Return on equity was -17.6% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024, 5.0% in 2025 and -5.5% in 9M 2026, far below the company's own target of at least 10% over a cycle.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Rental income rose only 2.0% in Q3, while the CPI adjustment was 2.9%; the comparable growth beyond CPI was −SEK 4m in the quarter. Property management profit fell to SEK 306m (328), and Cash Earnings per share fell to SEK 5.21 in 9M (5.42) because of higher interest expenses. Rental income has meanwhile shrunk from SEK 3,418m in 2023 to SEK 3,098m in 2025 after disposals to reduce debt.

      Entra is a pure office company. Central Oslo properties have 94.9% occupancy, but Sandvika (86.6%) and Drammen (90.5%) drag it down, and the occupancy rate has fallen from 95.3% in 2023 to 93.8%. The company itself writes that vacancy is higher in secondary areas with more competition, and that large tenants take longer to make decisions.

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −13 of −35
    • Debt and interest
      Loan-to-value is 49.0% (EPRA LTV 53.7%) and net debt/EBITDA 11.0x. Interest cover is 2.14x over 12 months, but only 1.96x in Q3 in isolation (target at least 1.8x). The average interest rate rose to 4.37% from 3.91% a year ago, only 63% of the debt is interest-hedged, and 44.8% of the interest hedges expire within 12 months, close to the limit of 50%. The Moody's rating Baa3 is the lowest investment grade.

    • Cash flow and payouts
      Operating cash flow was kr 1,240m over 12 months, and after investments in properties of kr 956m only kr 284m is left. The dividend of kr 2.20 (approx. kr 400m), the buyback of kr 100m and the new programme of up to kr 200m exceed free cash flow (FCF payout ratio 140%).
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Governance and major shareholders
      Balder owns 40.17% and Castellum 37.13%, and both have negative control. The ten largest owners hold 86%, insiders only 0.11%, and turnover is low (approx. 119,000 shares per day). Since the general meeting in April 2026 each of the two competing Swedish property groups has two board members, and Balder's CEO Erik Selin is chairman, which creates a risk of conflicts of interest towards minority shareholders.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −10 of −30
    • ROIC and returns
      ROIC is 2.2% vs WACC of 4.2%, and ROE is 3.1%. Net operating income in the intjäningsförmågan (kr 779m) gives only 4.1% on the property value of kr 19.0bn, vs an average interest rate of 3.3%. The spread between return and borrowing rate is thus very narrow.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation and growth per share
      Brinova bought K-Fast Holding's properties for SEK 10.8bn and paid with new shares at SEK 32.46, so the share count rose about 40%. Rental income rose 22% in 9M, but income from property management per share fell 11% to SEK 0.93 (1.04). The company's own target is at least +10% a year.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Margin
      The surplus ratio fell to 64.6% in 9M (68.5) and 69.1% in Q3 (70.8). It is 67% in the earning capacity, below the company's own target of 70%. The new CEO promises the effect of the savings only in 2027.
      mb.cision.com
      −2
    • Future segment
      Hyresbostäder are 65% of contracted rent, and the rest is samhällsfastigheter and commercial in Skåne and Småland. The occupancy rate is only 90% on commercial space, and there is structural vacancy in Växjö. About 13% of the buildings are in the lowest energy classes F-G.
      mb.cision.com

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −18 of −35
    • Debt and refinancing
      Net LTV is 57.4% vs a ceiling of 60%, and the debt ratio is 14.8x vs a target of at most 14. Interest cover is 1.8x vs a floor of 1.75x. Debt maturity has fallen to 1.5 years (2.0), and 30% of debt is short term. kr 2.6bn falls due within a year and kr 6.6bn in 1–2 years, so 83% of the bank debt of kr 11.0bn must be refinanced within two years. The debt runs at a variable Stibor rate, and only 54.9% is hedged.
      mb.cision.com
      −9
    • Large owner and governance
      K-Fast Holding owns 57.6% of capital and votes and was both seller and payment recipient in the deal of kr 10.8bn. In 2026 there have been related-party transactions with K-Fast of kr 65.5m, mainly projects. K-Fast wants to sell properties to reduce its own debt and then distribute the Brinova shares to its shareholders. That may create considerable selling pressure.
      inderes.se
      −4
    • Ownership concentration and liquidity
      K-Fast, Backahill and Balder together own about 76% of the capital, and the company has only 2,994 shareholders (3,069). On average about 31,000 shares trade per day, about SEK 0.36m. That is very thin for a company with property worth SEK 19bn.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Interest rate and value sensitivity
      The required yield averages 4.8% (residential 4.5%). If it rises 0.25 percentage points, property value falls by kr 901m, equal to about 34% of market value. The loan-to-value ratio would then rise to about 60%, i.e. the company's own ceiling.
      mb.cision.com
      −2
    • Cash flow and dividend
      Operating cash flow fell 5% to DKK 209m (TTM). No dividend was paid for 2025 because the company wanted to prioritise liquidity. Acquisitions and projects (DKK 367m) were financed by property sales (DKK 312m, incl. Ballerup).

The figures are from the Q3 report at 30/9-2026 and a price of SEK 11.60 (6/11-2026). Stockanalysis only has history for 2024-2025, because the K-Fast deal in April 2025 changed the company. Peer figures (Sveafastigheter, Heba, John Mattson, Stenhus) are from the same day's analyses. Not investigated (gives 0): loan terms (covenants) at the nine banks, K-Fast Holding's own debt and whether the Brinova shares are pledged, the timetable for K-Fast's sakutdelning and the listing of the A share, and sale prices vs book value beyond the sale in Ballerup. The CEO was replaced 15/6-2026 (retirement), which has not been deducted. Stockanalysis has no forecast page (404), so analyst coverage is probably very thin or absent.

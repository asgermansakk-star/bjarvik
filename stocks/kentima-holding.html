    In plain words

    The score is 59 out of 100: a micro-cap where operating profit fell sharply in the first half (margin 1.5% versus 8.6%), and the shares are expensive relative to recent earnings. One business area, which in practice serves a single large customer, makes up about 44% of sales, and cash is nearly empty. New orders rose 33%, and the CEO owns 27.9%. Watch the report due 12-13 November.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Micro company (market cap about SEK 70m, 32 employees) with no analyst coverage and no forward estimate, so PEG and upside cannot be measured. The Q3 report comes on 12–13 November 2026 and is not included; the latest figures are H1 2026. Stockanalysis' insider share of 0.39% is wrong: CEO Kent Nilsson owns 27.9% of the capital according to the company's owner list. Earnings are effectively not taxed because of tax loss carry-forwards, and TTM contains a gain of SEK 0.9m; the multiples have been adjusted for the gain. Peers (e.g. Beijer Electronics) have not been compared because the figures could not be retrieved; T2 rests on own history. Not flagged as cyclical, even though customised control systems for Sandvik's crushers (mining and construction) accounted for about 44% of sales in Q2; the rest is automation and security. The stock is very illiquid (about 4,000 shares a day).

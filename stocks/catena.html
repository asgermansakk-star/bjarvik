    In plain words

    The score is 71 out of 100 for a Swedish company renting out warehouses and logistics property. Debt is high and rising, vacant space has grown from 3.4% to 4.7%, and the share count has been raised repeatedly, latest about 9% in January. The ten largest tenants provide 51% of income. In its favour, the share trades at about 0.84 times its property value, and leases are long and inflation-linked.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.

Running earnings are measured as förvaltningsresultat per share (SEK 21.46 in 9M) and earnings capacity per share (SEK 30.72 after the purchase of ten Finnish properties 1/10). stockanalysis's trailing P/E 11.4 is lifted by value adjustments (SEK 622m) and derivatives (SEK 221m), and their financial figures only go to 30/6-2026. EV/EBITDA 22 on a TTM basis is distorted because the acquisition of SEK 8.8bn in Q2 is only partly included. Historical prices at quarter-end have been read from the report's quarterly overview, where the order in the PDF extract for Q2 and Q3 2026 has been interpreted from the 52-week low of SEK 347.80. The share count (+9.09% at the corrected issue in January 2026) has been checked against the cash flow statement (new issue SEK 2,731m). FCF after investments in existing properties is only approx. SEK 430m in 9M vs a dividend of SEK 315m, so the dividend gives no plus. The credit assessment of the largest tenants (DSV, DHL, ICA) and the properties' sensitivity to the yield requirement have not been reviewed in detail. The management changes in March 2026 have not been investigated. Only 8 analysts cover the stock, and several price targets are from before the Q3 report.

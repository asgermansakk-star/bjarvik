    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −9 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Organic growth is 10% in Q3 and 9M 2026, and ARR grew only 9% currency-adjusted (11% reported) vs 18–22% revenue growth in 2021–2024. The target is over 18% ARR growth in the medium term, so the company is growing at about half of its own target.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Revenue mix and gross margin
      Expert Services (consulting services) make up 30% of revenue in 9M 2026, and gross margin is only 50.6% TTM, low for software. That part of the business scales with staff and utilisation, not with licences.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Future segment and moat
      CRM for small and medium-sized businesses competes with Salesforce, HubSpot and Microsoft, and the CEO himself writes that AI will make the differences in functionality between the solutions shrink. The moat must therefore come from service, verticals and European data handling, while the AI products (AI Assist, Lime Go Agentic, Voice Agent) are still at an early stage.
      via.tt.se
      −3

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 3 findings    −6 of −35
    • Customer behavior and macro
      Management describes unchanged cautious customer behaviour with postponed decisions and longer sales cycles in Q3 2026. AI maturity among Lime CRM's customers is described as generally low, so AI revenue comes gradually.
      via.tt.se
      −2
    • Execution against financial targets
      The CEO maintains the target of over 18% ARR growth, but the route there rests on the smaller business units (Lime Go, Lime Connect, Sportadmin) running 'significantly faster', on new AI revenue and on acquisitions. Lime Connect got a new head in Q3. The target is nowhere near being reached organically.
      via.tt.se
      −3
    • Acquisition dependence
      Acquisitions are one of three levers for the growth target, and dialogues are under way with several companies. Most recently 51% of Supercompany (Trale, approx. NOK 7.5m ARR) was bought with the founders staying in management, and in January a portal solution from E.ON One. This increases integration risk and makes organic growth harder to follow.

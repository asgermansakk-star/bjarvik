    In plain words

    The score of 67 describes a forestry and paper group where earnings have fallen sharply and the price is middling. The profit margin has dropped from 26.5% in 2022 to 7.9%, and 39% of operating profit is a non-cash write-up of the forests. On the other hand, the forests are booked above the company's total value, and the share is 16% below equity per share. Watch rising debt.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    B share in Stockholm. Figures through Q3 2026 (report 22/10-2026). The forestry and paper industry has no industry of its own on the list; like UPM, Stora Enso and Metsä Board it is set to 'Commodities & uranium' and marked cyclical (pulp, board, paper, wood products and electricity follow price cycles). Holmen's operating profit includes the non-cash value change on biological assets (SEK 796m in Jan–Sep); findings on margin and earnings quality are therefore stated both with and without. Equity (SEK 365 per share) is in practice an intrinsic value because the forests are carried at market value after deferred tax; it is used as the NAV measure instead of a separate NAV calculation. FCF TTM (2,340m) is lifted by an inventory reduction of 784m. The price targets on stockanalysis are from August 2026 and from only 8 analysts (consensus Hold). Insider trades have not been examined beyond the ownership structure and score 0. The split of the Forest business area is an internal reorganisation, not a spin-off.

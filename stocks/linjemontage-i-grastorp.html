    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a recently listed Swedish power-line contractor with net cash, but falling sales and a high price. Sales fell 10.8% in the first half. The share is expensive against comparable companies, and an Indian parent owns 66.5% and wrote down a loan to a sister company. In its favour is an order book of 1.4 times sales. Watch for the parent possibly selling more shares after September 2027.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    • Governance and main shareholder
      India's Kalpataru Projects International owns approx. 66.5% after the listing. In 2025/26 a loan of kr 53.7m to another Kalpataru company was written down in full, and kr 58m was bought from the parent. This is a loss for the minority shareholders to the benefit of the group.

    • Customer concentration
      Revenue is 96% Swedish, and the order book is dominated by a few grid owners: Svenska kraftnät (orders of approx. kr 779m, 400m and 191m in 2026) and Ellevio (approx. kr 350m and 200m). If they change their investment pace, it hits directly.

      Work is mainly on fixed-price agreements, and the company has issued guarantees of kr 507m. Record projects such as the Svenska kraftnät station at kr 779m (about 25% of annual revenue) increase the risk that a single project overruns its budget.

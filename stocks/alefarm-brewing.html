    In plain words

    The model sees a small microbrewery that has lost money every year since 2015 but showed its first profit before tax in the first half of 2026. The shares are expensive: the price relative to sales and earnings is far above larger brewers such as Royal Unibrew. On the plus side is a raised earnings outlook. Watch the thin trading, family control and cash of only 0.63m kr.

    The model found several serious problems. That does not mean the stock will fall.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −17 of −30
    • Margin
      The company has made a loss every year since its founding in 2015: EBIT margin −58% (2022), −38%, −29%, −10% (2025) and −2.1% TTM, and net margin −4.7% TTM. H1 2026 is the first positive result before tax in its history, but only kr 0.11m (1.7% of revenue). Even on the raised guidance (EBITDA kr 1.25–1.75m), the result after depreciation of approx. kr 1.1m and interest is close to zero.
      stockanalysis.com
      −6
    • Scale and moat
      A microbrewery with DKK 12.2m in revenue, 7 employees and approx. 275,000 litres of capacity (before the new tanks) in a market for craft beer that the company itself describes as stagnating ('a market that, incidentally, is not growing'). There is no documented pricing power: growth comes especially from draught beer for bars, which is sold at a lower price per litre than cans, and the new capacity is partly to be filled with contract brewing for other breweries.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −5
    • Capital allocation
      A digital platform/app that the founder Kasper Tidemann developed alone over 3½ years was capitalised at DKK 1.8m and abandoned in June 2025. The unfinished app was sold back to his own company Tidemann&Co ApS at book value against 1.11m Alefarm shares at DKK 1.62 each. Equity has fallen from DKK 22.4m (2021) to DKK 7.4m after years of losses.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −4
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue grew only 12.8% in H1 2026 vs the 13–21% that full-year guidance (DKK 13–14m) requires. Sales to bars and restaurants (+124%) are carried by one large agreement with a Copenhagen restaurant chain and by Alefarm Taproom, which the company itself owns 20% of. Sales in retail and the digital channels were squeezed by a lack of can capacity.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −2

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −15 of −35
    • Cash flow and capital need
      Cash fell from DKK 1.34m to 0.63m in H1 2026 (about 5% of annual revenue). Operations gave only DKK 0.19m, because receivables rose DKK 1.39m to 2.1m, and DKK 0.39m went to investments and DKK 0.5m to a start-up loan and equity stake in the bar at Nørrebrogade 1. With tank investments and more staff in H2, the buffer is very thin, and that increases the risk of a new share issue (the latest was in November 2024).

    • Governance
      Chairman Jens Erik Thorndahl and CEO Kresten Thorndahl together own Thorndahls ApS; the board has only three members, and a Thorndahl was also chairman of the meeting at the general meeting. The family thus has close control over both board and management in a company that has traded with related parties (the app with the founder and 20% of a bar that is to take 5–7% of production).

      Only about 8,300 shares are traded a day (about kr 24,000), no analysts cover the stock, and the 52-week range is kr 1.68–5.05. Positions can only be traded at a large price risk.

The announcements are mutually unclear: the supplier is called Dutch (23/6) and later British (22/7), and the tanks are referred to as both 6,000- and 5,000-litre.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Micro-cap company: market cap about DKK 38m, 7 employees. No analysts, no forward estimate, no PEG and no price target on stockanalysis, so P/E and EV/EBITDA are calculated on the company's own EBITDA guidance (DKK 1.25–1.75m). The half-year report is neither audited nor reviewed. stockanalysis shows −1.8% in share count; the decline is because the platform/app was exchanged for own shares (1.11m, 7.86%), and not a buyback paid from FCF, so it gives no plus. Insider ownership in percent is not disclosed on stockanalysis and has not been found in other sources (only that Thorndahls ApS, owned by the chairman and CEO, bought 90,000 shares in 2025). Outstanding warrants from October 2024 and any loss of a prepayment to the bankrupt tank supplier have not been examined. Customer concentration has not been quantified. The web search budget was used up, so the research is based on stockanalysis, the company's reports and Nasdaq's announcement archive; Danish media and debate about the company have not been reviewed.

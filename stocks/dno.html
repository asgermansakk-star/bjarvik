    In plain words

    DNO scores 68 out of 100: an oil and gas company with big earnings now, but high risk. The main drags are profit resting on very high energy prices, Kurdistan production shut down by attacks, and a tax bill that has grown to $938m. In its favour, net debt fell from $886m to $232m.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    DNO reports in USD while the stock trades in NOK; stockanalysis mixes the currencies (e.g. EPS NOK 1.55 TTM vs USD 0.16 in the financial statements table). Sval Energi is included from 1/6-2025, so the 2025 figures and the ROIC history are not entirely comparable, and P/TBV is useless because goodwill from Sval makes tangible equity negative (hence P/B). FCF TTM (USD 907m) and P/FCF 2.25 are flattered by a tax liability that has grown from USD 320m to 938m. Net debt of USD 232m includes USD 400m held in a restricted account for the Capricorn bid, and excludes the hybrid bond of USD 400m. DNO is the bidder for Capricorn Energy, not the target, so there is no special situation; the bid (USD 5.214 per share, final) has been overtaken by Genel's bid, and the shareholder meetings are postponed indefinitely. Only 4 analysts cover the stock and only 2 have EPS estimates, so the estimate for 2027 (revenue −13%) is thin. Insider trades have only been checked superficially (reportable trades in 2026 relate to the cash-settled synthetic employee programme), and customer concentration has not been examined separately (oil and gas are sold on offtake agreements, including with ExxonMobil for about half of the North Sea oil). Q3 report 29/10-2026.

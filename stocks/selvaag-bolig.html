    In plain words

    The score of 67 describes a homebuilder with record sales but falling profit and rising debt. The profit on homes has almost halved since 2021, and net debt of 3.83bn crowns equals about 130% of the stock market value. On the other hand, sales this year have already beaten the previous annual record from 2016, and 64% of homes under construction are sold. Watch unsold finished homes, up to 93.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Residential developer: IFRS recognises revenue on handover, so the TTM figures (revenue +185%, EPS 2.78) are lifted by a record number of handovers in 2026 and swing sharply from year to year; interest on construction loans is capitalised in inventory, so EV/EBITDA (22) and interest cover (49.7) from stockanalysis are not meaningful. Stockanalysis shows −4.1% in share count, but the cash flow shows only buybacks of kr 28m vs issuance of kr 17m for employee programmes, and the share count is largely unchanged (93.8m issued), so no points were given for buybacks. Insider ownership (3.7%) does not include the main shareholder Selvaag AS (53.5%). Only 2 analysts cover the stock, and forward P/E is based on their estimates. The fair value of the land bank (NAV) has not been examined, and no external NAV estimate has been found. Customer concentration is not relevant (private customers).

    In plain words

    The score is 79 out of 100: Bjarvik sees a specialist aircraft supplier whose profit has fallen sharply from earlier peaks, while the share is still expensive. The profit margin has dropped from about 39% to about 22%, and free cash does not cover the dividend. The company depends on Airbus, Boeing and the dollar rate. The order book has grown and the outlook is raised.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    • Margin and ROIC

Private jet is having its weakest year in several years, and retrofit is standing still.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −13 of −35
    • Cash flow and dividend
      Operating cash flow fell to DKK 29.8m TTM (Q3: 19.5 vs 33.1) due to rising receivables; after investments in tangible and intangible assets (incl. expansion in Nybro) FCF is approx. 14m vs net profit of 39m. The dividend of approx. 30m is not covered, and the net cash of 76m (2023) has turned into net debt of 20m.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Customer concentration
      Dependent on Airbus and Boeing (A350, 787) and on distributors in the aftermarket: sales to Denmark (the distribution channel) fell from DKK 181m in 2023 to 52m in 2025 when the distributor reduced inventories – one channel can move half of earnings.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Execution and deliveries
      OEM deliveries of approx. kr 6m for the 787 pushed from Q3 to Q4 due to Boeing's supply chain, OEM guidance narrowed to 45–50% (from 45–60%), VIP projects delayed and no retrofit deliveries in Q3–Q4.
      mfn.se
      −3

      The company describes itself as 'extremely dependent' on USD/SEK: almost all revenue is in USD, while costs are mainly in SEK. A weaker dollar has already squeezed 2025, and Q3 got kr 2m in currency tailwind.

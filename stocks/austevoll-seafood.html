      Salmon, whitefish, fishmeal and fish oil are commodities where the market sets the price, and quotas are set by the authorities. The spread across salmon and pelagic gives some natural hedging (higher fishmeal prices lift Pelagia and FoodCorp, but raise Lerøy's feed costs), but the group is still a price taker.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 9 findings    −25 of −35
    • Salmon price, currency and feed
      The salmon price (SSI) was flat at NOK 71.7/kg in Q2, and the strong NOK weighed on revenue. More expensive marine feed raw materials (El Niño) feed through to Lerøy's cost price from Q4 2026 and especially in 2027.
      auss.no
      −3
    • El Niño in Peru
      The first anchovy season in Peru has been suspended since the end of April; Austral caught only 20% of its quota, sold 10,500 t of fishmeal vs 38,700 t and had negative EBITDA. The company writes that the uncertainty about the second season is greater than usual.
      auss.no
      −3
    • Quotas
      The mackerel quota has been cut 48%, the blue whiting quota 41% and the North Sea herring quota markedly in 2026, which gives low capacity utilisation in Pelagia's FOOD factories. In Chile a new quota law has moved the industrial fleet's share from 90% to 70%, so FoodCorp's own jack mackerel quota fell to 70,400 t from 80,900 t.
      auss.no
      −3
    • Resource rent tax and tax dispute
      Norwegian aquaculture pays 25% resource rent tax on top of 22% corporate tax (47% in total). The group has amended earlier tax returns for three of five taxable companies to deduct the opening biomass, but the Norwegian Tax Administration takes the view that the deduction will not be approved.
      auss.no
      −4
    • Competition law (EU cartel case)
      The subsidiary Lerøy is among the Norwegian farmers whom the European Commission in January 2024 accused of exchanging sensitive spot price data 2011–2019. A fine of up to 10% of revenue is possible, and British supermarket chains and a class action demand compensation.
      salmonbusiness.com
      −3
    • The traffic light system
      On 19 June 2026 the government proposed a further 6% lower MTB in production area 3; for the group this corresponds to 1,724 t (1,560 t in Lerøy and 164 t in KFO), without compensation. KFO is already running at 4,700 t vs originally 5,460 t.
      auss.no
      −2
    • Debt
      The group's net debt incl. leases is NOK 10.6bn (net debt/EBITDA 2.9x vs 1.1x in 2021), and interest cover is only 2.7x; the debt sits mainly in Lerøy (8.4bn) and Pelagia (5.7bn for 100%). Altman Z-score 1.93.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Guidance vs estimates
      Analysts expect EPS of kr 7.37 in 2026 vs kr 4.7 in 2025, but adjusted EPS in H1 was kr 4.7 (5.2 the year before), and Lerøy expects operational EBIT in H2 in line with H2 2025. The estimate requires the pelagic price rise to lift H2 sharply; only 5 analysts give an EPS estimate.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Governance
      The Møgster family's LACO AS owns 55.6%, and CEO Arne Møgster is from the family; with Lerøy as a listed subsidiary there are two ownership layers, and minority shareholders have limited influence. Board member Hege Solbakken stepped down on 10 September 2026.
      auss.no
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Holding company: about 80% of the transparent value is the 52.7% of Lerøy Seafood Group, which is fully consolidated. Revenue, EBIT, debt and FCF therefore include the minorities' share. stockanalysis' P/B of 0.64 is calculated on equity incl. minorities (26.0bn); P/B on AUSS shareholders' equity (15.0bn, NOK 74.15 per share) is about 1.1. Earnings follow the salmon price, fishmeal and fish oil prices and quotas (cyclical). The Q3 report comes 10/11-2026 and is not included. The NAV discount is a rough estimate from Lerøy's market price and book values for the unlisted parts (Pelagia, Austral, FoodCorp, KFO); the history of the discount has not been examined. stockanalysis shows +2.27% in share count, but there are 202.7m shares and only about 0.9m treasury shares, unchanged, so that gives no points. stockanalysis' FCF (3.6bn) is before interest (0.9bn) and leasing. Insider trades in 2026 have not been examined. The EU cartel case and the British damages cases against Lerøy are unresolved. Only 8 analysts and 5 EPS estimates.

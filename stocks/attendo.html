    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −9 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue is standing still: kr 18.97bn TTM vs 18.98bn in 2024, organic growth only 1.3% in Q3 and 1.0% in the first nine months of the year, and the number of places fell from 21,405 to 21,252. EPS growth (+23% adjusted in Q3) comes from margin, contract exits and buybacks, not from higher sales.

    • Margin and ROIC
      Lease-adjusted EBITA margin 8.4% in the nine months of the year (11.3% in Q3) and ROIC 7.6% vs a WACC of about 6.5%: clearly better than 2–5% in 2021–2024, but still a low-margin, labour-intensive business area where prices are set by public payers.

      −3
    • Moat
      Demand for elderly care is growing structurally, but Attendo has poor pricing power: contracts in tenders run 2–5 years, and in Scandinavia the company has had to leave outsourcing and home-care contracts without long-term sustainable terms.

    • Politics and public payers
      Almost all revenue comes from municipalities and Finnish wellbeing services counties. Welfare stocks fell in June 2026 when the red-green bloc stood strong in the polls before the Riksdag election, and the debate on profit in welfare could hit conditions in Sweden (approx. 40% of sales); the Finnish wellbeing services counties are at the same time under budget pressure.

    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a lowly priced share in a business that is shrinking and earns little (score 78 out of 100). Pulling down most: falling sales excluding acquisitions (-3.8% over nine months), low profitability (7.0% against a 10% target) and debt above the company's own target. Pulling up: a record order book, share purchases by management and the board, and a dividend covered twice by cash flow.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Engineering and advisory consultant; 'Construction & materials' was chosen as the closest industry, in line with Sweco, even though AFRY has a larger share in energy and industry. P/TBV is meaningless because goodwill (14.7bn) is larger than equity (13.2bn). stockanalysis' net debt (6.18bn) includes lease debt; the company's own net debt excluding IFRS 16 is 4.84bn (3.0x EBITDA). Customer concentration and contract terms (fixed price vs time and materials) have not been examined in depth; stockanalysis' insider share of 2.3% does not include the ÅForsk Foundation's control. The impact of AI on hour-based consulting work is an estimate. AFRY gives no short-term guidance, only targets for 2028. Only 6 analysts cover the stock.

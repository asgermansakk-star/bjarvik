    In plain words

    The score of 65 is middling: an offshore shipping company whose value mostly lies in stakes in other companies. Operating earnings are flat, and three customers account for 83% of revenue. There are close ties to major owner Aker, a shareholder lawsuit, and a 125m dollar ship purchase in 2027 to be financed with new debt. On the plus side: the dividend was raised and is covered by cash flow.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    • Governance and related parties
      Aker Capital owns 32.9% of Solstad Offshore and about 52% of Solstad Maritime, from which Solstad Offshore leases vessels (lease debt USD 223m) and is now buying Normand Maximus.       Kistefos (now 25.1% owner) and other shareholders sued the board, CEO, Aker Capital and Pareto in May 2024 for losses on the refinancing; the main hearing ran October 2025–January 2026 and demanded significant time from management. The company is not a party, but the conflict between the two largest shareholders persists.

      NIBD including leasing is USD 332m (Debt/EBITDA 3.1 according to stockanalysis). The purchase of Normand Maximus for USD 125m in October 2027 has to be financed with new external debt, which is not yet in place, and the term loan of USD 70m in the parent company falls due in November 2027.

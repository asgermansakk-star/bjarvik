    In plain words

    74 points out of 100. This year's earnings are lifted by the Middle East war, which raised prices, and a normalisation would remove much of that lift. The stock is pricier than in its own history, and the ARC Resources acquisition is mostly paid in new shares (about 4% dilution). Cash flow does cover dividend and buybacks, and analyst targets sit 11% above the price. Output in Qatar is hit.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −12 of −30
    • Future segment
      Shell has pulled back from much of the green transition and is betting on LNG and oil/gas. LNG is a strong niche, but the business is tied to fossil prices in a world where demand will flatten out over time.
      jamessharp.co.uk
      −4
    • ROIC and growth quality
      ROIC was 8.2–8.9% in 2023–25 and is only 11.4% TTM, because prices rose during the war in the Middle East. Production fell from 2,752 to 2,455 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day from Q1 to Q2 2026, so earnings growth comes from price and trading, not from volume.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Capital allocation (ARC Resources)
      The purchase of ARC Resources (EV USD 16.4bn, 20% premium) is paid 75% in shares, about 228m new Shell shares or about 4% dilution. That offsets a large part of a year of buybacks, and the buyback was paused in Q1 because of the deal.
      worldoil.com
      −3

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    2026 earnings are lifted by the war in the Middle East (higher realised prices and trading), and consensus expects EPS to double in 2026; this is cyclical peak earnings. BP: Shell rejected a bid in June 2025 (Rule 2.8, six-month lock-up expired in December 2025); in 2026 no new bid rumours have been found, and Shell bought ARC Resources instead.

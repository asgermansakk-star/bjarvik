    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −17 of −30
    • Margin
      Operating margin is only 3.7% and EBITDA margin 4.9% (TTM) vs 9.3–13.5% in 2022–2023; as an asset-light operator, Norden owns only approx. 5% of the just under 500 ships it operates, and earns a thin spread on freight.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Guidance for 2026 (USD 200–250m) includes USD 79m in ship sale gains, i.e. 32–40% of the year's result; the underlying improvement comes from tanker rates that are high because of the disruptions to oil flows after the conflict in the Persian Gulf.
      inderes.dk
      −7
    • Moat
      Dry bulk and product tanker shipping with no pricing power: rates are set by the market. The only advantage is scale, regional positioning and a flexible fleet with short charters.
      inderes.dk
      −3
    • ROIC and earnings through the cycle
      ROIC 7.7% (TTM) and 4.8% in 2025 vs 26% in 2023 and 48% in 2022; EPS fell from USD 21.10 in 2022 to USD 4.10 in 2025 (−81%). Earnings follow the freight cycle, not a structural improvement.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −17 of −35
    • Cash flow and debt
      FCF after leasing was −USD 406m (TTM) and −558m in 2025; even with ship sale proceeds (USD 334m) the cash flow is negative. Net debt incl. leasing has risen from USD 264m (2024) to 519m, while USD 113m has been paid out in dividends and buybacks; net debt/EBITDA 2.8.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Macro and geopolitics
      The tanker business (EBIT USD 34.5m in Q3, over two-thirds of the group's EBIT) lives off high rates after the disruptions in the Persian Gulf; management calls the geopolitical uncertainty high, and a normalisation will press rates.
      inderes.dk
      −6
    • Execution
      Dry bulk lost USD 45m at EBIT in Q1 2026 (chartering costs, Iran-related operating and insurance costs, and repositioning of vessels), and the logistics business had operating problems in Guinea; management warns that the timing of voyages may push earnings into 2027.
      inderes.dk
      −3
    • Chartering at the top
      Norden has in 2026 entered long-term charters of MR tankers and Capesize vessels and added Handysize and MPP vessels, while ship values have risen (5-year-old Capesize +25% in a year); lease obligations have risen to USD 558m.

      −2
    • Insider ownership
      Management and board own only 0.59%, and no insider purchases have been found; the major shareholder Motortramp (about 31%) is selling continuously pro rata in the buyback.

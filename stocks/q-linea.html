    In plain words

    The score is low because the company loses far more than it earns: SEK 15.6m in revenue against an operating loss of SEK 142.8m. Management promised 30-40 instruments sold by the end of 2025 but is now at 27. The board says the money lasts only 12 months. Costs are down 26%, and a US purchasing agreement could help sales.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

      Net revenue was 8.5m in January–September (8.2 the year before), i.e. almost flat. Only 8 instruments were contracted (13 the year before), and consumables sales per instrument (R12) fell to 247k (306). Recurring revenue is only 5.6m over 12 months.

      ASTar has the broadest panel and an FDA-approved extended menu, but the market for rapid antimicrobial susceptibility testing (rAST) is developing more slowly than the need.     Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Loss-making company: P/E, PEG, ROIC and FCF multiples are not meaningful, so the valuation is based on EV/Sales and P/B. The share history is distorted by the reverse split 1:1000 (July 2025) and the rights issue in November 2025 (share count +392%). Insider trades: management bought small stakes in June 2026 (incl. CEO approx. 15,000 shares), but several purchases were followed by sales the same day, which looks like a share programme and tax sales; therefore no plus has been given. No or almost no analyst coverage (no forecast page on stockanalysis). Revenue is small, and a single instrument sale moves the quarterly figures a lot. Competition within rapid AST (bioMérieux, Gradientech etc.) has not been examined in detail. Trading in the stock is thin.

    In plain words

    The score is 70 out of 100. The share is cheap relative to profit, but growth is mostly bought and has nearly stalled: October sales rose just 1%, and underlying growth is estimated near zero. Acquisitions are paid for with new shares, which dilute owners. On the plus side, there is 842m kr. in cash and only 62m kr. of debt. The 20% growth goal for 2026 is at risk.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    • Capital allocation and dilution
      The serial acquisition of other direct-sales companies' distributor databases (Zurvita, Valentus, Ecosystem, Bode Pro, Truvy, Sanki, It Works!) is paid almost entirely in new B shares: It Works! alone 1.84m shares (4.8% dilution), and there are earn-outs in new shares of up to USD 12m (Sanki) and USD 16m (Truvy). The share count is +4.5% in a year; Carlsquare judges that the acquisitions have contributed less than expected.

    • Moat
      Multi-level marketing of dietary supplements (omega-3 with testing) has low entry barriers and depends on a distributor network that can switch company. The margin lift to a 37.5% gross margin in Q2 is due partly to 'normalised distributor settlement', i.e. lower commission, which may squeeze recruitment.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Zinzino does not disclose organic growth; the organic development is our own estimate based on the monthly reports and the It Works!/Sanki contribution (about USD 45m and USD 12m a year), so the figure is uncertain. The cash of kr 842m includes customer prepayments (kr 210m at the end of 2025) and commissions owed to distributors; net cash is therefore effectively lower. No consensus on stockanalysis (the forecast page does not exist); the only coverage is paid analysis from Carlsquare. The Q3 report comes 17/11-2026 and is not included. FTC/authority cases in the US and churn in the distributor network have not been examined in detail.

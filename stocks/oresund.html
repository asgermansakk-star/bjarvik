    In plain words

    Öresund scores 64 out of 100: an investment company whose shares cost about 18% more than the value of its holdings. That is the highest premium among 19 Swedish peers, and the past ten years returned less than the market. On the plus side, it has no debt. Watch that 56% of its value sits in two shares.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Investment company: assessed on premium to net asset value (NAV). Stockanalysis' 'revenue', EPS and P/E (5.5) consist of unrealised value gains, and there is no forward estimate and no analyst coverage, so the PEG and upside formulas are meaningless for the company. The latest NAV is kr 130 per share at 31/10-2026 (after the dividend payment of kr 3.70 on 27/10); Scandi Standard has since risen from approx. kr 181 to 192, so the current NAV is estimated at approx. kr 132–133 and the premium at approx. 15–16% instead of 18% on the reported figure. NAV per share is calculated on 45.46m registered shares; 861,945 synthetically repurchased shares (swaps at an average price of kr 156) have not been examined further. Stockanalysis' insider ownership (3.85%) is wrong; the company's own list of owners (board 27.1%) has been used. Peers' premiums are from Ibindex via Börskollen 24/9-2026. The portfolio companies' own accounts have only been checked for Scandi Standard and Bilia (multiples).

      No product sales: revenue of SEK 5.8m in H1 2026 is a one-off milestone from Molteni. The operating loss was SEK 12.4m in H1 vs 2.6m a year earlier, and the company has never made money (operating result −SEK 25.7m TTM).

    • Moat
      BupiZenge is a new lozenge formulation of bupivacaine, an old generic local anaesthetic. Protection rests on formulation and use patents (new applications in 17 markets and for dentistry), not on a new molecule, and the comparator is cheap lidocaine.
      mfn.se
      −6
    • Capital allocation
      Everything is staked on one product in one indication (pain in oral mucositis). Financing has been piecemeal with expensive bridge loans, a guarantee commission of 14–17% and issues below the market price, and part of the issue (25%) goes to a MAA application before phase III has shown anything.

    • Funding and liquidity
      Cash was only SEK 0.75m at 30/6-2026 (current ratio 0.51). Twice in 2026 the company was left without liquidity for the coming month and had to borrow from Linc (1% per month, convertible at a 15% discount to VWAP) and John Haurum (1.5% per month plus a 5% fee). The SEK 7.5m loan from Linc is still outstanding, and running the phase III and MAA probably requires new capital before an approval.

    • Dilution
      The share count has risen from 11.7m (2021–2024) to 27.6m (+136%), of which more than a doubling in 2026 alone: Yangtian tranches, rights issue (dilution 40.8%), underwriting and set-off issues and a directed issue at SEK 3.00 with a 9.5% discount in October.
      mfn.se
      −6
    • Clinical and regulatory risk
      The entire value depends on the open (non-blinded) phase III study BEAM-Pain against lidocaine with 150 patients; only 12 had been recruited at the end of August, and the timing of data has not been stated. An open design with a pain endpoint increases the risk that the authorities will question the result.
      mfn.se
      −5

      The Chinese investor Sichuan Yangtian has withheld about SEK 10.1m of the last tranche after seven postponed payment deadlines; the company believes there is no legal basis for this, and the dispute is unresolved. Already the third tranche came late in January and triggered a liquidity crisis.

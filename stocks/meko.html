    In plain words

    The score of 56 shows a business with weak earnings at a low price. Most weighing it down: debt is high relative to earnings (above the company's target), returns on invested money are low, and a warehouse move caused delivery problems. In its favour, the chief executive bought 150,000 shares through a related party, and customers are spread across about 20,000 workshops. Watch the debt.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Q3 report does not come until 12 November 2026, so the analysis is based on H1 2026 (TTM to June). Only 2 analysts cover the stock, and the price targets are from May 2026. Forward P/E is based on adjusted EPS (SEK 7.42 for 2026) and depends on the one-off items (SEK −287m in 2025, −60m in H1 2026) actually ceasing. Marked non-cyclical: MEKO is an aftermarket spare parts wholesaler and not a subcontractor to car manufacturers. LKQ (26.6%) has announced a strategic review that could lead to a sale of the MEKO stake; it is not a bid. Customer concentration has not been examined beyond the number of workshop customers, and the peer comparison is only against Genuine Parts.

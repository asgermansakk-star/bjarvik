    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a Swedish property company with low debt, but a very low return on the money invested. The return is about 3% against capital costs of 7%, its NK department stores make a loss, and the shares are pricier relative to earnings than several rivals. In its favour, its debt relative to assets is the lowest among large Swedish rivals. Watch interest rates and empty offices.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

Interest-bearing net debt has risen from DKK 9.7bn in 2023 to DKK 11.0bn, and a new buyback of up to DKK 1bn must also be debt-financed.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Property company: P/E and ROE fluctuate with unrealised value adjustments. The earnings multiple is therefore calculated on profit before value adjustments and tax (rolling approx. SEK 1,259m, approx. SEK 6.5 per share, calculated from the Q3 report and the 2025 figures), and value is measured as price vs NRV/NTA. FCF from stockanalysis (SEK 1.34bn) does not include investments in properties; here FCF is measured after property investments. The share count fell 3.75% because 16.965m treasury A shares were cancelled in 2026. This fits the company's own share count (194.3m vs 211.3m issued). Lundbergs owns approx. 49% of the capital, so stockanalysis' insider share of 1.5% underestimates the ownership concentration. No documented insider purchases have been found in 2026. Sven-Olof Johansson's purchase for SEK 245m is not an insider purchase. Tenant concentration has not been investigated (0 points). Only 6–7 analysts cover the stock, and the price targets on stockanalysis are from May 2026.

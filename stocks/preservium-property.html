    In plain words

    Preservium scores 64 out of 100: a small property company with just two buildings and no employees. The old cheap loan was replaced by one at about 4% interest, so interest costs rise from about SEK 24m to SEK 37m. On the plus side, everything is let to two public-sector tenants with contracts to 2044 and 2032. Watch the ownership conflicts.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −19 of −35
    • Debt and interest rate sensitivity
      The bond loan of SEK 1,056.5m at 2.18% matured 20/10-2026 and has been replaced by a three-year loan of 930m at swap + 1.60% after an extraordinary repayment of 126.5m from cash. Loan-to-value is still approx. 66% (peers 45–55%), interest cover falls by our estimate from 2.7 × to approx. 1.6 ×, and repayments of 13.5m a year in the first two years eat most of the free cash flow. New refinancing in 2029.

    • Customer concentration
      All rental income comes from two tenants, Riksarkivet (agreement to 2044) and Regionarkivet in Region Stockholm (agreement to 2032). The properties are purpose-built for archives, so re-letting when Regionarkivet's lease expires will be difficult.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Property company with only two properties and no employees; valued on price vs net asset value (NRV) and förvaltningsresultat, not P/E, because the trailing P/E of 3.5 is driven by unrealised value increases. Stockanalysis shows last price 30/9-2026 (SEK 57), and the listing is shown under NGM, while the company itself states trading on Spotlight Stock Market. The new interest rate depends on the three-year swap rate at the payout in October 2026; the rate of approx. 4% and the lower förvaltningsresultat are our own estimate. There is no analyst coverage and no forward estimates. Insiders' ownership and trades (beyond SBB and PPI) have not been investigated. PPI's public purchase offer is running (acceptance period approx. 6/11–7/12-2026), so the price follows the offer; the board unanimously recommends rejecting it.

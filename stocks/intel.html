    In plain words

    Intel gets 53: the share is still expensive against its own history, even after falling about 28% in one day in October when the fourth-quarter outlook disappointed. The return on invested money is far below the financing cost, the factory business loses money, and the share count rose 15% in a year. The US government owns about 10% as a passive holder, paid for with $8.87bn of support funds, and can get 5% more if Intel owns less than 51% of the factory business, which makes a spin-off costly for shareholders. On the plus side, the CEO bought shares for about $9.7m after the fall. Watch whether the factory business wins more outside customers.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Re-evaluated 24 Nov 2026 (government equity stakes). Since 29 Sep: the Q3 results on 29 Oct beat guidance (revenue $16.5bn, +21%), but Q4 guidance ($15–16bn, gross margin about 37–38%) was about $1bn below consensus, and the stock fell about 28% on 30 Oct – from a peak of $142 to about $51–60 (about 35% below the 200-day average). The government stake is unchanged: the agreement of 22 Aug 2025 (433.3m shares at $20.47, about 9.9%, paid with $8.87bn of CHIPS funds; a 5-year warrant at $20 for a further 5% – up to 241m shares – which can only be exercised if Intel owns less than 51% of the foundry business; passive stake, votes with the board). 132m escrow shares had not yet been released on 26 Sep (66m conditional on Secure Enclave payments). The GAAP figures swing with the market value of the escrow shares (gain of $1.0bn in Q3, loss of $12.6bn in 9M; derivative liability of $13.7bn settled in shares) – so trailing P/E is n/a and PEG is not a clean growth measure. The government stake is treated as evidence: it provided funding in 2025 but does not change demand; the warrant effectively blocks a spin-off of foundry. The share issue in August ended at $23bn (242m shares at $95), and $14.2bn went to buying back Apollo's 49% of the plant in Ireland. The accident in Kiryat Gat (Israel) in October has not yet been quantified. The first external 18A customer is not named, and a class action over the PC slowdown has only been mentioned in the media. The share price rose 16% on 23 Nov with no company news found. Insider sales and customer concentration have not been investigated in the 10-Q.

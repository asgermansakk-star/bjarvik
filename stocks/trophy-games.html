    In plain words

    A score of 61 covers a small game publisher whose revenue is growing fast while profit is not keeping up. The share is expensive compared with other Nordic game companies, and activity in its games fell sharply in the third quarter. The company has cash and no interest-bearing debt, and management owns over half. New shares issued in June diluted existing owners.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −13 of −30
    • Margin
      EBIT margin 13% year to date and 15.6% TTM, EBITDA margin about 20% – mediocre for a mobile game publisher with no physical assets. Marketing (user acquisition) eats 38% of revenue (50.1m year to date), and after platform fees and direct costs the gross margin is only 35% (41% last year).
      files.quartr.com
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue is growing 56% year to date, but 2026 guidance for EBIT (18–24m) and EBITDA (29–35m) is in line with 2025 (EBIT approx. 20.5m, EBITDA 31.1m), even though revenue is expected to be up 35–42%. Growth is bought with a nearly doubled marketing budget and an acquisition and is not translating into higher earnings this year.
      files.quartr.com
      −5
    • Moat
      Niche games ('Manager' series in transport, agriculture and aviation) without strong IP; new titles have to be carried by paid user acquisition with approx. six months' payback. Low barriers to entry, and competition for ad spend on mobile is fierce.
      files.quartr.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The PC title The Ranchers has disappointed: a receivable from the studio was written down in Q3 (net financial items −SEK 2.3m). In H1 management had to cut marketing for Farm Manager because the unit economics could not bear the high level, and SEK 4–9m of development is still being capitalised per year.
      files.quartr.com
      −2

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 6 findings    −17 of −35
    • Customer behaviour and momentum
      In Q3 installs fell 29%, paying users 42% and transactions 35%, and management cites a general global slowdown in in-game purchases. Guidance (170–180m incl. about 7.5m from Playrion in Q4) implies organic Q4 revenue of about 29–39m vs 41.0m in Q4 2025 and Q4 EBIT of 1.3–7.3m vs about 10.5m.
      files.quartr.com
      −5
    • Title and genre concentration
      The Transport series is 82% of revenue in Q3, and the three largest titles accounted for 67% in 2025. The acquisition of Playrion (Airlines Manager, Airport Simulator) increases concentration in the same genre as Airline Manager, the company's oldest cash cow.
      files.quartr.com
      −3
    • Platform dependence
      Almost all distribution goes through the Apple App Store and Google Play with fees of up to 30% on in-game purchases, and growth depends on featurings and the price of user acquisition ads, which the company does not control. Server outages delayed the scaling of Truck Manager in H1.
      files.quartr.com
      −2
    • Liquidity in the stock
      Market cap DKK 374m on First North, average turnover about 15,000 shares a day (about DKK 0.2m) and a free float of about 17m shares; insiders and ATP own over 60%.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3

      Targeted issue in June 2026 of 1.806m new shares (approx. +6.5%) at kr 14.75 without pre-emption rights to ATP, BankInvest and Eiffel; the price is now kr 13.20. Only kr 16.8m of the proceeds of 25.8m has been used (Playrion).

Insider trades in 2026 have not been examined (the search budget was exhausted), and company announcements beyond the financial reports have not been read; the Investor Day presentation was only images.

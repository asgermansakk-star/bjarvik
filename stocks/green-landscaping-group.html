    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a business under pressure: the share looks cheap on profit, but debt is high. Margins are falling, growth was bought through acquisitions, and earnings per share have fallen three years running. Net debt is 2.8bn kroner, and the CEO left without a permanent successor. On the plus side, the 8.5bn kroner order book exceeds a year of sales, and several insiders have bought shares.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

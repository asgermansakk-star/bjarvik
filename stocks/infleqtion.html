    In plain words

    Infleqtion gets 37: the share costs over 20 times revenue, even though the company lost money on its sales in the third quarter and growth slowed sharply. One customer accounted for 81% of the quarter's revenue, one set of accounts has been corrected, and insiders have sold large blocks without buying. The US government only has a letter of intent to receive new shares worth $100m at a 15% discount; if it goes ahead, that is about 8% more shares at today's price, and the company does not need the money. On the plus side, it has $560m in cash and no debt. Watch whether the fourth quarter delivers the at least $12.5m the full-year target requires.

    The model found several serious problems. That does not mean the stock will fall.

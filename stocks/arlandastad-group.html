    In plain words

    A score of 66 is middling: the share is cheap against the booked value of its properties, but the core business loses money. Operations lost SEK 56m in the first half; profit comes from land marked up in value, and cash flow has been negative every year since 2021. Low debt and insider buying help. Watch the report due 13 November.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −18 of −30
    • Margin and ROIC
      Profit before value changes was −kr 56m in H1 2026 (−70), and the group thus does not make money on operations; the profit comes from unrealised value increases (kr 74m), sale of building rights (kr 41m) and JV shares (kr 18m). ROIC is 0.8% vs a WACC of 7.2% and has been between −0.8% and 0.4% since 2021.
      mb.cision.com
      −9
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue rose only 3% in H1 (174.9 vs kr 169.6m), and the underlying rental value in Arlandastad fell to kr 168.8m (173.7), partly because the test track has to be moved after the land sales. Net operating income rose, but part of it is due to a one-off rent loss the year before.
      mb.cision.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The board has made a dividend of 5% of equity (about kr 3.50 per share, about kr 225m a year) the company's only financial target, even though operating cash flow has been negative every year since 2021. The first dividend of kr 98m was paid from land sale proceeds, and the company has at the same time taken up an unsecured bond.
      mfn.se
      −4

      The Skavsta segment gave −SEK 31m before value changes in H1, because the airport operation has revenue of SEK 24m vs costs of SEK 54m. Development of the area is tied to Ostlänken and has a waiting time of up to 50 years built into the valuation.

    • Cash flow and financing need
      Operating cash flow was −DKK 132m in 2025 and −DKK 33m in H1 2026, and it has been negative every year since 2021 (FCF −DKK 162m TTM). Dividend and investments are financed by land sales (JSR 78.5 and Urban Partners DKK 158.3m) and new debt, so a dividend target of approx.     • Debt and refinancing
      The loan-to-value ratio is low (25%), but net debt/EBITDA is 17×, and the group's EBIT covers interest only 0.6×; interest cover in property management alone is 2.1×. 19% of debt (kr 319m) fell due within a year, and it has been refinanced with an unsecured bond of kr 300m at 3m Stibor + 6.00 percentage points vs an average rate of 4.7%.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Valuation of building rights
      Land and building rights of approx. kr 3.1bn are valued with a discount rate of 4% and waiting times of 0–20 years in Arlandastad and 0–50 years in Skavsta. The result depends on these unrealised revaluation gains (kr 74m in H1 and kr 185m in 2025).
      mb.cision.com
      −2
    • Governance and liquidity
      Gelba Management owns 42.9%, SBB 20.0% and Styviken Invest 13.2%, and chairman Leif West is also a director of Gelba, which sold 5.8% to SBB in January at SEK 42. The free float is about 24%, and average trading is only about 26,000 shares (about SEK 0.9m) per day. SBB is itself a pressured seller of assets, which can become an overhang.

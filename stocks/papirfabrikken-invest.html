    In plain words

    The score is middling: a property and hotel company in Silkeborg, where the share costs about 72% of the value of the company's assets. The return is low and debt is large compared with earnings, so higher interest rates could hurt. Almost all assets are in one town. In its favour, there are no vacant rental units and insiders own a large part. Only a few shares trade each day.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Property and hotel company (formerly Silkeborg IF Invest), which files only half-year reports and is not covered by analysts. The main multiple is price vs net asset value; stockanalysis has no forecast page and no PEG. The upgrade 11/9-2026 (result before fair value adjustment and tax DKK 30–35m from 10–20m) is due to a one-off gain of DKK 16.3m on redemption of a 1.5% bond loan below par and is not counted as raised guidance; the underlying expectation is approx. DKK 14–19m. Henton Group (CEO Kent Madsen) bought 1,885,365 shares at DKK 33.10 from Silkeborg Fodbold Holding, which is also closely related to the CEO; whether it is a real purchase with own funds or an internal transfer has not been clarified, so the insider purchase is given only +1. The tenants' names and concentration are not disclosed and not investigated, and whether the properties are valued by an external valuer has not been investigated. Web search was exhausted during the research; the figures come from the company's own announcements via Nasdaq, the company's website and stockanalysis.

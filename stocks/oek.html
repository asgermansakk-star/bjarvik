    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −22 of −30
    • Margin and returns
      Loss every year 2021–2025 (−kr 0.5m, −1.5m, −1.4m, −2.9m and −2.2m) and ROE between −1% and −8%. Operating costs (approx. kr 2.3–2.5m a year, mostly for the stock exchange listing, CEO and board) are twice as large as the expected dividends of approx. kr 1.2m in 2026, so the model structurally loses money.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −8
    • NAV development
      Equity has fallen from kr 41.7m at the end of 2022 (after the dividend of kr 10m) to kr 32.8m at 30/6-2026, approx. −21% in 3.5 years, and from kr 53.0m in 2021. The company is getting smaller, not stronger.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Moat and quality of the portfolio
      ØK owns only two minority stakes without control: 18.7% of Thai Poly Acrylic (controlled by Mitsubishi Chemicals via the joint venture AACL), which is squeezed by Chinese competition and has ROE of 1.7%, and 5% of the East Lake Villas property complex in Beijing, where Shougang Group owns 95%.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Capital allocation
      The strategy is the same year after year: active ownership and divestments 'at the right time', but no divestments are expected in 2026. Meanwhile listing costs eat into equity, and the liquidation of EAC China is expected to be protracted.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −4

      The joint venture East Lake Villas runs until December 2031 and has a shareholders' agreement which the company itself calls not favourable for ØK at expiry. The holding is carried at kr 11.6m, approx. 35% of equity, and ØK, as a 5% owner, has no control over the negotiations with Shougang.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −5
    • Cash flow
      FCF has been negative every year (−DKK 2.2m, −1.0m, −2.2m, −2.1m and −1.5m in 2021–2025, −1.1m in H1 2026). Liquidity has fallen from DKK 21.9m in 2021 to 7.2m, and the company itself expects approx. DKK 7.0m at the end of 2026.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Dividend halt in TPA
      TPA does not expect to pay a dividend for 2026 and, because of limited free reserves, can only pay again when earnings improve; the war in the Middle East is pressuring commodity prices. In 2024 TPA paid DKK 1m to ØK.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com
      −3
    • Liquidity in the stock
      On average 6 shares are traded per day (approx. kr 36,000), free float is 5,131 shares, and the price is down 30% over 52 weeks and 23% below the 200-day average. The price can move sharply on a few trades.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Governance
      The board has only three members, and two of them are not independent: CEO Kresten Valdal sits on the board himself and holds over 40 other board positions, and the new member comes from A.P. Møller Holding. Olav W. Hansen A/S owns about 23%.
      view.news.eu.nasdaq.com

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Investment company (shell company) with one employee: P/E, PEG, EV/EBITDA and ROIC are not meaningful, so price to NAV is the main multiple. No analysts and no price targets, so the upside and PEG points rest on missing data. The market value of the TPA stake is converted at an estimated rate of approx. kr 0.19–0.21 per THB and is transparent (17.4% is held via the joint venture AACL). Web search was used up during the research, so the Danish press and Børsen have not been reviewed; the findings are based on company announcements at Nasdaq and stockanalysis. No Q3 report has been published (the company reports semi-annually). It has not been examined who bought Martin Thaysen's shares, or whether he still owns shares.

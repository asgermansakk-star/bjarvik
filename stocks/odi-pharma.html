    In plain words

    The score is 50 out of 100. The micro company, with one employee, resells medical cannabis in Poland. Sales swing wildly, depend on a few wholesalers and one supplier, and three people own 90% of the shares. On the plus side, there is no interest-bearing debt and four quarters in a row of positive operating profit before depreciation. The shares trade rarely.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −17 of −30
    • Moat and business model
      Pure trading and distribution model without own cultivation or production: the product is bought from one large producer and sold via wholesalers and pharmacies. The company itself writes that rising competition has led to lower prices in Poland and sharp price pressure in Germany, so volume growth does not give corresponding value growth.
      mb.cision.com

    • Quality of growth
      Revenue swings wildly: kr 22.3m in 2023/24, 3.1m in 2024/25 and 32.9m in 2025/26. In Q4 sales fell to kr 3.2m from 12.7m in Q3, because the largest customer placed a large order in Q3. That is order timing, not a stable customer base.
      stockanalysis.com
      −6
    • Margin
      Gross margin 31% and operating margin 7.6% in the first profitable year; profit was kr 2.0m, and in Q4 only kr 0.15m. After years of losses the company has an accumulated deficit of about kr 25m vs paid-in capital of about kr 28m.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 7 findings    −25 of −35
    • Customer concentration
      Sales depend on a few wholesale customers; the timing of the largest customer's orders moved quarterly revenue by approx. 75%. The company has an exclusive supply agreement with the NEUCA group, which also handles production of the finished product.
      mb.cision.com
      −6
    • Supplier dependence
      The raw material comes from a single supply agreement with a subsidiary of the world's largest cannabis producer. If the agreement ends or changes, the company has no production of its own to fall back on.
      mb.cision.com
      −3
    • Regulation and macro
      Poland's tighter rules on prescribing controlled substances from the end of 2024 caused revenue to fall 86% in 2024/25. The whole business depends on regulation in one country, and it could happen again.
      mb.cision.com
      −4
    • Governance
      Three people, including board chair Volker Wiederrich with 38%, own 90% of the shares. Only approx. 10% (1.8m shares) is free float, and the company has one employee. The annual report has not been audited, so minority shareholders have limited influence and insight.
      mb.cision.com
      −4
    • Liquidity
      The agreement with the liquidity provider was terminated in January 2025. On average about 3,000 shares trade a day (about kr 5,000), and stockanalysis shows the latest trade on 30/9-2026. The price can move 15–30% in a single day.

      The share count rose approx. 20% in 2024/25 (from 15.2 to 18.3m) after a rights issue and a directed issue of approx. kr 2m in June 2025. Cash is only kr 3.4m, and the company itself writes that operations require a solid capital base; another bad year may require new capital.

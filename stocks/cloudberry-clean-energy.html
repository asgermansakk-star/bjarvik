    In plain words

    The score is 58 out of 100: a renewable-energy company that grows through acquisitions but earns very little on its investments. The main drags are high debt relative to earnings, a share count that has almost doubled, and only a small part of the power price being locked in. New plants were delivered on time and under budget. Watch power prices and the court case against the large shareholder Orrön.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    stockanalysis uses an outdated share count (336.08m at 30/6-2026) and therefore shows market cap NOK 4.37bn, P/B 0.59 and EV NOK 9.1bn. After the issue of 124.38m consideration shares to Orrön on 31/8-2026 there are approx. 460.5m shares, so the market cap at NOK 13.00 is approx. NOK 5.99bn; the multiples in the findings have been recalculated with the correct share count. The Orrön portfolio and the last 50% of MLK are included only for September, so the TTM figures understate operations, while the pro forma figures (the company's illustrative 2027 calculation at forward prices) are not guidance. Forward P/E 11.8 and the EPS estimate of 1.08 for 2026 rest on one analyst and the old share count; only 2 analysts have price targets (range NOK 13–14). NAV/intrinsic value has not been published and not assessed. The board's and management's total shareholding has only been partly investigated (CEO and CCO). The price follows the electricity price in the Nordics, which is currently lifted by low reservoirs and high gas prices. No special situation.

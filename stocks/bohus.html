    The score is high (90 out of 100), but the company only listed in June 2026. Pulling it down: the Norwegian furniture chain carries heavy debt (about 3.6 times yearly operating profit incl. leases), has just NOK 25m in cash and sells only in Norway. In its favour, margins have risen and management bought shares. Earlier owners sold in the listing.

      Net debt incl. leasing NOK 1,731m (3.6x EBITDA), interest cover 2.7x and quick ratio 0.04; cash has fallen from 454 to 25m after an extraordinary distribution of 150m and a dividend of 50m at the IPO. Without leasing, however, net debt is only 219m, and the liquidity reserve 624m.

    • Ownership and supply overhang
      The IPO was a pure sale by existing owners, especially former shop owners: board member Ole Kristian Sagvik's company sold 4.7m shares and Arve Nymoen's companies 1.5m. Only 51.3 of 99.5m shares are free float, so the rest may come up for sale when the lock-up expires.

      Only four quarters of full own operation; the company itself writes that the risk profile is now more closely tied to own stores, higher fixed costs, lease obligations and interest-bearing debt, and that profitability and cash flow are hit if the benefits are not realised. Five new stores in 2027 add further to the lease burden.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Listed 18/6-2026 (IPO at NOK 31), so there is no own multiple history, and the 52-week change and 200-day average are missing on stockanalysis. In July/September 2025 the company went from franchise to own operation (55 stores bought), so comparisons are based on the company's own pro forma figures for 2025; stockanalysis' growth rates (+125% TTM) are acquisition-driven and not used. Only 2 analysts cover the stock, and ABG Sundal Collier and DNB Carnegie were both joint global coordinators in the IPO. Trailing P/E includes IPO costs (NOK 39m in 2026). The share count has risen about 2.4% in 2026 through a non-cash issue (Møbelringen Lade) and settlement of the synthetic share programme by set-off, not in cash. Lock-up dates for the former store owners beyond the primary insiders' 360 days have not been investigated in the prospectus. Peers are only compared with Europris, Tokmanni and Puuilo from own dataset; Kid, Skeidar and Jysk have not been investigated.

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 2 findings    −5 of −30
    • Moat
      Low-price sports chain with about 10% market share in a Norwegian sports market in upheaval (XXL, Gresvig/Intersport), where Stadium has launched its Outlet concept in Norway. Own brands (about 60% of Sport Outlet's sales) give some differentiation, but low prices are easy to copy.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −8 of −35
    • Insider selling
      The existing owners (primarily management and founders) sold 24.2m shares for approx. NOK 800m in the IPO incl. greenshoe, while the company itself raised only NOK 500m. Before the listing they had already drawn approx. NOK 400m in dividends over three years. Management's new subscriptions were small relative to the sales, and Yokru Invest (the Kids Outlet management) sold down to 9.77%.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −3
    • Execution and seasonal fluctuations
      Earnings are in the second half: the EBIT margin was 10.3% in H1 2026 vs 14.8% LTM, and the company has not yet reported a quarter as a listed company. The rollout is to happen at the same time as the construction of a large new central warehouse, which part of the proceeds is to finance.
      outletgroup.com
      −2
    • Capital need and dividend target
      The dividend target of 50–80% of net profit must be balanced against capex for the central warehouse, 20–25 new stores a year and a possible rollout in Poland. The company itself mentions the capex need; net debt and FCF after leases are not stated in the announcements.
      outletgroup.com
      −2
    • Free float and liquidity
      Insiders own 46.4% and the free float is only about 24m shares (22%). The lock-up expires after 180 days for selling shareholders (March 2027), which may create an overhang.

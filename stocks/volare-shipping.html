    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −17 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Earnings are a pure freight cycle: six spot-operated VLCCs earned pro forma USD 208,300/day in Q3 and USD 717,300/day on 43% of the days in Q4, vs a cash breakeven of about USD 35,630/day. The rates are driven by the Hormuz crisis, not by the company's strength.
      volareshipping.com
      −8
    • Moat and organisation
      Crude oil transport without pricing power. The company has no commercial or operational organisation of its own: everything is run by Trafigura Maritime Logistics under management agreements, and there is no track record as a standalone company (Q3 went to TML).
      volareshipping.com
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      The fleet was assembled in 2026 close to the peak: four VLCCs from 2020–2022 bought for USD 125–130m each in March–May, Volare (built 2026) for USD 161.5m in September, and eight newbuilds at an average of approx. USD 114m with delivery 2027–28.
      volareshipping.com
      −3
    • Future segment
      The company's own document puts the order book for VLCCs at approx. 32% of the active fleet, and the newbuildings are delivered in 2027–28, when new tonnage hits the market.
      volareshipping.com
      −2

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −20 of −35
    • Macro and rates
      Spot rates depend on the closure of the Strait of Hormuz (VLCC transits fell from approx. 13.7 per day to almost zero in March–June 2026). The strategy is spot first, and the Q4 rate is approx. 20 times above breakeven, so a normalisation feeds straight through.
      volareshipping.com
      −8
    • Governance and related parties
      Trafigura owns approx. 53% and is both major shareholder and sole supplier: USD 300/day plus 1.25% of gross revenue for commercial operation, plus USD 750 and 2 × 550/day, non-cancellable for three years. TML allocates cargo between its own and Volare's fleet on a 'first come, first served' basis. The chair is Trafigura's head of shipping, the board owned 0 shares, and the board has a general authorisation to issue shares with no upper limit.
      volareshipping.com
      −5
    • Funding need
      The company itself calculates a liquidity shortfall of approx. USD 59m over the next 12 months. Approx. USD 422m in newbuilding instalments fall due in the period, and there are no binding loan agreements; in the worst case prepaid instalments may be lost.
      volareshipping.com
      −3
    • Sanctions and China exposure
      All six ships are financed by sale-and-leaseback with Chinese BOCOMM (16–21 years), and all eight newbuilds are being built at one Chinese yard (Hantong). This creates risk of sanctions against counterparties and of US Section 301 port fees.
      volareshipping.com
      −2
    • Overhang and price support
      The price was supported by stabilisation purchases of 1.19m shares (average NOK 150.94) until 28/10, and the greenshoe option has now been exercised. The price has fallen from 171.80 to 154.26 since 30/10, and Trafigura's lock-up (approx. 53%) lasts only 12 months.
      volareshipping.com
      −2

Peers (DHT, Okeanis, Frontline) have not been fetched again today. There is no data on insider trades after the listing; the board owned 0 shares at the listing. ROIC, margins and FCF cannot be assessed and score 0, so the grade is a cap. PEG, upside and revision points will lack data.

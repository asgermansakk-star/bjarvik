    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a biorefinery with stable operations but stagnant earnings. Earnings per share (excluding one-offs) have stood still for four years, and investments in start-ups have been written down by 582m Norwegian kroner. Management expects pricier energy, and the US has imposed duties on the company's cellulose. On the plus side: a broad customer base, cost savings, falling debt and a dividend covered by cash flow.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Reported EPS and trailing P/E (57.9) are distorted by writedowns on bio start-ups (NOK 245m in Q4 2025 and 337m in Q2 2026, booked in financial items) and a tax rate of 40%; the multiples have been assessed on the company's EPS excluding one-off items (NOK 8.67 in 2025, 7.13 in 9M 2026). stockanalysis' ROIC (7.6% TTM) is affected by the same. Lease payments are not shown separately in the cash flow statement (IFRS 16 debt NOK 467m), so FCF is before leases and therefore slightly too high. Insider trades have only been checked via search (employee share programme and options in February 2026); the ITC decision on US anti-dumping duty falls in Q4 2026. Only 5 analysts cover the stock.

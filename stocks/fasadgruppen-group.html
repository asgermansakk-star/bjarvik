    In plain words

    Fasadgruppen scores 52 out of 100: a renovation group whose shares are cheaper than ever, but whose business is under pressure. The biggest drags are falling margins and returns, shrinking revenue, and high debt. New shares in 2026 (up 62.5%) diluted owners. On the plus side, the order book is growing and the chairman bought shares. The CEO has left.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Q3 report comes on 12 November 2026 and is not included; the figures are based on H1 2026 (TTM to June). Forward P/E (5.9) is based on adjusted EPS before amortisation of acquired intangible assets from only 2–3 analysts, and the 2026 estimate (EPS 1.74) gives P/E of about 8.8. Stockanalysis' net debt (SEK 1.62bn) includes leasing, but not acquisition-related liabilities of 806m (options on minority shares 713m and contingent considerations 93m), which are added in T2 and T3. Peers' multiples (Instalco, Bravida) have not been fetched directly, so the comparison with peers is only qualitative and scores 0. Customer concentration in Sweden (housing co-operatives and property owners) has not been examined; only earnings concentration in Clear Line has been assessed. The 'Yes' column for the chair's and Tomas Ståhl's purchases in FI's register may mean that the purchase was made through a related person or company. The C1 shares (hurdle shares) in the incentive programme were subscribed at SEK 1.96 and give only a small dilution, which is not included.

    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a distributor to the healthcare sector that grows through acquisitions and is expensive relative to its low profitability. Only 5 of the 15% growth in Q3 came from its own businesses. Cash flow gives a return of only about 1.5%, and debt is rising. On the plus side: stable demand, spread-out customers and insider buying after the September price drop. Watch the main owner Interogo (about 40%).

    In the 70+ zone: a score above 70 with more than 20% upside to the median analyst target.

      ROIC 7.3% (TTM) vs 8.3–8.4% in 2024–2025 and 10.0% in 2021. Goodwill and other intangible assets (kr 10.3bn) exceed equity (kr 7.35bn); the company's own return on operating capital (R/RK 68.8%) ignores goodwill.

      Of the sales growth of 15% in Q3 2026, only 5% was organic and 10% from acquisitions; organic growth in adjusted EBITA was 4% (8% without double costs). In West, the largest area, organic growth was only 3%.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Only listed since March 2025, so the multiple history covers just FY2025 (inflated by IPO costs); there is no 5–10-year history. Forward P/E (22.9) is based on adjusted EPS (2.92), while IFRS EPS for 9M is only kr 1.63. The price fell 18% on 11 September 2026 (71.90 → 58.90) with no company announcement; the reason has not been found and is not scored. Customer behaviour and price pressure in public tenders (regions and municipalities are the main customers), competition and ratings have not been examined and score 0. The global peer comparison (Henry Schein, Owens & Minor etc.) has not been made; only AddLife is used. Only 5 analysts cover the stock. The share count rose 8.6% in a year due to the new issue at the IPO (kr 1,407m in the 2025 cash flow) and has since been fixed at 383.04m; not scored.

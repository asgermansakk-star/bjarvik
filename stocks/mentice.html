    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a small medtech company with high gross margins but no lasting profits. It has lost money every year since 2021, and losses were partly funded by issuing shares, which dilutes owners. Sales did rise 25.5% last quarter, and there is more cash than debt. Nearly 90% of sales go to a few large customers, and several managers have left. Watch the 10 November report.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 5 findings    −19 of −30
    • Margin
      Gross margin is high (88%), but operating margin is −2.8% TTM and −11.3% in 2025, and there has been an operating loss every year since 2021 (best −0.3% in 2023). EBIT in Q2 2026 was SEK 0.3m, but that included SEK 3.4m of currency gains under other income, so the underlying operations still made a loss. ROE is −4%, and ROIC is −11%.

      −7
    • Quality of growth
      Sales rose 25.5% in Q2 2026 (28% organic), and order intake rose 37%. This comes after two years of stagnation (+5.5% in 2024 and −3.9% in 2025), however, so the comparison base is weak, and growth is project-based and swings widely from quarter to quarter.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Moat and market
      Mentice is a leading niche player in endovascular procedure simulation and has partnerships such as the one with Siemens Healthineers on Ankyras. The market is small, however: revenue is about SEK 300m after 20 years, and Surgical Science (Simbionix) is a larger competitor with net cash.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The acquisitions of Vascular Simulations and Biomodex (2022–2023, approx. SEK 28m in cash) were merged in Denver in 2025, and the activities in Paris and Stony Brook were closed. At the same time up to 18% of employees were laid off, with SEK 7.5m in restructuring costs. The acquisitions have not delivered a profit.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Dilution
      The losses were financed with issues: SEK 60m in 2022 and a rights issue of 31.95m in August 2025, which increased the share count by 10% from 25.57 to 28.13m. This matches the cash flow statement (share issuance 31.96m in 2025).

    In plain words

    ING gets 98 out of 100. The biggest drag is the price: the share is up 44% in a year and now costs much more against the bank's book value than before. The bank has also been fined heavily over weak money-laundering controls. On the plus side, expectations were raised and it gained over 1m new customers in a year. Watch the competition for customer deposits.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful; valued on P/B, P/TBV and P/E, and payouts are measured against net profit. Stockanalysis' net profit (8.7bn TTM, EPS 2.99, trailing P/E 10.6) is distorted and does not match ING's own net profit of 6.7bn over 12 months (EPS about 2.35, P/E about 13.5) or its own forecast table (2025 EPS 2.12); P/B is therefore used as the main multiple. The forward P/E (11.6–12.8) is on adjusted EPS. Share count −6.3% according to stockanalysis; buybacks of 2.8bn TTM equal only about 4%, the rest is earlier programmes. Price targets were raised after Q2, but the median is only about 2% above the price. The Russia exposure (wind-down) has not been examined separately. Q3 report on 29 Oct.

    In plain words

    The score is 72 out of 100: a small Swedish service for self-employed workers with very thin margins. Points are lost because only 1.25% of revenue becomes operating profit, the CEO owns about 62%, and a trade union is demanding negotiations. On the plus side, earnings have risen four quarters in a row and the roughly 5.2% dividend is covered by cash flow. The share trades very thinly.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −12 of −30
    • Margin
      Operating margin is only 1.25% (TTM) of gross revenue of SEK 1.94bn and gross margin 3.3%; in 2021–2025 the operating margin was 0.5–1.2%. Of the gross profit of SEK 63m, approx. 38% becomes EBIT, but margins this thin make the result very sensitive to small changes in fees or costs.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue grew 5.5% (TTM), but growth is slowing: July −2.3%, August +10.3% and September +0.3%, in total approx. +2.4% in Q3. The number of egenanställda (self-employed people employed through the company) in Sweden rose only 1.7% in September and 0.7% in July. The earnings improvement comes mainly from cost savings, which are a lever that can only be used once.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Moat and future segment
      Egenanställning is a simple, digital service with low barriers to entry and price competition from other providers. Workforce Management (B2B, approx. 16% of revenue) fell 13.1% in Q2 on lower demand from the largest customer. The business model rests on a labour-law grey area that is being regulated.

      International expansion is only 7% of revenue, and the French subsidiary is already being wound down in 2026 after a centralisation in 2025. There are no acquisitions or goodwill, but also no documented success outside Sweden beyond Norway and Denmark.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Revenue is gross incl. the wages of the self-employed, so margins are small by nature; operating margin is also assessed against gross profit. The cash of SEK 110m is largely funds for wages and tax that must be paid on (accruals and other current liabilities of SEK 242m), so stockanalysis' EV of SEK 85m and EV/EBIT of 3.5 are too low; adjusted, EV/EBIT is approx. 6–8. No analyst coverage and no forward estimate, so PEG is n/a and there is no price target. The Q3 report comes 11/11-2026 and is not included; Q3 revenue is calculated from the monthly figures for July–September. Stockanalysis shows 4.08% insider ownership, but CEO Stephen Schad owns approx. 62.3% according to the notice of the extraordinary general meeting. Insider trading in 2026 has not been investigated (0 points). The stock is traded on Spotlight according to MFN (Spotlight Value from 24/9-2025), not NGM; the stockanalysis path is quote/ngm/FRILAN. The company arose through a reverse takeover with SPACtvå in 2023, so history before 2023 is limited.

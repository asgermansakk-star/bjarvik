    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −15 of −30
    • Returns
      ROIC is 2.1% and has been 0.8–3.1% since 2021; ROE was only 2.8% (annualised) in H1 2026 vs the company's new target of at least 10%. The properties' direct return has fallen to 3.72% (4.29), while the interest expense of SEK 14.7m in the half year corresponds to approx. 3.3% of debt, so the spread between net operating income and interest is almost gone.

    • Quality of growth
      Revenue rose 77% to SEK 44.1m in H1 2026 because of the acquisition, but förvaltningsresultatet per share fell 19% to SEK 3.74 (4.60), and earnings per share fell to SEK 3.03 (6.38). The margin fell to 65.7% (66.1). Management explains part of it with one-off costs of taking over administration from Pareto.
      cdn.bequoted.com
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      Bonäsudden doubled its property value by buying two newly built residential properties in Växjö and Kalmar for SEK 676m against 1,033,060 new shares (+61%) at an NRV price of SEK 227.64. With a normalised rent of about SEK 40m, that is a gross yield of only about 5.9%, and the purchase lowered the portfolio's direct yield. The seller was Smålandsvist, which is related to the CEO, and the purchase has so far not lifted income from property management per share, as the company promised.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Scale and organisation
      The company has 13 properties (approx. 54,600 m²) in regional towns in Östergötland and Småland, no employees and external management. The agreement with Pareto Business Management has been terminated, and day-to-day operations now rest with P&E Fastighetspartner. Central administration cost SEK 3.4m in H1, equal to 8% of rental income.

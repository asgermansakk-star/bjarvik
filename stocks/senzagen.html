    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a small company that is only just nearing a profit, and the score is middling. It has lost money for years and grew just 7% over the past year. The share count is up about 10%, and an acquisition was funded with a loan that can turn into more shares. On the plus side, 90% of sales come from returning customers, and the CEO has bought shares.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

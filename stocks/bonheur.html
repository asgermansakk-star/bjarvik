    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a family-controlled holding company in wind energy, wind services and cruises, where earnings per share fell 29%. The biggest drags are the oldest wind farms losing subsidies in March 2027, early-stage costly offshore wind projects, and power sold at changing daily prices. The Olsen family controls about half the shares. The chairman has bought shares, the group has net cash, and the order book is growing.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Holding company (Fred. Olsen group) with four segments: renewable energy (wind farms with 51% ownership), wind services (FOWIC 82.95%, GWS 96.08%), cruise and other investments (incl. 55% of NHST). stockanalysis' P/B 0.88 uses equity incl. minorities; against the parent company's equity (9,270m), P/B is about 1.2. No full sum-of-the-parts/NAV has been done; the MEAG deal priced FOWIC at about EUR 600m before new capital, corresponding to about half of Bonheur's market cap for Bonheur's share, so there is probably a holding discount that is not rewarded. Marked cyclical because the largest EBITDA source sells power at spot price, and wind installation follows an investment cycle. Only 4 analysts and low trading volume (about 35,000 shares a day). Customer concentration in wind services has not been examined (0 points).

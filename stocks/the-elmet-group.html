    In plain words

    The Elmet Group gets 57: earnings have been lifted by a sharply rising tungsten price, and analysts expect earnings per share to fall in 2027. The company listed in April and has already committed to several large investments and acquisitions, while operating cash flow is negative. The Department of War bought $200m of preferred shares with a 5.5% annual dividend and received warrants equal to 19.9% of the company fully diluted, plus a veto over dividends, debt and a sale of the company. On the plus side, the order backlog has almost quadrupled in a year, mostly from defence. Watch the government's final $250m, which comes from March 2027 at the earliest and at the government's own discretion.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    New stock, scored 24 Nov 2026 in the re-evaluation of companies with US government ownership. Ticker ELMT on Nasdaq; listed via an IPO at $14 per share, priced 23 Apr and completed 24 Apr 2026 (9.86m shares, net $128.2m; Cantor Fitzgerald was lead underwriter). Only about 7 months of price history and no multiple history as a listed company, so Step 2 rests only on peers and absolute levels; no 200-day average, so the price-drop guard cannot trigger, even though the share price ($16.80 on 24 Nov intraday) is 18% below the 50-day average. The government deal (8-K 14 Sep 2026): the Department of War bought 200,000 Class A preferred shares for $200m (stated value $1,000, 5.5% PIK dividend, senior ranking, redeemable by the government on liquidation, default or after 10 years) plus a penny warrant for 5,675,506 shares at $0.001 and a warrant for 1,891,835 shares at $15.92, both exercisable after 12 months and for 10 years (7,567,341 shares in total = 19.9% fully diluted); exercise of the penny warrant writes down the preferred shares' liquidation preference by 0–65%. A further up to $250m in preferred shares in tranches from March 2027, with the government approving the terms at its own discretion. The preferred shares vote with 19.9%, the government appoints one board member and one observer and has a veto over dividends, buybacks, debt and change of control. The forward P/E varies between sources (17.0 on 2026E adjusted EPS of $0.98; stockanalysis statistics show 25.3), and analysts expect EPS to fall 12% in 2027. Only 4 analysts, all positive, and several banks took part in the IPO; the price target ($28–30) was set before and just after Q3, so upside points are halved. GAAP figures for 2026 are distorted by IPO costs and stock options (Q2 operating loss −$7.6m) and the conversion from S to C corporation. Not examined: the price and financing of the OSRAM acquisition (settled only at closing), Masan High-Tech Materials' own finances and Kaz Resources' competing, Trump-linked tungsten project.

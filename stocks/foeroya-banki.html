Operating income fell 5% in Q1–Q3 2026 to 382m, insurance income fell 39% (combined ratio 91 vs 77), and the Greenland segment's profit before tax fell 42% to 27m. Only net interest and fee income grew (+1%), and lending rose just 3.5%.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 3 findings    −7 of −35
    • Concentration in two small economies
      The bank operates only in the Faroe Islands (approx. 55,000 inhabitants) and in Greenland, where fish and fish farming account for approx. 95% and 90% of goods exports; the bank itself cites dependence on fish prices and quotas as a key risk. Lending is 13% real estate, 8% fishing/agriculture and 8% transport/hotels, and large exposures amount to 141.7% of capital (limit 175%).
      via.ritzau.dk
      −3
    • Credit quality and upgrade on low impairments
      The upgrade in October is mainly due to lower impairments than expected; net impairments are a reversal of kr 2.5m in Q1–Q3. Beneath the surface, stage 2 provisions rose from 25.9 to 71.4m (net charge 45.6m), offset by reversals in stages 1 and 3 – more loans have migrated to elevated risk. The level can only normalise upwards (cushioned by a management judgement of 121.5m).
      via.ritzau.dk
      −2
    • Liquidity and ownership concentration
      On average only about 5,100 shares are traded a day (about kr 1.5m). The Faroese Government owns 34.8%, Ruth Holding 14.6%, GTM Familie Holding 10.4% and RMV Holding 5.1%, so free float is about 35%; one board lawyer sits in all three private holding companies. Spreads and price swings on larger trades can be considerable.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful; valuation on P/TBV (≈ P/B, only DKK 3m in intangible assets) and P/E, and dividend is measured against net profit. No analysts on stockanalysis (forecast = 404), so there is no forward estimate; P/E on guidance is calculated on DKK 225–255m and 9.57m shares. P/B history only covers 2021–2025 (stockanalysis and the annual report). Insider trades in 2026 have not been examined (only the board's holding at the end of 2025), and Faroese dividend tax for Danish investors has not been examined. The price jump from DKK 162 to DKK 286 in 2025 coincides with the Greenland speculation (FT, January 2026), but the bank has only approx. 10% of its income in Greenland; the reason for the re-rating is not documented further. The insurance result fluctuates greatly from quarter to quarter.

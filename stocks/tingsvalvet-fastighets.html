    In plain words

    The score is 59 out of 100: a Swedish property company with tenants in smaller towns, whose shares are up 33% in a year and now look pricey against the value of the properties. Debt is 60.6% of property value, and interest is fixed for under a year, so higher rates bite quickly. Occupancy is high (98.9%). Watch the share options that could create up to 19m new shares.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −10 of −30
    • ROIC and returns
      ROIC is 3.9% vs WACC of 4.95% and has been −0.3–4.3% since 2021. Return on equity is 8.7% (rolling 12 months) vs the company's own target of 12%, and net asset value per ordinary share rose 10.6% in a year (kr 46.33 → 51.22) vs the target of 12%.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue rose 26.8% and förvaltningsresultatet per ordinary share 48.9% in H1 2026, but growth comes especially from acquisitions (kr 210m in H1 vs only 8.6m in unrealised value increases). Earning capacity per ordinary share is almost unchanged from Q1 (3.69 → kr 3.71), the margin fell to 79.1% (81.1), and the CEO writes that letting is taking longer because tenants are cautious.
      mb.cision.com
      −2
    • Portfolio quality
      The portfolio consists of retail (29.9%), industrial (29.7%), office (20.4%) and warehouse/logistics (16.8%) in central Swedish regional towns and smaller towns such as Askersund, Fagersta, Gnosjö and Åmotfors. The yield requirement is high (6.9%), and among the largest tenants are discount and leisure concepts such as Dollarstore, Nordic Wellness, O'Learys and Jumpyard, so the portfolio is more cyclically sensitive than at big-city owners.
      mb.cision.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The acquisitions are paid for with debt and a series of in-kind share issues: since 2023, nine in-kind issues have been made of preference shares (SEK 260–348) and B shares (SEK 40.78–46.33 per share, below the later reported net asset value). The preference shares receive SEK 24 a year (SEK 29m, approx. 31% of the förvaltningsresultat in the earning capacity of 93.7m), so the capital costs approx. 7–8% vs a direct yield of 6.6%.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −21 of −35
    • Debt and refinancing
      The loan-to-value ratio rose to 60.6% (57.7; the bond covenant is 65%), and interest cover is 1.8× below the company's own target of 2.0× (covenant 1.5). The fixed-interest period is only 0.88 years, and 66% of debt has interest maturity within a year. Debt maturity is 2.42 years, and kr 597m (28.6%) falls due within 12 months. Net debt/EBITDA is about 12×, and the bond of kr 500m costs STIBOR + 425–500 bp.
      mb.cision.com
      −10
    • Dilution and warrants
      According to the Q2 report, there are 9.66m TO1 options, each giving the right to two A shares at kr 55 until 2030, i.e. up to 19.3m new A shares vs 17.46m ordinary shares today. This is a large overhang on the share price and on the distribution of votes. In addition, the number of ordinary shares has risen 5.1% in a year and the number of preference shares 15% via non-cash issues.
      mb.cision.com
      −4
    • Liquidity in the stock
      The A share trades on average only about 105 shares a day (about kr 5,000), and free float is about 5.6m shares. The price can move a lot on small orders.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Governance and reporting
      With three share classes and options, the ten largest owners control 78% of the votes, and the listed competitor NP3 Fastigheter owns 21.9% of the capital. The company had a consultant as CFO until September 2025 and changed Certified Adviser in March 2026 and liquidity provider in October 2025. The Q2 report has sloppy errors: it refers to the annual report for 2024 and to the price 'per sista mars' and writes '254,5,0 mkr'.
      tingsvalvet.se
      −2
    • Tenant concentration and behaviour
      The ten largest tenants account for 36.3% of rent (42.5), with SBAB alone at 8.7%. That is a more concentrated tenant base than at peers such as Emilshus (15%) and Altra (12%). There is vacancy in Askersund, Eskilstuna, Fagersta and Karlskoga, and management describes new tenants as cautious.

    In plain words

    Eos Energy Enterprises gets 31: the company loses money on every battery it sells, and cash lasts less than a year at the current burn rate. The share count has doubled in a year, preferred shares and convertible loans can create many more shares, and the price has fallen about 90%. About 83% of third-quarter revenue came from a company controlled by Cerberus, which is also a lender and owns the preferred shares. The US government has lent the company $178m more cheaply than the market would, and the Department of Energy received a warrant for 570,000 shares (under 0.2%) as payment for approving new financing; the rest of the loan, $99.5m, only comes if conditions are met. Watch for new share issues at low prices.

    The model found several serious problems. That does not mean the stock will fall.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    New stock, scored 24 Nov 2026 in the re-evaluation of companies with US government ownership. The government's stake is small and indirect: on 18 Nov 2025 the Department of Energy (DOE) received a warrant for up to 570,000 shares at $0.01 (5 years, automatic cashless exercise if the 15-trading-day VWAP is above $30 on the 12-month anniversary or every 6 months thereafter, otherwise at expiry on 21 Nov 2030; no voting rights before exercise) as payment for DOE's consent to convertible bonds and a share issue under the loan guarantee from 26 Nov 2024. That is about 0.16% of the 366m shares – not a stake that changes anything, and used only as evidence of the dependence on the DOE loan. GAAP net income of $540m TTM (Q3: $125m) is non-cash fair-value gains on warrants, derivatives and preferred shares, because the share price has fallen about 90% in a year; it has been removed from all findings, and the forward P/E of 1.96 and the 2026 EPS estimate of $0.65 are useless for the same reason. No going concern note in the Q3 10-Q. The price of $1.26 (24 Nov intraday) is far below the 200-day average ($5.09), so the price-drop guard applies; the median price target of $4 implies over 100% upside and thus 0 upside points. 11 analysts, but 7 rate it Hold, and the price targets ($2–10) were set before and just after Q3. Director Alexander Dimitrief bought 25,000 shares on 12 Nov and 25,000 shares on 20 Nov (about $83,000 in total) – too little against management's sales to earn a plus. The $2.19bn accumulated deficit and the preferred shares' redemption right from June 2029 are included in the debt and dilution findings. Not examined: patents/technological edge over lithium iron phosphate, warranty obligations on delivered systems and FPUSA's own financing beyond Cerberus' commitment.

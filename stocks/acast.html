    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −15 of −30
    • Margin
      Gross margin is fixed at about 39% because roughly 61% of revenue passes on to the podcasters. Operating margin is 4.8% TTM and 6% (adjusted) in the record quarter Q3, and the company only became profitable at operating level in 2025 (1.1%). The target of a 10% EBIT margin in 2028 is still far off.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Organic growth fell from 29% in Q2 to 18% in Q3, and Europe grew only 5%. Listens and views rose just 4% (2% over nine months), so growth comes almost entirely from higher earnings per listen (ARPLV +20%) and from acquisitions. Backyard Ventures contributed SEK 28m in the quarter.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Moat and future segment
      Podcasting is becoming video. Acast only counts views in Apple's HLS format, while YouTube and Spotify own the largest video platforms and sell ads themselves. Podcasters can switch networks, and Acast has to buy its way into video and newsletters through acquisitions.
      mfn.se
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      Losses totalling approx. SEK 766m in 2021–2023 swallowed a large part of the 1.25bn raised at the stock exchange listing; cash has fallen from 1,365m (2021) to 479m. SEK 144m has been spent on cash acquisitions TTM (incl. Backyard Ventures for USD 20m), and goodwill has risen from 390 to 621m in a year.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3

    • Multiples vs own history
      P/E 36.9 TTM, EV/EBIT 34 and EV/EBITDA 26. P/TBV 9.6 is a record vs 1.4–9.0 in 2021–2025, and P/B 4.1 is high vs 0.9–5.3. EV/Sales 1.65 is above the 2024 level (1.19). Forward P/E 22.8 is lower than in 2024–2025, but that history is built on almost no earnings; P/E is about 34 on analysts' EPS for 2026 (0.81).

    • FCF yield
      Operating cash flow is SEK 178m TTM. After capex (13m), capitalised development (65m) and leasing (33m), FCF is only approx. 68m, equal to approx. 1.3% of the market cap of SEK 5.15bn (P/FCF approx. 75).
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Peers
      EV/EBIT 34 is almost on a par with Spotify's 37.5, but Spotify has an EBIT margin of 14.6% and ROIC above 100% vs Acast's 4.8% and 15.7%. Acast is only cheaper on EV/Sales (1.65 vs 5.5), and that is due to the low margin.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −11 of −35
    • Dilution
      The share count has risen 4% in a year, and in September 2.78m new shares came from the 2023/2026 option programme. With 9.1m unexercised warrants and 1.2m shares to Backyard and Wake Word, the share count could reach 195.5m, i.e. 5.6% above the current 185.2m. Share-based compensation is SEK 35m a year.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Cash flow
      Operating cash flow in Q3 was only SEK 21.7m vs EBITDA of 73.7m, because receivables rose to 780m. In addition, Acast has withheld SEK 96m in tax for employees' option exercises. The amount has to be paid and effectively reduces the cash of 479m.
      storage.mfn.se
      −2
    • Platform and ad market risk
      All revenue is advertising, which follows advertisers' budgets. Q2 was lifted by World Cup advertising, and Europe grew only 5% in Q3. Distribution depends on Apple, Spotify and YouTube, which are both partners and competitors for ad spend.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Management
      Both the chief executive and the CFO have changed within about a year. Ross Adams was replaced by Greg Glenday as CEO, and Emily Villatte (CFO since 2019) by Anders Hägg from 15/1-2026. A new head of Europe will only join in 2027.
      mfn.se
      −2
    • Insider ownership
      Insiders own only 1.33% of the shares. Management's new shares come mainly from option exercise: CEO Glenday received 367,332 shares on 25/9 at SEK 0.0065 per share.
      marknadssok.fi.se
      −1

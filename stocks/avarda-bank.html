    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 2 findings    −5 of −30
    • Business model and moat
      Earnings rest on unsecured credit cards and consumer loans with a net loss rate of 4.9% of loans in the first nine months of the year; Everyday Finance (credit cards and e-commerce payment) has a loss rate of 6.0%. The moat is credit models and a cheap platform (C/I 34%), not customer lock-in; the product competes with banks, BNPL players and fintechs.

      Lending growth of 20% comes almost entirely from credit cards in Germany and Austria (+45%), while consumer loans in the Nordics are shrinking (−1%). Credit losses rose 34% vs 20% in lending, so growth is in the segment with the highest losses.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −11 of −35
    • Credit risk and macro
      The net loss rate rose to 5.1% in Q3 (4.5), and management itself notes that the ECB raised rates again in September after June. Unsecured credit cards are the first place a weaker economy and higher rates show up; stage 3 loans are kept down by ongoing sales of non-performing loans, so the loss level also depends on prices in the NPL market.
      mb.cision.com
      −4
    • Capital
      CET1 fell to 12.5% (14.3% a year ago), and the total capital ratio of 15.2% is only 2.3 percentage points above the requirement of 12.9% – below the bank's own target of at least 2.5 percentage points. The bank therefore issued SEK 250m in AT1 at Stibor + 450 bp to refinance SEK 100m; growth towards the target of SEK 35bn in lending ties up capital and limits the dividend.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Governance and related parties
      TFB Holding (28.2%), Tiberon (15.0%) and Erik Selin Fastigheter (12.5%) own 56% together, and free float is approx. 23m shares. The bank does business with companies that have the same owners as TFB Holding, and the divestment of Rediem Capital was made to Alektum Holding and the major shareholder Erik Selin Fastigheter (one-off gain of SEK 21.6m in 2026).
      mb.cision.com
      −2
    • Geographic concentration
      Germany accounts for 49% of lending and 63% of deposits, and according to management Germany is the primary driver of both growth and earnings; a weakening of German consumption or hard competition for deposits there hits both the asset and liability sides.
      mb.cision.com
      −1
    • Regulation
      The EU's new Consumer Credit Directive (CCD2) is to apply from 20 November 2026 with stricter creditworthiness assessment requirements and also covers small credits and deferred payment, which hits credit cards and e-commerce payments in all of the bank's markets.
      eur-lex.europa.eu
      −1

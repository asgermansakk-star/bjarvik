    In plain words

    The score is 59 out of 100: a small Swedish flour mill where sales have fallen and the shares are expensive. They cost about 41 times annual profit, while larger food companies sit at 13-19 times. On the plus side, there is no debt and about SEK 50m in cash. Watch grain and energy prices, which are rising again and may erase this year's tailwind. The shares trade very thinly.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Microcap (approx. kr 556m) with no analyst coverage and no forward estimate, so PEG is n/a and there is no price target. Trading is very thin (approx. 500 shares a day), so the price and thereby the multiples may be noisy. stockanalysis' TTM cash flow repeats full-year 2025; FCF is therefore calculated manually from the interim report (operating cash flow 21.9m and investments 7.5m over 12 months). The company has no lease debt. Customer concentration is not disclosed in the interim report and is set to 0. Insider trades have only been checked in Finansinspektionen's register (one purchase in 2024). Earnings are affected by grain prices, but the mill is not marked cyclical, because flour is a staple food, and it is the margin and not demand that fluctuates.

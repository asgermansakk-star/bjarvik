    In plain words

    Bjarvik gives 84 out of 100 to a small Norwegian savings bank (the security is an equity certificate). Profit per certificate fell despite a merger, the price is higher than before relative to book value, and about 75% of business loans are to property. On the plus side, the yield is about 6.3% and capital is well above the requirement. Another merger is planned.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The security is an equity certificate (ECC), not an ordinary share; the ownership fraction is 45.12%, so 55% of equity is self-owned (primary capital). It is economically equivalent to a share and is scored as such. stockanalysis's P/B of 0.53, P/E of 15.5 and share count change of +214% are based on total equity and on the merger with Tinn Sparebank (3/11-2025), in which five savings bank foundations received new certificates; the bank's own figures per equity certificate have therefore been used (book value NOK 304.5, EPS NOK 19.37 for Q1–Q3). Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful; dividend is measured against net income. On 29/10-2026 the boards approved a merger plan with Romerike Sparebank, in which Skue is the acquiring bank and issues 3.54m new certificates at a price of 338.30; the plan requires approval at the general meetings on 8/12-2026 and from Finanstilsynet and Konkurransetilsynet, and completion is expected approx. 1/9-2027. Skue's certificate price does not follow the deal terms, so it is not flagged as a special situation. Romerike Sparebank's own earnings and credit quality have not been examined. Analyst coverage is thin (no forecast page on stockanalysis). The peer comparison is based on figures from other Norwegian savings banks scored the same day.

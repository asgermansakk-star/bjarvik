      The state owns 99.6% after the mandatory offer in 2025; only 0.4% (approx. kr 180m) is free float, no analysts cover the stock, and in 2025 an average of 273 shares a day were traded (stockanalysis: 36 shares/day over the last 20 days, approx. kr 0.2m).     Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Danish state owns 99.6% after the mandatory takeover offer at the end of 2025; no squeeze-out or delisting has been announced as of 6/11-2026, so special situation is not set. If a squeeze-out comes, the stock must be flagged. No analysts cover the stock: there is no forward estimate and no price target, so PEG and upside are not based on data (expected n/a). stockanalysis' 'float' of 4.84m shares is wrong; the real free share count is about 0.4% (annual report). stockanalysis' TTM ends in Q2; the key figures in the findings are calculated with the Q3 report from 5/11-2026 and adjusted for kr 134m in other income (mainly gain on the sale of Smarter Airports). The pre-covid multiples (2015–2019) have not been examined, as stockanalysis only shows 2021–2025, which is marked by recovery. stockanalysis has no forecast page (404). Insider trading is not relevant (state-owned, no options for management). The airport is placed under 'Transport & logistics' like Aena.

    • Debt and financing cost
      The Fenja loan of SEK 90m runs to August 2028 at STIBOR (floor 2%) + 4%, an arrangement fee of 1.8m and a minimum return to the lender of 25% of the loan amount, equivalent to an effective interest rate of approx. 12–13% a year. On top comes a subordinated loan of EUR 5m at 6% from the largest shareholder. Net debt is approx. 120m vs an adjusted EBITDA of approx. 27m a year, and EBIT does not cover interest.
      mfn.se
      −6
    • Cash flow after capitalised development and leasing
      Operating cash flow was −SEK 8.9m in H1 2026. After capitalised development (−4.9m) and leasing (−0.5m), FCF is approx. −14.4m, and for 2025 it was approx. −2.3m. Cash was 8.5m at 30/6 and was lifted to approx. 25m only by the new loan. stockanalysis' FCF of +10.3m does not include the capitalised development.
      mfn.se
      −5

      The convertible (price SEK 31.56) and warrants to Fenja (SEK 35.35) give up to 8.8% dilution, and 825,386 employee warrants were exercised in October (approx. 2%). In February management said the company was ready for a capital raise and that Houlihan Lokey is working on debt financing of further acquisitions.

      The vendor note from the acquisitions has been refinanced as a EUR 5m loan from the largest shareholder, House of K, which the company itself calls a related-party transaction. The board switched to IFRS to avoid goodwill amortisation and 'show' a profit, and management highlights adjusted EBITDA despite restructuring costs three years in a row. The half-year report has not been reviewed by the auditor.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Q3 report is not published until 9/11-2026, so the analysis is based on Q2 (24/8) and announcements up to and including 30/10; the Q2 report has not been reviewed by the auditor. stockanalysis shows net cash of SEK 7.6m and an FCF of +10.3m, but overlooks the long-term loans of 126.9m and counts capitalised development as investment; the findings use the company's own figures. The company has switched to IFRS and has bought Safestate (April 2025) and Safeweb (November 2025), so the history on stockanalysis is not comparable; growth is measured against the company's own pro forma figures for 2025. It is unresolved whether House of K, the largest shareholder, is linked to the CEO or to the chair; the full ownership list has not been examined. No analysts cover the stock, and there are no forward estimates. The price rose 9.9% on 6/11 without an announcement. Trading is thin: about 30,000 shares a day on NGM.

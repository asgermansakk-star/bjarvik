    In plain words

    Score 77 out of 100: a healthcare group whose stock is expensive compared with similar companies. The biggest drags are a price 50-200% above competitors despite a profit margin of only 7.9%, and growth that is mostly price rises, not more patients. Debt is large, with 225m euros due within a year. In its favour is the sale of the Indian hospital arm to KKR, not yet completed.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The divestment of Medicover Hospitals India (MHI) to KKR has been signed but is not expected to complete until Q4 2026; the company reports MHI as a discontinued operation, so the findings are based on the company's own figures for continuing operations (LTM revenue EUR 2,281m, EBIT EUR 179.5m), while stockanalysis' TTM figures and history mix in India and are used only as background. Analysts' EPS estimate for 2026 (EUR 0.68) may still include MHI, and only 7 analysts cover the stock. The divestment is a sale of a subsidiary, not a bid for the Medicover shares, so there is no special situation. Not investigated: voting split between A and B shares and the main shareholder's (Celox) share, credit rating, and the share of revenue from public payers beyond the FFS shares; these criteria give 0.

    In plain words

    IBM gets 71: the price is in the middle of its own history, but part of the growth is bought, and the acquisitions were partly funded with loans, lifting net debt to $56.1bn. Consulting stands still, and the share is down 22% in a year on fears that AI tools will squeeze IBM's older core business. The US government holds no IBM shares but has a minority stake in the subsidiary Anderon in return for up to $1bn in support; the amount is small for IBM, and the government can cancel the deal. On the plus side, recurring software grows 8% and cash flow covers the dividend comfortably. Watch whether the gross margin disappoints again.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    • Guidance and gross margin
      Revenue guidance was cut in July to 4–5% from above 5% and has not been raised again after Q3. The gross margin fell in Q3 (operating 58.5%, −0.2 points; GAAP 56.8% against 57.3%) and disappointed analysts, and the share fell about 4% after the report.

      The stock is −21.7% over 52 weeks and trades below the 200-day average ($233 vs $245). It was de-rated on fears that AI coding tools can modernise COBOL systems and bypass IBM's mainframe and consulting base; IBM Z fell 20% in Q3. The risk is structural.
      finance.yahoo.com

      Cash, restricted cash and securities fell $3.4bn to $11.1bn in 2026, while debt rose $2.6bn to $63.8bn (of which $12.7bn in IBM Financing). Debt/EBITDA is 3.7 and the current ratio 0.85, and IBM borrowed a further ¥108.8bn in November. The rating is stable (A-/A3/A-).

      At the Quantum Developer Conference (11–13 Nov), IBM showed Nighthawk r3 (520 qubits, 2027) and a coupled 1,040-qubit system, but the 2026 target of a fault-tolerant module (Kookaburra) was not confirmed as met. Starling (2029) is still only a target.

      The Commerce Department got a minority stake in the subsidiary Anderon LLC (not in IBM) against $650m at closing and $350m against milestones; the agreement gives the government a right to terminate 'for convenience' and repayment claims. The amount is below 1.5% of IBM's revenue, so the funding does not change much, while the risk is political: the Democrats take over Congress in January 2027 and have announced hearings on the government's corporate stakes and the CHIPS authority.

    Re-evaluated 24 Nov 2026 (government equity stakes). Since 29 Sep: the Q3 results (21 Oct) beat expectations on revenue and EPS, but the gross margin disappointed; revenue guidance (4–5% at constant currency) was maintained and the FCF target raised to over $1bn more than 2025. The government stake is NOT in IBM: Anderon LLC (an IBM subsidiary) signed a final CHIPS agreement on 14 Sep (published 16 Sep) for up to $1.0bn ($650m at closing, $350m against milestones), IBM has contributed $1.0bn in cash plus IP, and the Commerce Department got a minority stake in Anderon; IBM keeps control and consolidates Anderon. The ownership percentage is not disclosed; minority interests rose $576m in 2026 ($557m as a financing inflow), so the government's share is booked at about $0.6bn. The quantum business is still an option below 1% of revenue. Frank Baker (Siris Capital) joined the board on 1 Oct; IBM issued ¥108.8bn in bonds in November. Kookaburra (fault-tolerant module 2026) was not confirmed at QDC in November. Insider trading and customer concentration in consulting have not been investigated.

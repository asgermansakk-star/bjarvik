      Revenue rose to SEK 47.9m in H1 (13.1), but the growth is delivery of battery hardware in two individual projects (total SEK 76m, of which approx. 44m invoiced by the half-year). It is project-based and will not repeat by itself; the recurring income from own plants does not yet exist.

    • Moat
      Byhmgard is a small integrator that resells Chinese battery systems (MoU with SANY on battery supply) and competes in a market with many EPC and project developers. The gross margin of 18–20% shows no pricing power. The in-house control software has no documented revenue.

SEK 150m, close to the whole market cap), and Hitura (50 MW) and the target of 1 GW in 2027 require far more.

      The entire order backlog of SEK 76m stems from two agreements with one customer, Solarigo Systems Oy. The new order of about SEK 14m (September) and the Hitura project also come through Solarigo, so revenue and pipeline hang on one Finnish partner.

    • Dilution
      The rights issue in September (181.5m new shares at SEK 0.20, +20%) was only 41% subscribed by shareholders; the guarantors took 59% for a 12% cash premium, and costs were approx. SEK 7m of 36m. Warrants TO2 (SEK 0.21, March 2027) are fully guaranteed against a 20% premium in new shares. In total the share count could go from 908m to approx. 1,307m (+44%) in under a year, and the price is below the issue price.

      −6
    • Funding need
      Half of the framework for the Latvian projects (about EUR 6.8m) is to be covered by financing partners through capital contributions in Byhmgard or in the project companies or through guarantees. Construction start has not yet been ordered, and Hitura presupposes equity from SANY after a non-binding MoU. Cash was SEK 18.5m at 30/6 vs 19.4m in trade payables.

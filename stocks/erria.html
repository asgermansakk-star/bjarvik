    The score is 45 out of 100: a small transport company where earnings are falling and the share has become more expensive. Sales are flat, and the outlook for the year is cut to about 12% below last year. Three fundraisings increased the number of shares by 59%. On the other hand, the board and the CEO subscribed for new shares. Watch the fourth quarter.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −21 of −30
    • Margin
      EBIT fell to DKK 4.0m in Q1–Q3 (2025: 5.6), and H1 net profit was only DKK 0.35m (2.0). Gross margin has fallen from 35% (2022) to 28% (TTM), and EBITDA margin is only 4.2%; management itself calls the margin in ship management too low.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −6
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue is standing still (Q1–Q3 kr 137.0m vs 136.0m) and is now expected at the bottom of kr 190–210m, i.e. approx. 12% below 2025 (215m). EBITDA guidance of kr 8.5–10.5m is 13–29% below 2025 (12.0m); Maersk has terminated the dry cargo depot in Binh Duong, and the growth in 2025 came partly from the acquisition of Nordic Marine Partner.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −6
    • Capital allocation
      Three capital raises in February–May 2026 (directed issue at kr 4.39, rights issue at kr 3.51 and a new directed issue at kr 3.60) increased the share count from 12.3m to 19.5m (+59%) while earnings are falling; kr 20m of the proceeds sits in bonds with no acquisition completed, and a few weeks after the issues the company bought own shares at up to kr 6.86. ROE has fallen from 41% to 20%.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −6
    • Moat
      Four small niche businesses (ship management, container seals in Singapore, offshore equipment and container repair in Vietnam) with limited pricing power; ROIC is high (43%) because of the asset-light model, but on a very small capital base, and the company has not shown that it can scale.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 6 findings    −22 of −35
    • Cash flow
      Reported operating cash flow of kr 9.0m in H1 is mainly due to an increase in deferred income (customer prepayments) from 12.5 to 22.4m; without it, cash flow is roughly zero. In Q3 total liquidity fell from kr 41.6m to 24.3m (of which 19.9m in bonds) with no explanation in the release, and kr 8.4m is tied up in subsidiaries with currency controls.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −6
    • Customer concentration
      Erria Container Services depends on Maersk (the dry depot ended 31/3, the reefer agreement was only extended 2 years), Cathay Seal on large container shipping lines such as Hapag-Lloyd, and Mermaid's Q4 revenue on deliveries to McDermott; management calls revenue outside ECS's primary customer relationships a key structural focus area.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −4
    • Execution and guidance
      To reach guidance, Q4 must deliver EBITDA of kr 2.7–4.7m and EBIT of kr 3–5m – in line with or above the whole of the first nine months (EBIT 4.0m). Since H1 the company has made expectations conditional on a 'material improvement in the conversion rate', and the promised acquisition in H2 has not been completed.
      via.ritzau.dk
      −3

      New chair (Bo Foged) came in via a targeted issue, and his company Erria Invest KS then received an additional targeted issue without pre-emption rights; the CEO received 400,000 warrants (2.8%) with acceleration on exit; the buyback was run without Safe Harbour in an illiquid stock, and the closing announcement stated a wrong share count (13.8 vs 19.5m).

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Microcap on First North (market cap about DKK 88m) with no analyst coverage and no forward estimate; P/E is therefore calculated on the company's own guidance (EBIT DKK 7–9m) and is a rough estimate. stockanalysis only updates half-yearly, so the Q3 figures (unaudited) have been taken from the company announcement. Cash has been assessed as adjusted for customer prepayments (accruals of DKK 22.4m as of 30/6) and DKK 8.4m tied up in subsidiaries with currency controls; the Q3 balance sheet has not been published, so the exact net cash as of 30/9 is unknown. The share count has been checked against the cash flow (capital increase of DKK 26.8m in H1). There are no directly listed peers within maritime niche services; NTG and DFDS have been used as rough comparisons. Web search was not available during the research, so the analysis is based on the company's own announcements (via Ritzau) and stockanalysis; press coverage, price targets and any undisclosed matters have not been examined.

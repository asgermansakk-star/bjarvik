    In plain words

    The score is high (90 out of 100): the model sees a very large order book and a reasonable price. Pulling it down: the company keeps only about 2 of every 100 in sales as profit, has diluted owners with new shares, and the US Navy cancelled four frigates. The order book equals about eight years of sales, and 2026 expectations were raised. Third-quarter results are due on 12 November.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Re-evaluated after the report of 2026-11-12. The 9M release contains only key figures (no net profit, full cash flow or ROIC), so the margin, ROIC and FCF findings rest on stockanalysis' TTM to H1 supplemented with the company's 9M figures. Industry: no category fits exactly – cruise ships are over half of revenue, naval vessels and underwater systems the rest; 'Defence, aerospace & space' was chosen as the nearest. Stockanalysis' gross debt (€2.5bn) includes construction loans; the company's own adjusted net debt was €730m at 30 Sep (1.0x EBITDA, 1.7x excluding the capital increase), and customer prepayments on cruise ships have not been deducted from cash – so no plus has been given for the balance sheet. The share count has actually risen (capital increases of about €389m in 2024 and about €485m in February 2026 appear in the cash flow); stockanalysis' −41% in 2024–25 is affected by a reverse split. Forward P/E (15.5) now rests on a later estimate (EPS about €0.74); on the company's 2026 guidance the P/E is 23–29. The underwater acquisitions (about €600m) are not expected to complete until early 2027, so pro forma figures are not used. State-owned CDP controls the majority (float about 41%) – mentioned, not scored. Analysts' price targets (median €17) were last updated before the report and the September fall.

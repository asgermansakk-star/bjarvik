    In plain words

    Bjarvik gives Loomis 82 out of 100. It lives off handling cash, which is used less and less, and its new payments business loses money. Debt is rising after the Hermes purchase and nears the company's own limit. On the plus side, margins are rising, the US is doing well, and cash flow covers the dividend and buybacks. Watch the debt and the gold price.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Only 4 analysts cover the stock (1 Strong Buy, 3 Hold), and the price targets are from July 2026, i.e. from before the Q3 report. Trailing P/E (16.9) is lifted by one-off items (goodwill impairment of SEK 314m and restructuring in Q4 2025), so trailing vs forward P/E overstates EPS growth. stockanalysis' FCF (SEK 3.8bn) is before lease payments; the findings use the company's own FCF after IFRS 16 payments, interest and tax (SEK 2.6bn TTM). The cash of SEK 11.5bn includes SEK 3.3bn of cash inventory and SEK 1.8bn of customer prepayments. Hermes (Peru) is consolidated from 5/10-2026 and is not in the balance sheet at 30/9; net debt after the acquisition is an estimate. The company gives no quantitative guidance. Customer concentration (largest bank customers), the contract duration of the segments and the details of the legal disputes in Denmark and Chile have not been examined. The industry is set to Transport & logistics because Loomis is security logistics for cash and valuables.

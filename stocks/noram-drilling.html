    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −15 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Operating profit rose from USD 4.3m (2024) to 13.8m (2025), and EPS rose 560%. This is almost entirely because the life of the rigs was extended to 10 years from 1/1-2025, so depreciation fell from USD 19.7m to 5.9m. Adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 24.1m to 20.0m. Revenue (approx. USD 101–106m incl. re-invoiced expenses) is still below 118m in 2023.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −5
    • Margin
      The EBITDA margin is 17–19% TTM (adjusted EBITDA about USD 18.9m on 106m), and in H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 10.9m from 12.0m a year earlier, although revenue rose 10%. Other operating costs rose from USD 4.1m to 6.7m. ROIC of 26% is inflated by a fleet that has been depreciated to USD 51m against an acquisition cost of about 214m.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Moat and scale
      The company has 11 land rigs in one basin (Permian) and rents out a uniform service where day rates are set by the market. The rigs drill fast and have low costs, but the competitors Helmerich & Payne and Patterson-UTI have fleets many times as large. The company has no pricing power and no growth plan beyond keeping the rigs working.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      The strategy is to pay out all excess cash flow. That means a payout ratio of 132% of net profit and only USD 1.5–3.4m per year in capex for a fleet of 11 rigs. No money is being set aside to renew the rigs in the longer term, and the share count has risen 1.6% in a year because options have been exercised.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2

    • Cash flow and dividend cover
      In H1 2026 FCF was USD 6.5m (operating cash flow 8.4m minus capex 1.9m), while dividends were USD 8.9m. Cash fell from 10.4 to USD 8.1m, below the target of minimum liquidity of about 11m. The monthly dividend of about USD 2.0m corresponds to 24m a year vs FCF of 17.4m TTM.

    • Customer concentration
      7 of the 11 rigs work for supermajors and 1 for a large E&P, so a few customers account for most of the revenue. The customers are solid, but if one of them moves rigs or stops a programme, it hits a large part of a fleet of only 11 rigs.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −3
    • Visibility and macro
      The order backlog is only USD 27.0m (8/10-2026), equal to about 3 months of revenue, and 5 of 11 rigs run on pad-to-pad contracts where the rate is renegotiated every 2–3 months. Earnings follow WTI and the Permian rig count, which swing with the Iran war (WTI USD 58–113 in 2026). The rig count bottomed at 240 in February.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Governance
      John Fredriksen's Geveran Trading owns 48.1%, and the Fredriksen-related SFL owns 2.9%, so free float is only approx. 20.7m shares. There is thus one controlling shareholder on a growth exchange with lighter requirements. The low turnover (approx. 105,000 shares per day) makes the share illiquid.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The stock trades on Euronext Growth Oslo (the Newsweb market MERK), not on the main list; the trading venue is stated as in the entry. The accounts follow Norwegian GAAP (NRS) and are in USD, while the price is in NOK. TTM figures run to 30/6-2026. The Q3 report was not published on 7/11-2026, but in Q3 11 of 11 rigs were working. Forward P/E (8.2) and the price target build on 1–2 analysts. The exact split of revenue by customer is not disclosed: only that 7 rigs work for supermajors and 1 for a large E&P. Day rates per rig are not published and have therefore not been examined. Short data does not exist. The dividends are paid in accounting terms from the share premium reserve, but they are financed by ongoing cash flow. Analysis framework, not investment advice.

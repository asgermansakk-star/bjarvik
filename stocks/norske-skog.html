    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −23 of −30
    • Margin and ROIC
      Operating margin is −2.3% TTM and ROIC −2.3% vs 16.8% in 2023 and 39% in 2022. Without the NND gain, EBITDA was only approx. kr 110m in Q3 and kr 73m in Q2 on revenue of kr 2.6bn per quarter (3–4% margin). The packaging segment had EBITDA of 0 in Q3 and −kr 27m year to date.
      stockanalysis.com
      −10
    • Future segment
      78% of revenue (kr 7.8bn of 9.95bn) still comes from publication paper. The company itself expects a fall in demand for newsprint of about 7% in 2026, and capacity utilisation in Western Europe is 82%. According to the company, the market requires more closures. Organic growth (+5% TTM) comes solely from the ramp-up of packaging paper.
      mfn.se
      −5
    • Capital allocation
      Investments of NOK 6.9bn in 2022–2025 (mainly the conversion to containerboard in Golbey and Bruck) have produced negative FCF six years in a row and net debt that has risen from NOK 1.1bn to NOK 4.4bn. Golbey PM1 still does not make money. Now comes a new rebuild of PM6 at Saugbrugs for up to NOK 850m.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Moat
      Newsprint, magazine paper and recycled containerboard are commodity-like products with overcapacity: capacity utilisation for RCCM in Western Europe is 86%, and management expects intense competition in the short term. The price follows the market, and rising energy costs can only partly be passed on as surcharges.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Q3 2026 result is lifted by a one-off gain of NOK 338m from the property sale to NND. The findings are based on underlying earnings (EBITDA about NOK 110m in Q3 and NOK 73m in Q2). Q1 2026 EBITDA of NOK 451m has not been broken down into one-off items, so the LTM figures are uncertain. stockanalysis has no forecast page (404) and shows forward P/E 476, so analyst coverage is thin. Price targets, customer concentration and credit rating have not been examined. The share price rise of 143% over 52 weeks is linked to the data centre plans at Saugbrugs. The letter of intent with Green Mountain expired on 31/10 without extension. The company pays with synthetic share options, which have not been assessed.

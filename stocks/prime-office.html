    In plain words

    The score of 48 shows a property company with rising debt and low earnings. Debt is 52.3% of property value against 41.3% a few years ago, interest costs are rising, and the chief executive's company earns a fee that grows with debt. On the plus side, the share costs only 0.67 times the properties' value, and loans need no repayments until 2028. The German sale has no timetable.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    • Quality of growth
      Revenue fell 1.6% in H1 2026 and profit before value adjustments and tax 9.3% (kr 34.5m vs 38.1m), because interest expenses rose 27%. The occupancy rate has fallen in all segments in a year: residential 95.5% (96.7%), office 95.5% (100%) and commercial 82% (89.6%). Net asset value per share has risen only from kr 338 to 348 in a year.
      primeoffice.dk
      −3
    • Capital allocation (Mejlbryggen)
      The company is building 305 apartments at Aarhus Ø for approx. DKK 1.5bn (DKK 1,058m spent as of 30/6). With Colliers' market rent of DKK 1,800–1,900 per m², approx. 26,000 m² of housing gives a gross rent of approx. DKK 50m, approx. 3.3% of the construction cost before operating costs. Sale as owner-occupied apartments at the area's average price of DKK 54,227 per m² gives approx. DKK 1.4bn. The project is carried at cost, and a valuation on completion in 2028 could lead to a write-down. At the same time, no treasury shares have been bought at 0.51–0.67 × book value, although the authorisation allows 20%.
      primeoffice.dk
      −5
    • Moat and segment
      The portfolio is about 3,400 homes in northern German provincial towns (Lübeck, Heide, Schleswig, Lensahn), of which the company owns only 59%, offices in Hamburg, Kiel and Lübeck, and 12 mixed commercial properties in MC Property Fund. Operations are outsourced to BUWOG (about kr 17.5m a year), and there is no moat beyond the regulated German rent increases.

    • Governance and related parties
      CEO Mogens Vinther Møller's company Moller & Company A/S receives 0.15% of the balance sheet a year (DKK 6.2m in 2025), so the fee grows with the debt. The company also receives extra fees for property sales (DKK 2.5m in 2024) and for construction management of Mejlbryggen (DKK 2.5m in 2025). If Prime Office terminates the CEO, the company must buy his shares (27.3%, valued at DKK 225.8m). Marie Vinther Møller sits on the board as a dependent member, and in 2024 the company spent significant sums on lawyers and auditors to discuss the CEO contract.

The Mejlbryggen loans are not interest-hedged, several cheap swaps (−0.11% to 0.83%) expire 2026–2031, and the ECB raised the rate in June 2026.

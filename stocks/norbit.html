    In plain words

    Bjarvik gives 84 out of 100 to a Norwegian technology company making sonars and road-toll equipment. Expectations were cut in August, the toll business rests on one customer, sonar sales fell 7%, and net debt has almost doubled. On the plus side, the return on invested money is high (28%), and the CEO bought shares. The next report is due 12 November.

      PIR, which provides R&D services and contract manufacturing to key customers, is now the largest segment with kr 367m of kr 832m in Q2 (44%) and an EBIT margin of 22% vs 33% in Oceans. In contract manufacturing the customer often owns the design.

      Gross margin has fallen from 59.8% (2024) to 55.6% (2025) and 52.8% (TTM) because the mix is shifting towards PIR and Connectivity. EBIT margin of 21.5% and ROIC of 28% are still high.

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −10 of −35
    • Guidance
      In May Norbit expected over NOK 3.0bn in revenue and an EBIT margin above the 2025 level of 22%. In August guidance was lowered to NOK 2.9–3.1bn (the lower end, because PIR orders for Q4 come later) and a margin of 20–23%, because rising prices of memory chips squeeze Connectivity (gross margin 57% vs 64%).
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −4
    • Customer concentration
      The toll business rests on one customer, Toll4Europe (orders of kr 155m and 325m in 2026); satellite-based tolling alone was kr 379m TTM. The large PIR orders (225m in June, 185 + 100m in November) come from an unnamed European customer in defence and security.
      newsweb.oslobors.no
      −3
    • Cash flow and debt
      Operating cash flow in H1 of DKK 488m was lifted by released working capital, primarily customer prepayments. A dividend of DKK 320m in May and Water Linked at DKK 330m in cash in July lift interest-bearing net debt from DKK 340m to approx. 670m (plus leasing of 215m). The dividend equals 71% of net profit.
      ml-eu.globenewswire.com
      −2
    • Macro and currency
      Oceans' revenue is mainly in USD and EUR, and the components are bought globally. The company itself names tariffs, trade barriers and currency as risks, and Q3 guidance is kr 620–680m vs 832m in Q2.

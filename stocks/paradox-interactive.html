    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a solid game company at a fair price, but with cracks. Sales have stood still for four years, and the company has repeatedly written down the value of game development, about SEK 1bn over five years. On the plus side, it has more cash than debt, earns steadily from older games, and its founder owns about a third. Watch whether delayed games also have to be written down.

    A strong score: the model found few serious problems, but analysts see limited upside from here.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −12 of −30
    • Margin
      Gross margin fell to 44% in 9M 2026 vs 50% a year earlier (Q3: 41% vs 49%). This is due to higher amortisation of EU5 and Bloodlines 2 and royalties to the external developer of Transport Fever 3. Without the gain of 40m from the sale of Playrion, EBIT in Q3 fell to about 102m (112) and EBIT margin in 9M to about 27% (30%). In 2021–2024 it was 33–44%.
      mb.cision.com
      −4
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue has stood still for four years (1,973m in 2022, 2,642 in 2023, 2,201 in 2024 and 2,192 in 2025). The 7% growth in 9M 2026 comes mainly from the external Transport Fever 3, which has a low margin, and from a stronger USD. Analysts expect −2.8% for the full year 2026.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      There are repeated write-downs of capitalised development outside the core areas: SEK 236m in 2021, 185m in 2023, 208m in 2024 and 355m in 2025 (Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines 2, written down 30 days after launch). That is about 1bn over five years vs annual development investment of about 600m. Management itself admits that the game was outside the core areas.

    • Cash flow and payouts
      FCF after capitalised development and leases was only approx. SEK 126m in 9M 2026 (OCF 610 − development 454 − leases 25 − equipment 5). Operating cash flow fell from 723m due to 151m in tied-up receivables and an advance of USD 25m to Urban Games.     • Execution and project risk
      Prison Architect 2, which was postponed indefinitely in 2024, still sits as capitalised development on the balance sheet together with Afterworld and LEGO Skylines. That creates a risk of new write-downs. Capitalised development costs are SEK 1,153m (53% of equity).

    • Customer concentration
      Sales go mainly through Steam: the company itself cites dependence on a few sales partners as a particular risk, and 84% of revenue is invoiced to the US. This is normal in the industry, but bargaining power over the platform is limited.

      Revenue is almost entirely in currencies other than SEK. The CEO cites currency headwind in 2025, while Q3 2026 was helped by a stronger USD. The company discloses no currency hedging.

    In plain words

    Bjarvik sees a Nordic hotel chain with almost no growth (1.2% in Q2) and returns below its cost of capital. It rents most of its hotels and has lease debt of SEK 44bn, about twice its market value. The acquisition of Dalata's hotels, completed on 2 November, raises debt further. On the plus side, the dividend is covered by cash flow. The Q3 report is due on 10 November.

    A good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.

    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −14 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Organic growth was only 1.2% in Q2 2026, and RevPAR rose 0.3% (kr 882 vs 879). In 2025 revenue rose 1.5% (about 4% excluding currency), and room rates fell 1.6%. The target of at least 5% organic growth a year is not being met.
      mfn.se
      −3
    • Margin and ROIC
      Adjusted EBITDA margin excluding IFRS 16 is 11.1% (12 months) and 10.9% in 2025, i.e. on the edge of the target of at least 11%. ROIC is 4.8% vs a WACC of 5.0% and has been 3.8–4.8% since 2022 without getting above the cost of capital.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation (Dalata acquisition)
      Scandic has bought Dalata's hotel operations for EUR 500m (approx. kr 5.4bn, approx. a quarter of market capitalisation), financed with cash and loans. The acquisition lifts net debt/adjusted EBITDA from 0.1x to up to 2.0x and brings new 30-year leases on 31 hotels with the seller consortium. The consortium consists of Pandox and Eiendomsspar, which is also a major shareholder in Scandic. Affärsvärlden assesses that Scandic may have overpaid.
      mfn.se
      −5
    • Business model and moat
      Scandic owns no properties and leases 248 of 324 hotels on long contracts, so the company bears all of the operating risk. This gives a lower margin and valuation than the fee-based franchise models at Hilton and Accor. Franchise still makes up a small part of revenue.
      affarsvarlden.se
      −3

    • Lease debt and interest cover

    • Execution (Finland)
      Finland, with 60 hotels and 12,470 rooms, continues to weaken. In Q2 revenue fell to SEK 1,051m (1,156), and adjusted EBITDA fell to SEK 58m (142), so the margin went from 12.3% to 5.5%. Management says the recovery is taking longer than expected, and expects only a second half in line with last year.

    • Macro and hotel cycle
      Hotel demand follows the economic cycle. With long leases, many of which have a fixed minimum rent, a fall in RevPAR hits earnings hard, as the pandemic showed. In 2026 a hotel strike of over six weeks hit Norway, and price development was weak in the first half.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The Q3 report comes on 10/11-2026, so all figures go to 30/6-2026. The acquisition of Dalatas' hotel operations (57 hotels, EUR 500m) was completed on 2/11-2026 and is not included in the historical figures. Pro forma figures come only with the Q3 report, so the stockanalysis history and PEG are before the acquisition, and the trailing multiples are not entirely comparable (Data hygiene 1.3, point 4). IFRS 16 distorts EBITDA, ROE (equity is only SEK 2.8bn), P/B and stockanalysis' FCF; FCF and debt are therefore measured on the company's figures without IFRS 16, where rent is deducted. Stockanalysis' share count change of −6.9% has not been used: the share count fell only from 218.3 to 215.1m (buybacks of SEK 248m in 2025). Industry: hotel = 'Transport & logistics' as for Hilton. The price targets are from July 2026, and only 8 analysts cover the stock. Dalatas' rent level after the takeover and any goodwill have not been examined and score 0.
